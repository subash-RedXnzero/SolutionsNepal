<h2 id="what">What Is Namsari?</h2>

<strong>Namsari (नामसारी)</strong> means name transfer: the official process of updating registered vehicle ownership with the Department of Transport Management under the Motor Vehicle and Transport Management Act, 2049 (see the published Act via the <a href="https://www.lawcommission.gov.np">Nepal Law Commission</a> and current procedures at <a href="https://www.dotm.gov.np">dotm.gov.np</a>). Day-to-day processing happens at the provincial Transport Management Office covering the vehicle's registered district.

- Buying or selling a used motorcycle, scooter, or car
- Gifting a vehicle to a family member
- Inheriting a vehicle after the registered owner has passed away
- Moving a vehicle into or out of a registered company's name
- Settling ownership after a vehicle loan has been fully paid off

<strong>Namsari is not bluebook renewal.</strong> Renewal keeps an existing owner's registration current. Namsari changes the registered owner, and you cannot renew a bluebook in your name until the transfer is complete.

> **Why it matters:** Until namsari is recorded, the seller remains the legal owner on file and can be tied to accidents, traffic fines, or unpaid tax. Treat the Yatayat visit as part of the purchase.

<h2 id="documents">Documents Required for Namsari</h2>

<h3 id="seller-documents">Seller Brings</h3>

- Original bluebook, not a photo or photocopy
- Citizenship certificate, original plus at least one photocopy
- Two recent passport-size photos
- Proof that vehicle tax is fully paid and current
- Signed vehicle ownership transfer application in the prescribed form

<h3 id="buyer-documents">Buyer Brings</h3>

- Citizenship certificate, original plus at least one photocopy
- Two recent passport-size photos
- The signed transfer application, countersigned by the buyer
- Company incorporation certificate and board authorization letter for company purchases
- Payment for the transfer fee and any outstanding tax

Exact requirements can vary slightly by office. Call the specific Yatayat Karyalaya before visiting.

<h2 id="process">Yatayat Namsari Steps: The Full Process</h2>

1. <strong>Confirm the vehicle status and price.</strong> Check the bluebook, tax status, and registered owner before paying.
2. <strong>Clear outstanding vehicle tax</strong> before the office processes the file.
3. <strong>Prepare both document sets</strong> including citizenship documents, photos, and originals.
4. <strong>Fill the namsari application form</strong> at the office or through an online provincial form where available.
5. <strong>Visit the correct TMO together</strong> — the office where the vehicle is registered.
6. <strong>Submit documents and take a token.</strong> The officer checks the bluebook against chassis and engine numbers.
7. <strong>Pay transfer fees and applicable transfer tax</strong> under the province's current schedule.
8. <strong>Complete vehicle inspection</strong> if required by the office.
9. <strong>Wait for the ownership record to be updated</strong> in the DoTM system.
10. <strong>Collect the re-stamped bluebook</strong> showing the buyer as registered owner.

> **Do not postpone it:** Paying in full and planning to handle paperwork later leaves the seller legally tied to the vehicle and prevents you from properly insuring or renewing it.

<h2 id="fees">Namsari Fees and Tax Clearing</h2>

There is no single fee nationwide. Nepal's seven provinces set their own vehicle charges through annual Provincial Economic Acts, and rates are revised most fiscal years.

- **DoTM processing/service fee** — Administrative processing and bluebook re-stamping; set federally
- **Ownership transfer charge** — Percentage or slab based on vehicle value, type, or engine capacity; set by province
- **Outstanding vehicle tax** — Unpaid annual road tax that must be cleared before transfer; set by province and vehicle category

Outstanding vehicle tax must be cleared before the transfer is processed. Confirm the exact total with the registered Yatayat office or through the Nagarik App; one province or fiscal year's figure will not necessarily apply elsewhere.

<h2 id="vehicle-types">Bike Namsari vs. Car Namsari: What's Different</h2>

<h3 id="motorcycles">Motorcycles and Scooters</h3>

- Usually simpler, with lower transfer and tax brackets for smaller engine capacity
- Chassis and engine checks are especially important because bikes are often resold several times
- Confirm any EMI loan is closed before transfer

<h3 id="cars">Cars, Jeeps, and SUVs</h3>

- Higher tax and transfer brackets generally apply to larger engine capacity
- Bank financing is common, so a release letter is often required
- Check fitness history, commercial use, taxi conversion, and accident repairs

<h2 id="online">Is Namsari Available Online?</h2>

Partially, and the system is evolving. DoTM's EDLVRS and the Nagarik App or Fonepay-linked bank apps can support related preparation and vehicle-tax payments in many provinces. A complete nationwide online process from application through final bluebook stamping is not yet confirmed, so treat digital tools as preparation rather than a substitute for the in-person Yatayat visit. Bagmati has been active in piloting digital verification.

<h2 id="special">Special Situations</h2>

- <strong>Registered owner deceased:</strong> inheritance transfer generally requires a death certificate, relationship proof, and succession documents.
- <strong>Family gift:</strong> the same underlying namsari process applies, though close-relative transfers may receive different tax treatment.
- <strong>Company-owned vehicle:</strong> use incorporation and board authorization documents instead of personal citizenship documents.
- <strong>Vehicle under bank loan:</strong> complete the loan and obtain the bank release before attempting namsari.
- <strong>One party unavailable:</strong> use a notarized power of attorney naming an authorized representative; an informal verbal arrangement is not enough.

<h2 id="blocks">What Can Block a Transfer</h2>

- Unpaid vehicle tax
- Expired bluebook
- Lost bluebook without a police report and duplicate application
- Mismatched chassis or engine numbers
- Missing or unauthorized signature

<h2 id="checklists">Buyer and Seller Checklists</h2>

<h3 id="buyer-checklist">Buyer's Pre-Purchase Checklist</h3>

- Physically match chassis and engine numbers to the bluebook
- Confirm tax is fully paid and current
- Ask about any bank loan
- Confirm the seller is the sole registered owner

<h3 id="seller-checklist">Seller's Pre-Sale Checklist</h3>

- Clear pending tax before listing the vehicle
- Prepare the original bluebook and citizenship certificate
- Attend the transport office with the buyer
- Keep the transfer receipt as proof that legal responsibility moved

<h2 id="scams">Common Namsari Scams to Watch For</h2>

- Unofficial “fast-track” cash offers
- Sellers asking you to skip the transport office
- Altered or photocopied bluebooks presented as originals
- Agents guaranteeing same-day transfer for an extra fee

<h2 id="confirm">Confirming the Transfer Went Through</h2>

Before leaving the office, check that the bluebook has been physically re-stamped and lists you as the registered owner. Keep the transfer receipt and every tax or fee confirmation for insurance, resale, and future disputes.

> **Verify current rules:** This article is for general informational purposes. Namsari fees and procedures vary by province and are revised annually under each province's Economic Act. Confirm current details with your registered Transport Management Office or <a href="https://www.dotm.gov.np">DoTM (dotm.gov.np)</a> before proceeding (as of October 2026).
