<h2 id="check-date">Quick Answer: How to Check Your Driving License Trial Date</h2>

Nepal's driving-license system moved to <strong>eDLVRS (Electronic Driving License and Vehicle Registration System)</strong> on 1 Shrawan 2083 (17 July 2026), replacing the old central portal. Each of Nepal's seven provinces now operates its own eDLVRS portal.

1. <strong>Log in to your province's eDLVRS portal</strong> using the mobile number registered with your application. Your dashboard shows pre-enrollment, biometric, written-exam, and trial stages.
2. <strong>Check your TMO notice.</strong> Transport Management Offices publish trial routines and name lists with exact date, time, and venue.
3. <strong>Watch for SMS.</strong> Many offices send the trial schedule to the mobile number on file.

Keep your application or citizenship number and registered mobile number ready. If your date is missing, the written result may not be finalized or your office may not have published the next batch. Schedules can change because of examiner, vehicle, course, quota, monsoon, or holiday conditions.

New to the process? Our <a href="/guides/nepal-online-driving-license-edlvrs-portal-guide">EDLVRS online application guide</a> covers how to register, pay, and book biometrics on your province's portal.

<h2 id="what">What Is the Driving License Trial in Nepal?</h2>

The trial (प्रयोगात्मक परीक्षा) is the practical driving test after biometric registration and the written exam. An examiner watches you operate a motorcycle, scooter, or car through the course at your TMO. It is required for new applications and category additions, but not for straightforward renewals or duplicate/copy requests.

<h2 id="process">Nepal Driving License Trial Process</h2>

<strong>Application → Biometrics and verification → Written exam → Trial date → Practical trial → Result → Card printing</strong>

1. Apply online through your province's eDLVRS portal. National ID or Nagarik App data may auto-fill details.
2. Visit the TMO for document verification and biometric capture.
3. Sit the written exam where applicable.
4. Find your trial date through the dashboard, SMS, or published routine.
5. Take the practical trial for your category.
6. Receive the result, usually on the same day.
7. After passing, enter the smart-card printing queue.

<h2 id="written">Written Test (Likhit): Pattern & Pass Marks</h2>

The written test (लिखित) comes before the trial. Study from the DoTM question bank (traffic signs, road rules, and general knowledge) and read the live EDLVRS notice for your province and exam date, because the format is reported in more than one way.

- <strong>Commonly described format:</strong> about 60 questions × 1 mark with a 40-mark pass and roughly 15–30 minutes on a computer, no negative marking.
- <strong>Also reported variants:</strong> 20 questions × 5 marks, ~50 questions, or a ~60% pass line — the format varies by EDLVRS notice and province.
- <strong>What dominates the paper:</strong> traffic-sign shapes and colours, right-of-way rules, and alcohol/helmet laws — roughly 80% of the paper is signs and rules.
- <strong>Bring:</strong> printed admit card or application confirmation, original citizenship certificate, and the fee voucher/receipt.
- <strong>Validity:</strong> passing the written test qualifies you for the trial; if you miss or fail the trial within the window your office gives you, you may have to redo the written stage. Confirm the window on your province's EDLVRS portal or at your TMO.

The exact question count, marks, and duration should be taken from the current EDLVRS notice for your exam date, not from a general article.

<h2 id="rules">Nepal Driving License Trial Rules</h2>

<h3 id="two-wheeler-rules">Motorcycle / Scooter Trial (Category A/K)</h3>

- Minimum age is generally cited as 16 years (confirm the current age rule in the DoTM notice or your provincial eDLVRS portal — last checked October 2026).
- The course typically tests slow-speed balance, a figure-of-eight or similar turning pattern, and controlled braking.
- The office may provide the trial vehicle or permit your own two-wheeler.
- Carry your original citizenship certificate on exam day.

<h3 id="four-wheeler-rules">Car / Four-Wheeler Trial (Category B)</h3>

- Minimum age is generally 18 years.
- The course tests turning, reversing, parking, stopping, and basic control.
- Other categories such as tractor, truck, minibus, and bus may require a minimum age of 21.

DoTM sets the general exam format, but the exact size, turns, obstacles, and layout differ by TMO. Do not assume a route shown in a video from another district is identical to yours.

<h2 id="seventy">70 Percent Rule in Nepal Driving Trial</h2>

> **Commonly reported; confirm with your TMO.** No TMO/DoTM scoring sheet could be verified at last check (October 2026).

Under the revised practical scoring approach, applicants generally need around <strong>70 marks out of 100</strong> rather than a near-perfect run. Small deductions do not necessarily end the trial, but losing control, dangerous maneuvers, or failing a mandatory safety component can still cause an automatic fail.

The 70-point approach was rolled out in stages, so confirm the current scoring sheet with your TMO. Point deductions for touching a boundary, stalling, or missing an indicator can vary in detail.

<h2 id="route">Driving License Trial Route</h2>

Layouts differ from office to office, but two-wheelers and cars are tested on recognizable station sets. Below are the commonly reported stages — treat them as a practice map and confirm your own office's current course.

<strong>Two-wheeler (bike / scooter) — the commonly cited 7 stages:</strong>

1. <strong>Figure-8 (Aath)</strong> — keep feet up, engine running, inside the double line; a foot touch or line touch is an instant fail
2. <strong>Sumy (width control)</strong> — the raised plank; stay centred with steady throttle, no wheel drop
3. <strong>U-turn (Ghumaune)</strong> — a tight U without putting a foot down or crossing the outer line
4. <strong>Uphill (Ukalo)</strong> — stop on the slope and restart without rolling back more than about 30 cm
5. <strong>Downhill (Oralo)</strong> — controlled descent, no engine-off, foot brake only
6. <strong>Rough road (Khurkhure)</strong> — hold your line over an uneven surface without wobbling
7. <strong>Traffic and road test</strong> — signals, mirror checks, helmet and indicator discipline plus a short road ride

<strong>Car / four-wheeler:</strong>

- Forward figure-8, reverse-S, and side parking
- Uphill start using the handbrake without rolling back
- Turning within boundary lines and smooth gear/clutch control

<strong>Top reasons people fail:</strong> putting a foot down in the figure-8, rolling back on the uphill start, hitting a pole or line, missing an indicator, and stalling the engine.

Visit your specific trial ground or ask an instructor about its exact layout before the appointment. Routes are not standardized down to identical dimensions nationwide. Practising the exact pattern at your own office matters more than general riding skill — even 30 minutes on the actual Aath course fixes many first-time failures.

<h2 id="fees">Driving License Trial Fee in Nepal</h2>

Fees are no longer uniform nationwide. Since fiscal year 2083/84, provinces set schedules under the eDLVRS system.

- **Application/registration** — Previously NPR 500 nationally; now varies by province
- **New Category A license** — Older baseline around NPR 2,000–2,500; verify current provincial amount
- **New Category B license** — Usually higher than Category A; verify current provincial amount
- **Retrial** — Commonly NPR 300–500 per attempt (commonly reported; confirm the current provincial amount — last checked October 2026)
- **Smart-card printing** — A separate smaller production fee may apply

> **Pay officially:** Confirm the exact amount on your provincial eDLVRS portal or at the TMO counter. Never pay an agent or helper to speed up a government payment.

<strong>The payment flow (do this carefully):</strong>

1. Check the current fee schedule on your province's eDLVRS portal when you apply (national policy notices at <a href="https://dotm.gov.np">dotm.gov.np</a>).
2. Pay through the channel the portal assigns — typically ConnectIPS, eSewa, Khalti, internet banking, or a bank voucher.
3. Keep every payment voucher and receipt. A name mismatch between the voucher and your application is one of the most common causes of counter delays.
4. Make sure any category you pay for matches the category on your application.

<h2 id="prepare">How to Prepare for the Driving Trial</h2>

- Practice the exact maneuvers in your category, not only general road driving.
- Use the same vehicle type and become familiar with its controls.
- Practice slow-speed control repeatedly for two-wheelers.
- Practice smooth early braking, indicators, mirror checks, and blind-spot checks.
- Visit the actual course if possible and arrive early.

<h3 id="common-fails">Common Reasons Applicants Fail</h3>

- Losing control or putting a foot down where prohibited
- Touching or crossing a boundary line
- Missing or using the wrong indicator
- Repeated engine stalling
- Poor mirror and blind-spot observation
- Harsh or late braking
- Ignoring a sign or mandatory course component

<h3 id="two-wheeler-tips">Motorcycle and Scooter Tips</h3>

- Prioritize balance and slow throttle control over speed.
- Use the clutch smoothly on manual bikes and both brakes progressively.
- Look ahead rather than down at the front wheel.
- Signal every turn, even when it seems obvious.

<h3 id="car-tips">Car Trial Tips</h3>

- Practice smooth clutch, gear, steering, reversing, and parking control.
- Brake early and smoothly at marked points.
- Check mirrors and blind spots before turns and reversing.
- Use indicators consistently and stay calm on gradient sections.

<h2 id="rejections">Why Applications Get Rejected</h2>

Beyond simply failing the trial, files often stall or bounce for paperwork reasons. The recurring causes:

- <strong>Age or eligibility mismatch</strong> — the category you applied for does not match your age or your document trail.
- <strong>Name inconsistencies</strong> — your citizenship, application, and medical form must agree exactly; even a spelling difference can stop the file.
- <strong>Missed exam windows</strong> — skipping your scheduled written or trial date forfeits that attempt, and rebooking means a new queue.
- <strong>Unpaid or mismatched vouchers</strong> — the classic silent killer; the payment must be made, and its name and category must match the application.
- <strong>Photo/biometric issues</strong> — poor captures at registration cause identity-check failures later.
- <strong>Trying to skip stages</strong> — the system enforces the written → trial → license sequence strictly.

The process rewards exactly two things: a consistent document trail and genuine trial practice. Everyone who does both gets a license; the stories about endless rejection almost always trace back to one of the reasons above.

<h2 id="trial-day">What to Take on Trial Day</h2>

- Original citizenship certificate and photocopy
- Original license if adding a category
- Application receipt or payment slip
- Shoes, not sandals or slippers
- No mobile phone inside the written or practical exam area

<h2 id="retrial">What Happens If You Fail the Trial?</h2>

> **Commonly reported retrial rule; confirm with your TMO.** The "three retrials within 90 days" arrangement could not be verified against a current TMO/DoTM notice at last check (October 2026).

- You can generally attend a maximum of three retrials within 90 days of the first failed trial.
- Each attempt usually has a separate fee, commonly NPR 300–500.
- Rebook through your applicant account or TMO; retrial is not automatic.
- After the three-attempt window, ask your TMO about the next reapplication step.

<h2 id="after">What Happens After Passing?</h2>

1. Examiners confirm the result, usually on the same day.
2. Biometric and record details are finalized if needed.
3. Your approved record enters the smart-card printing queue.
4. Check print status through DoTM tools, SMS short codes, or your provincial eDLVRS portal.
5. Some provinces may offer a digital copy after issuance; confirm acceptance with your TMO.
6. Collect the physical card with citizenship certificate and payment receipt when notified.

> **Verify before your trial:** Fees, scoring details, routes, and print timelines are changing with the province-based eDLVRS rollout. Confirm current details on your province's portal, at your local TMO, or in DoTM notices at <a href="https://dotm.gov.np">dotm.gov.np</a> (last checked October 2026).
