<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: How Much Is the Malpot Registration Fee?</h2>

The Malpot registration fee is the charge paid to the Land Revenue Office when ownership changes. There is no single nationwide flat rate. A typical planning range is about <strong>2% to 6%</strong> of the applicable property value, depending on location, property type, transaction type, and current fiscal-year law.

- **Metropolitan city** — Approximately 5% in common planning examples
- **Sub-metropolitan city** — Approximately 4.5%
- **Municipality** — Approximately 4%
- **Rural municipality** — Approximately 2–3%
- **Apartment special rate** — Some units may have a special rate around 1%, subject to current rules and concessions

The calculation generally uses the higher of the declared transaction price and the government minimum valuation. For example, if the agreed price is Rs 1 crore but the minimum valuation is Rs 1.2 crore, the office may calculate the registration fee on Rs 1.2 crore.

For FY 2083/84, registration rates are set through provincial Finance Acts and can differ by local-body type. The following Bagmati planning references are useful for budgeting, but the current rate and any surcharge must be confirmed at the relevant Malpot office.

- **Metropolitan inside Kathmandu Valley** — Commonly reported at 5.3% for Kathmandu and Lalitpur (unconfirmed — confirm at Malpot/IRD)

- **Metropolitan outside the Valley** — Commonly reported at 5.15% (unconfirmed — confirm at Malpot/IRD)
- **Sub-metropolitan city** — Commonly reported at 4.65% (unconfirmed — confirm at Malpot/IRD)
- **Municipality outside the Valley** — Commonly reported at 4.5% (unconfirmed — confirm at Malpot/IRD)
- **Rural municipality** — Commonly reported at 3% in Bagmati (unconfirmed — confirm at Malpot/IRD)

The fee basis remains the higher of the declared transaction value and the current government minimum valuation for the exact ward, road category, land classification, and parcel. Market price, declared deed value, and government minimum valuation are different numbers.

- <strong>Women buyers:</strong> a registration-fee concession may apply when ownership is registered in an eligible woman's name; rural and single-woman concessions can differ.
- <strong>Other concessions:</strong> senior citizens, parentless minors, joint ownership, family transfers, inheritance, and special deeds can have separate conditions.
- <strong>Bagmati charges:</strong> do not assume a separate surcharge or apartment rate from an older calculator; check the current provincial Act and local office calculation.
- <strong>FY changes:</strong> a valuation schedule or rate from FY 2082/83 may not apply to FY 2083/84.

> **Planning only:** These figures are orientation points, not an official quotation. Ask the Land Revenue Office for the current schedule that matches the property's district, ward, road access, and transaction type.

<h2 id="malpot">What Is Malpot Karyalaya?</h2>

<strong>Malpot Karyalaya</strong> is the Land Revenue Office. It records land ownership, processes transfers, collects land-related fees, maintains records such as the Lalpurja, and verifies that applicable taxes and charges are paid before registration.

- Use the Malpot office with jurisdiction over the property location, not necessarily the buyer's or seller's nearest office.
- The office may collect seller-side capital-gains tax on behalf of the Inland Revenue Department.
- Malpot registration fee, capital-gains tax, local charges, and documentation costs are separate items.

<h2 id="office-roles">Malpot vs Survey Office vs Municipality vs IRD</h2>

- **Malpot / Land Revenue Office** — Registers ownership transfers, maintains Lalpurja records, collects registration fees and land revenue, and processes deed execution.
- **Survey Office / Napi Karyalaya** — Maintains cadastral maps, kitta boundaries, measurements, and Mero Kitta-related services.
- **Municipality or rural municipality** — Handles local property tax, building permits, ward recommendations, and local business or property processes.
- **Inland Revenue Department** — Administers income-tax and capital-gains-tax policy; Malpot may withhold CGT during registration.

<h2 id="valuation">What Is Malpot Valuation?</h2>

Malpot valuation, government valuation, minimum valuation, or <em>sarkari mulyankan</em> is the official minimum value set for land and property in a locality. It helps prevent under-declaration, standardizes fee calculations, and reflects factors such as road access, infrastructure, demand, and distance from urban centres.

- Urban Kathmandu Valley locations generally have higher minimum values.
- Suburban and rural areas generally have lower values, but locality and road category still matter.
- District committees update valuation schedules periodically, often for a new fiscal year.
- Outdated valuation figures can make a purchase budget inaccurate.

<h2 id="calculation">How Land Registration Fees Are Calculated</h2>

1. <strong>Identify the property location:</strong> province, district, municipality or rural municipality, ward, and relevant Malpot office.
2. <strong>Check the official valuation:</strong> confirm the current minimum value for the ward, road type, land type, and property details.
3. <strong>Choose the calculation basis:</strong> use the higher of declared transaction price or government minimum valuation.
4. <strong>Apply the registration rate:</strong> use the current rate for the location, property type, and deed type.
5. <strong>Add other charges:</strong> local or municipal charges, stamp or service fees, deed preparation, legal, valuation, and financing costs.
6. <strong>Confirm with the office:</strong> treat online estimates as planning figures until the relevant Malpot office verifies the final amount.

Where available, check an official Malpot fee calculator by selecting the district, municipality, land type, and deed value. Do not assume a rate from Kathmandu applies nationwide.

<h2 id="who-pays">Buyer vs Seller: Who Pays What?</h2>

- **Land registration fee** — Typically buyer; commonly 2–6% depending on location
- **Capital-gains tax** — Typically seller; calculated on taxable gain
- **Local or municipal charges** — Typically buyer, subject to local rule and negotiation
- **Stamp/service fees** — Typically buyer, but may be negotiated
- **Legal/documentation costs** — Negotiable between buyer and seller
- **Bank and mortgage costs** — Typically buyer when financing

Legal responsibility and practical payment arrangements are not always the same. Parties can negotiate who bears a cost, but the office may still require the legally responsible tax or fee to be settled before registration.

<h2 id="capital-gains">Capital Gains Tax on Land in Nepal</h2>

Capital-gains tax applies to the seller's taxable profit from a property sale, not usually the entire sale price. A simplified gain is sale price minus acquisition cost and allowable documented deductions.

- **Holding period 5 years or more** — Unconfirmed planning reference: 5% of taxable gain under a cited FY 2083/84 framework (conflicting CGT schedules circulate, including 3.75%/5% and flat-7.5% variants — confirm at IRD/Malpot; last checked October 2026)
- **Holding period under 5 years** — Unconfirmed planning reference: 7.5% of taxable gain under a cited FY 2083/84 framework (same caveat — confirm at IRD/Malpot)
- **Simplified example** — Buy Rs 50 lakh, sell Rs 1 crore → Rs 50 lakh gain → 5% example tax = Rs 2.5 lakh (illustrative only, using the unconfirmed rate above)

The seller is generally liable, and the tax is settled through the Malpot process before transfer. Exemptions, primary-residence relief, annual thresholds, business status, deductions, and property type can affect the result. Ask the Inland Revenue Department or a tax professional for a case-specific calculation.

<h2 id="cost-factors">What Determines Land Registration Cost?</h2>

- <strong>Location:</strong> metropolitan, sub-metropolitan, municipality, rural municipality, province, and local charges.
- <strong>Government valuation:</strong> ward, road access, land classification, and current minimum valuation.
- <strong>Declared transaction value:</strong> the sale price, subject to the government minimum floor.
- <strong>Property type:</strong> residential, commercial, agricultural, apartment, or special category.
- <strong>Transaction type:</strong> sale, gift, inheritance, family, or spousal transfer may have different treatment.
- <strong>Concessions:</strong> women ownership, first-time buyers, apartments, and family transfers may receive concessions in some jurisdictions.
- <strong>Financing:</strong> mortgage registration, bank processing, valuation, and insurance costs add to the buyer budget.

Typical planning patterns are around 5% for metropolitan cities, 4.5% for sub-metropolitan areas, 4% for municipalities, and 2–3% for rural municipalities. Verify all rates locally.

<h2 id="documents">Documents Needed for Land Registration</h2>

<h3 id="buyer-documents">For the Buyer</h3>

- Citizenship certificate, original and copy
- Recent photographs
- Tax clearance where applicable
- Marriage certificate for relevant joint or spousal transfers

<h3 id="seller-documents">For the Seller</h3>

- Citizenship certificate, original and copy
- Original Lalpurja
- Updated land/property records
- Tax clearance where applicable
- Capital-gains tax payment proof settled through the process

<h3 id="transaction-documents">Transaction and Special Documents</h3>

- Sale deed (Rajinama)
- Advance or agreement documents if applicable
- Property valuation report for bank financing where required
- Death, heirship, gift, power-of-attorney, company, or NRN documents when relevant

Requirements vary by transfer type and office. Confirm the current checklist with the relevant Malpot Karyalaya before preparing the deed.

<h2 id="process">Land Registration Process at Malpot Karyalaya</h2>

1. <strong>Verify ownership:</strong> check Lalpurja, plot/kitta number, area, classification, ownership, mortgages, and restrictions.
2. <strong>Confirm the correct office:</strong> use the Malpot office with jurisdiction over the property.
3. <strong>Check valuation and charges:</strong> confirm minimum valuation, registration rate, local charges, and taxes.
4. <strong>Prepare documents:</strong> gather buyer/seller originals, copies, clearance documents, and Rajinama.
5. <strong>Submit at the office:</strong> buyer, seller, or properly authorized representatives attend as required.
6. <strong>Pay fees and taxes:</strong> settle buyer registration/local costs and seller capital-gains tax at designated counters.
7. <strong>Update records:</strong> the office records the transfer and updates the Lalpurja.
8. <strong>Collect ownership documents:</strong> keep the updated Lalpurja, deed, receipts, and payment confirmations safely.

<h2 id="due-diligence">Due Diligence Before Paying for Land</h2>

- <strong>Title:</strong> confirm the seller is the registered owner and identify every co-owner.
- <strong>Parcel details:</strong> match kitta number, area, ward, boundaries, classification, and road access with Lalpurja and survey records.
- <strong>Restrictions:</strong> check mortgage or rokka, court orders, acquisition notices, Guthi restrictions, easements, and boundary disputes.
- <strong>Valuation:</strong> obtain the current FY 2083/84 minimum valuation for the exact ward and road category.
- <strong>Taxes:</strong> confirm land-revenue and local-property-tax clearance before the deed is executed.
- <strong>Payment:</strong> keep bank records and avoid under-declaring the real transaction value; it can create tax, resale, and enforcement risk.
- <strong>Maps:</strong> use the Survey Office or Mero Kitta services to cross-check cadastral information where available.

<h2 id="budget">How to Estimate Your Total Land Buying Budget</h2>

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes When Buying Land in Nepal</h2>

- Calculating fees only from the negotiated price and ignoring government valuation
- Using outdated fiscal-year rates or assuming Kathmandu rates apply nationwide
- Confusing registration fee, capital-gains tax, and local tax
- Assuming the buyer pays every cost even when the seller has a separate legal tax obligation
- Not checking local charges such as a provincial or municipal fund
- Failing to verify Lalpurja, kitta, area, ownership, encumbrances, and tax dues
- Forgetting legal, valuation, financing, and documentation costs
- Relying on old social-media or blog figures instead of the relevant Malpot office

<h2 id="checklist">Important Checks Before Paying for Land</h2>

- <strong>Ownership:</strong> seller identity matches Lalpurja and all co-owners are accounted for.
- <strong>Property details:</strong> kitta, area, location, ward, tole, and classification match the records.
- <strong>Encumbrances:</strong> no undisclosed mortgage, dispute, restriction, easement, or right of way.
- <strong>Tax:</strong> current land taxes and dues are cleared.
- <strong>Valuation:</strong> current minimum valuation and applicable rate are confirmed.
- <strong>Office:</strong> correct Malpot jurisdiction and current document checklist are verified.
