<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer</h2>

Exact age is a subtraction in three parts — years, then months, then days:

1. <strong>Years</strong> — subtract the birth year from the current year. If your birthday has not yet occurred this year, subtract one more.
2. <strong>Months</strong> — subtract the birth month from the current month, borrowing 12 if needed.
3. <strong>Days</strong> — subtract the birth day from the current day, borrowing the real length of the previous month if needed.

Example: born <strong>15 August 1995</strong>, and today is <strong>17 October 2026</strong>.

- Years: 2026 − 1995 = 31, and the August birthday has already passed, so <strong>31 years</strong>.
- Months: 10 − 8 = <strong>2 months</strong>.
- Days: 17 − 15 = <strong>2 days</strong>.

<strong>Result: 31 years, 2 months and 2 days.</strong>

<h2 id="why-exact">Why Exact Age Matters in Nepal</h2>

Age is not a curiosity — it is a field that gates services and documents. Situations where an exact date of birth is required include:

- **Passport applications** — both the e-passport application and renewals record the date of birth and, for minors, require additional supporting documents. See our <a href="/guides/e-passport-online-application-nepal-guide">e-passport guide</a> and <a href="/guides/nepali-passport-renewal-process-documents-fees">passport renewal guide</a>.
- **Driving licences** — the licence category you can apply for depends on your age, and the trial and portal steps are age-checked. See <a href="/guides/nepal-online-driving-license-edlvrs-portal-guide">applying online</a> and the <a href="/guides/nepal-driving-license-trial-date-route-guide">trial, date and route guide</a>.
- **Voter registration** — voter ID is issued to citizens aged 18 and above; the age is assessed on the qualifying date used by the Election Commission. See the <a href="/blog/voter-id-registration-nepal-online-guide">voter ID guide</a>.
- **National ID (NID)** — date of birth is a core field in NID registration; see the <a href="/guides/national-id-card-online-registration-nepal-guide">NID registration guide</a>.
- **Senior citizen allowance and pension schemes** — these use age cut-offs that differ from scheme to scheme, so check the current rule with the responsible office rather than assuming one number. See our <a href="/guides/social-security-allowance-senior-citizen-rates-nepal">social security allowance guide</a>.
- **School admission and exams** — forms routinely ask for age in years and months.

Because Nepali documents mix the BS and AD calendars, the same person can appear to have two different dates of birth until you convert between the systems.

<h2 id="method-ad">The Manual Method (AD Dates)</h2>

Work top-down and never subtract all three at once:

1. Write the current date as year, month, day, and the birth date the same way.
2. <strong>Subtract years.</strong> If the current month/day is <em>before</em> the birth month/day this year, subtract 1.
3. <strong>Subtract months.</strong> If the current day is smaller than the birth day, borrow one month and add the number of days in the previous month to the current day.
4. <strong>Subtract days.</strong>
5. Check your answer by adding the result back to the birth date — you should land exactly on the reference date.

Step 5 is the step most people skip, and it is the fastest way to catch a borrowing error.

<h2 id="worked-ad">Worked Examples in AD</h2>

| Birth date | Reference date | Working | Exact age |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 August 1995 | 17 October 2026 | Years 31; months 2; days 2 | 31 y 2 m 2 d |
| 20 December 1998 | 5 March 2026 | Birthday not yet reached → years 27; Dec 20 + 2 m = Feb 20, + 13 d | 27 y 2 m 13 d |
| 3 January 2000 | 3 January 2026 | Birthday exactly today | 26 y 0 m 0 d |
| 7 July 1985 | 1 March 2026 | Birthday not yet reached → years 40; Jul 7 + 7 m = Feb 7, + 22 d (Feb 2026 has 28 days) | 40 y 7 m 22 d |

To check the last row: 7 July 1985 + 40 years = 7 July 2025. + 7 months = 7 February 2026. + 22 days = 1 March 2026. Correct.

<h2 id="method-bs">Calculating Age in Bikram Sambat</h2>

The subtraction is identical, with one catch: <strong>BS months are not a fixed length.</strong> They run from 29 to 32 days depending on the year, so when you borrow across a month you must use the actual length of that specific BS month, not 30.

That is the whole reason BS age arithmetic is harder than AD arithmetic:

- A BS year is roughly 365.25 days long, so years and months behave predictably at the top level.
- Within a year, month lengths are set by the official calendar and change year to year.
- Some BS years include an extra month, which shifts the numbering for the rest of the year.

Practical approach: convert or calculate with a calendar-based tool rather than by hand, and use the official national calendar when a document depends on the answer. Our <a href="/nepal/age-calculator">age calculator</a> and <a href="/nepal/date/bs-to-ad">BS to AD converter</a> use BS month-length tables, so the borrowing is handled for you.

<h2 id="worked-bs">A Worked BS Example</h2>

> **BS-age worked example:** Suppose you were born on 2040-05-15 BS and today is 2083-06-24 BS. Years: 2083 − 2040 = 43, and since month 06 is past month 05, the birthday has occurred — so 43 full years. Months: 06 − 05 = 1 month. Days: 24 − 15 = 9 days. Result: <strong>43 years, 1 month, 9 days</strong> (BS). Because BS month lengths vary, borrowing across months must use the actual length of the specific BS month — which is why a converter is more reliable than mental maths.

Here the days and months subtracted cleanly, so no borrowing was needed. If the reference day had been earlier than the birth day — say 2083-06-10 against a 2040-05-15 birth date — you would have borrowed the length of BS month 05 of 2083, whatever that happened to be, and the answer would depend on the calendar for that year.

<h2 id="other-units">Age in Months, Weeks and Days</h2>

Age in months:

```text
months = (years × 12) + months
```

So 31 years 2 months is (31 × 12) + 2 = <strong>374 months</strong>.

Age in days and weeks: multiply years by 365 only as a rough estimate, then be aware that leap years add about one day every four years. A person aged 31 has lived approximately 11,323 days (31 × 365 = 11,315, plus 8 leap days) — but the exact figure depends on which leap years fall inside their lifetime and on whether the counting rule is inclusive.

For anything where the day count matters — eligibility windows, notice periods, statutory deadlines — count with a date tool rather than multiplying.

<h2 id="official-age">Official Age vs Actual Age: Completed Years</h2>

Almost every official form asks for age in <strong>completed years</strong>. That means:

- You are 27 until the day you turn 28.
- "27 years" is correct even if you are 27 years and 11 months.
- Birthdays do not round up.

This is why the same person filling in a form in March and again in December can legitimately write two different ages — and why the exact date of birth, not the age number, is what actually appears on passports, citizenship certificates and licences.

Related convention: because BS and AD calendars do not align, a document that records a BS date of birth may show a different "birthday" than a document recording the AD equivalent. Both can be accurate. What matters is that the underlying date is the same day, converted correctly.

<h2 id="edge-cases">Leap Years and 29 February Birthdays</h2>

- **Leap years** — 29 February is added every four years, which is why day counts drift from the 365-day estimate.
- **Born on 29 February** — in a non-leap year there is no 29 February. Some institutions treat the birthday as 28 February, others as 1 March. There is no single universal rule, and the two readings can shift an age-related eligibility by a day. If a document depends on it, confirm with the office issuing the document.
- **Year-end birthdays** — someone born on 31 December and assessed on 1 January has "just" had a birthday, whereas the same person assessed on 30 December is a full year younger in completed-years terms. Always check the reference date the authority uses.
- **BS new year** — Bikram Sambat rolls over in mid-April, so a person's BS birth year and AD birth year will differ by 56 or 57 depending on the month of birth.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>

- <strong>Subtracting the birth year directly when the birthday has not occurred.</strong> This is the single most common error, and it inflates age by one year.
- <strong>Borrowing 30 days in BS arithmetic.</strong> Borrow the real length of that BS month instead.
- <strong>Multiplying years by 365 for an exact day count.</strong>
- <strong>Averaging months as 30 days when converting to weeks.</strong>
- <strong>Using the AD date for a BS-based form without converting.</strong> Always state which calendar a date is in.
- <strong>Assuming a "birthday" exists in every year</strong>, which breaks for 29 February births.
