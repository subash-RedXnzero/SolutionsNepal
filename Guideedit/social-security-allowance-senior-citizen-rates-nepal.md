<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: Social Security Allowance Rates in 2026</h2>

Nepal has several Social Security Allowance categories, so there is no single universal monthly payment. Eligibility depends on:

- age
- disability classification
- widow or single-woman status
- Dalit or former-Karnali eligibility
- child-grant rules
- endangered-community status
- citizenship
- registration
- current government policy

- **General senior citizen** — 68+ years (lowered from 70; confirm the current age rule at DONIDCR) — commonly Rs 4,000/month (as of October 2026)
- **Dalit senior citizen** — 60+ years — commonly Rs 4,000/month
- **Former Karnali/designated remote senior** — 60+ years — commonly Rs 4,000/month
- **Single woman** — 60+ years — commonly Rs 2,660/month
- **Widow** — No general age floor — commonly Rs 2,660/month
- **Red Card/Class A disability** — No age floor — commonly Rs 3,990/month
- **Blue Card/Class B disability** — No age floor — commonly Rs 2,128/month
- **Endangered ethnic group** — Eligible listed groups — commonly Rs 3,990/month
- **Child grant** — Under 5, eligible categories — commonly Rs 532 per child/month

> **Verify locally:** Rates, categories, NID transition requirements, and payment schedules can change. Confirm current details with DONIDCR and your ward, municipality, or rural municipality.

<h2 id="what-is">What Is Social Security Allowance?</h2>

<strong>Social Security Allowance (Samajik Suraksha Bhatta)</strong> is Nepal's non-contributory, tax-funded cash-transfer programme for defined vulnerable groups. It is administered locally and supported by national civil-registration and beneficiary systems.

- Policy and rates are set through national government decisions and budgets.
- DONIDCR supports civil registration, beneficiary data, NID integration, and identity management.
- Ward offices, municipalities, and rural municipalities process applications and local verification.
- Payments are generally coordinated through bank accounts.

<h2 id="rates">FY 2083/84 Allowance Rate Summary</h2>

- **Senior Citizen (general)** — 68 years and above (see age note below) — commonly Rs 4,000/month as of October 2026; confirm FY 2083/84 budget figures with DONIDCR
- **Dalit senior citizen** — 60 years and above — Rs 4,000/month
- **Former Karnali/remote senior** — 60 years and above — Rs 4,000/month
- **Single woman** — 60 years and above — commonly Rs 2,660/month
- **Widow** — Any age, subject to proof — commonly Rs 2,660/month
- **Fully disabled** — Red Card/Class A — commonly Rs 3,990/month
- **Severely disabled** — Blue Card/Class B — commonly Rs 2,128/month
- **Endangered/protected group** — Listed groups — commonly Rs 3,990/month
- **Child grant** — Eligible child under 5 — commonly Rs 532/month per child, usually up to two

These are monthly entitlement figures as commonly published as of October 2026. The actual bank deposit usually combines several months into one instalment. Category figures other than the senior Rs 4,000 (widow/single-woman Rs 2,660, red-card Rs 3,990, blue-card Rs 2,128, child grant Rs 532) still need explicit FY 2083/84 budget confirmation — treat them as commonly published, not gazetted, until verified with DONIDCR and the current budget notice.

<h2 id="senior-age">Senior Citizen Allowance Age</h2>

The general senior-citizen allowance age is commonly <strong>68 years</strong> under the current framework (lowered from 70 — confirm the current rule at <a href="https://donidcr.gov.np">DONIDCR</a> or the latest budget notice before applying, as of October 2026). People already receiving benefits during a rule change may have grandfathering protection, so existing beneficiaries below a new threshold should confirm continuity with the ward office rather than assuming cancellation.

- Dalit senior citizens commonly qualify from age 60.
- Eligible former-Karnali or designated remote-area seniors commonly qualify from age 60.
- Single women commonly qualify from age 60 under their separate category.
- Eligibility is not automatic when someone reaches the age; registration and verification are still required.

Applicants also generally need Nepali citizenship, local registration, and the identity documents requested by the local government. Non-resident status or living abroad can affect eligibility.

<h2 id="senior-rate">Senior Citizen Allowance Rate</h2>

The commonly published general senior-citizen amount for FY 2083/84 is <strong>Rs 4,000 per month</strong> (as of October 2026 — confirm against the current budget notice and DONIDCR). A senior citizen may therefore see Rs 12,000 for a three-month cycle or Rs 16,000 for a four-month cycle, depending on the local payment schedule.

Local governments administer enrolment and payment. Timing can differ by municipality, banking arrangement, verification status, and budget release.

<h2 id="women-widows">Single-Woman and Widow Allowance</h2>

- **Widow** — No general minimum age; commonly Rs 2,660/month. Spouse death certificate and identity documents are normally required.
- **Unmarried, divorced, or legally separated woman** — Commonly age 60+ and Rs 2,660/month, with proof of marital status.
- **Dalit or eligible former-Karnali woman** — May qualify under the senior-citizen category at Rs 4,000/month from age 60 rather than the single-woman rate.

Widow and single-woman categories are distinct. A change in marital status can affect eligibility and should be reported to the local office.

<h2 id="disability">Disability Allowance</h2>

- **Red Card/Class A** — Complete or profound disability — commonly Rs 3,990/month
- **Blue Card/Class B** — Severe disability — commonly Rs 2,128/month
- **Yellow Card/Class C** — Generally not included in the cash allowance category
- **White Card/Class D** — Generally not included in the cash allowance category

There is generally no age floor for qualifying disability categories. A valid government-issued disability identity card and local verification are required.

<h2 id="child">Child Grant</h2>

The Child Grant (Bal Suraksha Anudan) is a cash transfer for eligible children under five. The commonly cited rate is Rs 532 per child per month, often limited to two children per household.

- Dalit children under five can qualify nationwide under the programme rules.
- Children under five in designated districts can qualify under geographic rollout rules.
- Birth registration and parent or guardian identity documents are essential.
- The payment is often accumulated and paid through the caregiver's bank account.

<h2 id="endangered">Endangered Ethnic Groups</h2>

Members of listed endangered or protected indigenous groups may qualify for an allowance commonly published at Rs 3,990 per month, subject to official recognition and local verification.

Commonly listed groups include:

- Bankariya
- Hayu
- Kisan
- Kusbadhiya
- Kusunda
- Lepcha
- Meche
- Raji
- Raute
- Surel

Confirm the current official list and proof requirements before applying.

<h2 id="donidcr">DONIDCR and NID Integration</h2>

The Department of National ID and Civil Registration (DONIDCR) supports civil-registration records, beneficiary data, National ID integration, and biometric identity management. Local governments remain the primary point of contact for applications and payments.

- NID and biometric verification are increasingly required for new enrolment and payment continuity.
- Existing beneficiaries may receive a transition period to complete NID enrolment.
- Name, date of birth, Citizenship, and NID mismatches can delay or suspend payment.
- Alternative verification may be available when fingerprints or other biometrics cannot be captured.

<h2 id="apply">How to Apply Through Your Ward or Municipality</h2>

1. <strong>Check eligibility:</strong> confirm category, age, citizenship, status, and supporting proof.
2. <strong>Visit the local office:</strong> go to the ward office or municipality/rural-municipality registration section.
3. <strong>Submit documents:</strong> provide Citizenship, photographs, category proof, NID information, and bank details.
4. <strong>Complete verification:</strong> officials check civil-registration records, age, status, and category.
5. <strong>Receive beneficiary registration:</strong> the local office adds you to the relevant beneficiary database and may issue a Social Security ID card.
6. <strong>Complete NID/biometrics:</strong> enrol or update National ID and biometric information when required.
7. <strong>Set up payment:</strong> provide or open a bank account accepted for allowance transfers.
8. <strong>Check the first payment:</strong> confirm the bank deposit and local payment cycle.

Senior citizens may have a local registration deadline such as Mangsir 15 for timely inclusion, while other categories can have different local schedules. Confirm with the ward office.

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

- **All applicants** — Citizenship original and copy, photographs, local address details, NID where available, and bank information.
- **Widow** — Spouse death certificate and marital-status documents.
- **Single woman** — Proof of unmarried, divorced, or legally separated status.
- **Disability** — Valid Red or Blue disability identity card.
- **Child grant** — Child birth-registration certificate, parent/guardian Citizenship, and Dalit or designated-district proof.
- **Dalit/Karnali/remote category** — Proof of community status or qualifying residence where requested.
- **Endangered group** — Citizenship or other official record showing recognized ethnicity.

Local offices can request additional documents. Carry originals and copies and confirm the current checklist before visiting.

<h2 id="payment">Payment Process and Lump-Sum Cycles</h2>

Allowance rates are monthly entitlements, but payments are generally credited in accumulated instalments every three or four months. A Rs 4,000 senior allowance therefore appears as approximately Rs 12,000 for three months or Rs 16,000 for four months.

- Payments are generally made through bank accounts rather than cash at the ward.
- Zero-balance account arrangements may be available for eligible beneficiaries.
- Payment dates differ by local level and budget release.
- Keep bank statements and payment messages as evidence.

<h2 id="status">How to Check Status or Missing Payments</h2>

1. Check the beneficiary bank account statement.
2. Ask the ward office or municipality to confirm your beneficiary status and payment period.
3. Verify bank account number and beneficiary details with the local office.
4. Ask whether NID, biometric, civil-registration, or bank-data matching is pending.
5. Use DONIDCR or National ID online services only when the current official system provides a status feature.

There may not be one universally available nationwide online status portal. The local ward or municipality is normally the first and most reliable contact.

<h2 id="renewal">Verification, Updates, and Suspension</h2>

Beneficiaries usually do not reapply from scratch every year, but they may need identity, biometric, bank, or status updates. Report changes promptly.

- Update address when moving to another municipality.
- Report marriage, remarriage, or other marital-status changes.
- Report the beneficiary's death immediately.
- Correct Citizenship and NID discrepancies.
- Update bank details through the local office.
- Complete periodic verification when requested.

Payments may be suspended for death, loss of eligibility, duplicate registration, false documents, incorrect records, or failure to complete required verification. Ask for the reason and request correction or review if the suspension is wrong.

<h2 id="ssf">Social Security Allowance vs SSF</h2>

- **Social Security Allowance** — Non-contributory, tax-funded welfare benefit for eligible vulnerable groups.
- **Social Security Fund (SSF)** — Contribution-based insurance and retirement scheme for covered workers and employers.
- **Funding** — Allowance: government revenue | SSF: employee and employer contributions.
- **Eligibility** — Allowance: age, status, disability, child, or community category | SSF: employment or contributor enrolment.

Do not confuse the allowance with SSF. A household may receive a Social Security Allowance for an eligible parent while a working family member contributes to SSF.

<h2 id="problems">Common Problems and Fixes</h2>

- **NID details do not match** — Visit the ward or DONIDCR with Citizenship and supporting records, request correction, then confirm the beneficiary record is updated.
- **Biometric verification fails** — Request alternative verification or accommodation through the ward/NID office, especially for elderly or disabled beneficiaries.
- **Allowance not deposited** — Check bank statement, payment cycle, beneficiary status, bank details, and local-office notices.
- **Moved municipality** — Report the move and request beneficiary-record transfer at the new local office.
- **Beneficiary died** — Report death with the certificate immediately and stop further withdrawals.
- **Duplicate registration** — Ask the local office to remove the duplicate and retain the correct beneficiary record.
- **Citizenship error** — Correct the source Citizenship record first, then update NID and allowance records.

<h2 id="checklist">Application Checklist</h2>

- Confirm the correct allowance category and age/status rule.
- Prepare Citizenship original and copies.
- Bring category-specific proof such as death, birth, disability, marital, Dalit, residence, or ethnicity documents.
- Carry NID information or complete NID enrolment if required.
- Bring photographs and bank-account details.
- Apply at the ward or municipality registration office.
- Complete biometric and record verification.
- Save the Social Security ID or beneficiary reference.
- Check the bank statement after the local payment cycle.
- Report address, marital-status, bank, identity, or death changes promptly.

<h2 id="sources">Official Sources and Final Note</h2>

Use the <a href="https://donidcr.gov.np">Department of National ID and Civil Registration</a>, the relevant ward, municipality, or rural municipality, Ministry of Home Affairs, Nepal Law Commission, and current Government of Nepal budget notices (Ministry of Finance) for the latest rates and procedures. Age and rate figures above are as of October 2026 and should be rechecked against the gazetted FY 2083/84 budget and DONIDCR notices.

Social Security Allowance rates, eligibility, NID requirements, biometric procedures, local deadlines, and payment schedules can change. This guide is general information and should be confirmed with DONIDCR and the relevant local government before applying.
