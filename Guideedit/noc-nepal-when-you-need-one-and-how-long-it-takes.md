<h2 id="what">What Is the NOC?</h2>

The NOC — officially the Foreign Study Approval Letter — is a government document issued by Nepal's Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST) through its Foreign Study Permit Branch. It confirms that the Government of Nepal has no objection to you studying at a specific institution in a specific country and permits you to use Nepali banks to legally send tuition and living expenses abroad.

As of 2026, the process runs through the official portal, <strong>noc.moest.gov.np</strong>. The approved certificate is a digitally signed PDF with a scannable QR code that banks, embassies, and universities can verify.

<h2 id="who">Who Needs an NOC?</h2>

- You are enrolling in a language course, diploma, bachelor's, master's, or PhD program abroad
- You have an offer letter, CAS (UK), or COE (Australia) from a foreign institution
- You need to remit tuition or living expenses through a Nepali bank
- Your destination country's embassy requires it for your student visa

You generally do not need one for a short conference or workshop under three months with no formal enrollment, or for a program fully funded by the host government with fees handled externally. When in doubt, apply.

<h2 id="fees">NOC Fee and Processing Time (2026)</h2>

- **NOC fee** — NPR 2,000 (all levels of study)
- **Payment methods** — ConnectIPS, eSewa, Khalti, or a designated bank counter
- **Processing time** — Commonly 1–5 working days after document verification; allow up to 7 working days in peak season
- **Application mode** — Fully online; Sanothimi visit rarely needed
- **Validity** — Until course intake/commencement, generally treated as around 6 months from issue
- **Scope** — Tied to one institution, one course, and one intake

During peak application months, roughly February to August, processing tends toward the higher end of the range and can take up to 7 working days. Build in buffer time.

<h2 id="documents">Documents Required</h2>

Prepare clear scans in PDF or high-quality JPEG format. Scanner-quality images are strongly preferred over phone photos because blurry uploads are a common cause of rejection.

- Nepali citizenship certificate, both sides
- Valid passport bio page and any pages with visa or travel history
- Academic transcripts, including SEE/SLC and +2 or equivalent
- Offer letter, CAS, or COE from the foreign institution
- Language proficiency certificate (IELTS/TOEFL/PTE) if the offer is conditional on it
- Bank statement or other financial document showing study support
- Recent passport-size photo
- NOC fee payment receipt

Originals are preferred. If unavailable, notarized copies may be accepted according to the current portal or office instructions.

<h2 id="process">Step-by-Step: How to Apply for NOC Online</h2>

1. <strong>Secure an unconditional offer letter.</strong> It should show your name, institution, country, program, study level, and intake date.
2. <strong>Register on the MoEST NOC portal.</strong> Go to <a href="https://noc.moest.gov.np">noc.moest.gov.np</a>, create an account with your email and mobile number, and verify the OTP.
3. <strong>Fill in your application.</strong> Match your citizenship details, academic background, institution, course, level, and intake exactly.
4. <strong>Upload your documents.</strong> Review every file and field before submitting.
5. <strong>Pay the NOC fee.</strong> Pay NPR 2,000 through ConnectIPS, eSewa, Khalti, or the designated bank counter, then save the confirmation.
6. <strong>Submit and track.</strong> MoEST typically works Sunday to Friday, with verification commonly taking 1–5 working days (up to 7 in peak season).
7. <strong>Download your NOC.</strong> After approval, download the digitally signed certificate with its QR code and keep printed and digital backups.

<h2 id="status">How to Check Your NOC Application Status</h2>

1. Go to <strong>noc.moest.gov.np</strong> and log in with your registered credentials.
2. Open your application dashboard to see whether it is under review, awaiting additional documents, rejected with comments, or approved.
3. Watch for SMS updates as your status changes.
4. If the status says <strong>Rejected with comments</strong>, correct only the specified issue and resubmit. Do not alter unrelated fields.
5. Once approved, use the dashboard download option to get your certificate.

There is no need to repeatedly call or visit the office while your status is unchanged; updates are pushed automatically once a decision is made.

<h2 id="banking">The NRB Banking Rule You Should Know Before You Transfer Fees</h2>

A Nepal Rastra Bank rule is reportedly applied alongside the NOC whereby, after your first tuition or living-expense transfer through a specific bank, subsequent payments for that course must go through the same bank, with the original NOC submitted as supporting documentation each time — confirm with your bank and NRB before your first transfer, as the exact directive could not be sourced to a dated NRB circular as of October 2026. Compare remittance speed, exchange rates, and service quality before making that first transfer.

<h2 id="validity">NOC Validity: What Trips Students Up</h2>

- The NOC may not carry a printed expiry date, but in practice it is treated as valid for around six months from issue and only until the course start date.
- It is institution-, course-, and intake-specific. A changed university, program, or intake requires a fresh NOC.
- If your visa application is rejected and you reapply, you need a new NOC.
- If your intake is deferred, a new NOC reflecting the updated offer letter is required.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes That Delay or Reject Applications</h2>

- Applying before the offer letter is unconditional
- Name spelling that does not exactly match your citizenship, passport, and offer letter
- Low-quality or blurry document scans
- Institution or course details that do not exactly match the offer letter
- Starting too close to a visa appointment
- Assuming one NOC covers more than one application, institution, or intake
- Not saving the fee payment receipt
- Choosing a remittance bank without accounting for the reportedly applicable NRB single-bank practice

<h2 id="timeline">Suggested Timeline</h2>

- **4–6 months before intake** — Finalize university applications
- **3–4 months before intake** — Receive a confirmed, unconditional offer letter
- **Immediately after offer** — Register on noc.moest.gov.np and start the application
- **1–5 working days after submission (up to 7 in peak season)** — Download your approved NOC
- **After NOC approval** — Choose your remittance bank and transfer tuition fees
- **After fee transfer** — Complete your visa application
- **4–8 weeks before intake** — Receive your visa and book travel
