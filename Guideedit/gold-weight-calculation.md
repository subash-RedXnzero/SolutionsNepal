  - q: How many grams is 1 tola of gold in Nepal?

  - q: What is 5 grams of gold in tola?
    a: About 0.4287 tola. Work it out as 5 ÷ 11.6638 = 0.4287. In the other direction, 0.5 tola is about 5.832 grams.
  - q: Is gold measured per tola or per 10 grams in Nepal?
    a: Gold rates published for Nepal are quoted per tola (hallmark gold), and prices per gram and per 10 grams are derived from that. Silver is commonly quoted per tola and per 10 grams. Always confirm which unit a quote uses before comparing.
  - q: What is 1 pawa of gold?
    a: Pawa (and kori) are older or informal subdivisions of the tola that do not have one consistent definition across shops and regions. Because the value is ambiguous, avoid quoting or paying by pawa — ask for the weight in grams instead.
  - q: How do I convert a gold price per tola into a price per gram?

<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer</h2>

Gold in Nepal is quoted by the <strong>tola</strong>, not by the gram, which is why a rate that looks like a round number can be hard to compare with a shop bill.

- <strong>1 tola</strong> = 11.6638 grams (shop talk rounds this to 11.664 g)
- <strong>Tola to grams</strong> — multiply by 11.6638
- <strong>Grams to tola</strong> — divide by 11.6638
- <strong>Half tola</strong> — about 5.832 grams
- <strong>Quarter tola</strong> — about 2.916 grams
- <strong>10 grams</strong> — about 0.8574 tola
- <strong>Per-tola rate to per-gram price</strong> — divide the rate by 11.6638

> **Convert any weight instantly:** use the <a href="/nepal/gold/tola-to-gram">tola to gram converter</a> or the <a href="/nepal/gold/gram-to-tola">gram to tola converter</a>. For today's published rate and price calculations, see the <a href="/nepal/gold-price-nepal">gold price calculator</a>.

The two units you actually need are <strong>tola</strong> and <strong>gram</strong>. Everything else — lal, aana, pawa, kori — turns up in conversation and in old bills, but is not defined consistently enough to rely on. If a shop quotes you a weight in lal or pawa, ask for the same weight in grams.

<h2 id="units">Gold Measurement Units in Nepal</h2>

Nepal kept the traditional subcontinental gold units rather than switching to the metric system for bullion:

- <strong>Tola</strong> — the base unit of the Nepali gold market, equal to 11.6638 grams. Rates published for Nepal are quoted per tola, so this is the number your bill is built from.
- <strong>Gram</strong> — the metric unit used in manufacturing, hallmarking and export paperwork. One gram is about 0.0857 tola.
- <strong>10 grams</strong> — a convenient metric unit used for coins, bars and silver, and easy to compute from the per-tola rate.
- <strong>Aana</strong> — a traditional subdivision of the tola. Nepali gold pricing references commonly treat 12 aana as 1 tola, making one aana about 0.972 grams. Treat it as approximate and confirm the shop's basis.
- <strong>Lal</strong> — the smallest traditional subdivision. References differ: some divide the tola into 96 lal, others into 100. That is a difference of about 4% on every lal you buy.
- <strong>Pawa and kori</strong> — older terms that appear in informal and historical usage. They do not convert cleanly to grams, so they cannot be used for pricing.

> **Note:** older or informal breakdowns describe a tola in pawa or kori terms. Because the definitions vary between shops and regions — and because different Nepali references divide the tola differently between lal and aana — always confirm the weight in grams before agreeing a price.

<h2 id="sub-units">Lal, Aana, Pawa and Kori — Handle With Care</h2>

Here is the problem stated plainly: <strong>the sub-tola units are not standardised.</strong>

- Divide 1 tola into <strong>96 lal</strong> and 1 lal is about 0.1215 grams.
- Divide 1 tola into <strong>100 lal</strong> and 1 lal is about 0.1166 grams.
- Treat 1 tola as <strong>12 aana</strong> and 1 aana is about 0.972 grams.
- Ask two shops what a <strong>pawa</strong> is and you may get two different answers.

That 96-versus-100 difference looks trivial and is not: on a 1-tola purchase the two readings are roughly 4% apart, and the same ambiguity exists for aana and pawa. Percentages of this size are larger than the spread between competing shops on the same day.

The practical rule:

1. <strong>Ask for the weight in grams</strong> and write it on the bill.
2. <strong>Check the fineness number</strong> (999, 916, 750) at the same time.
3. <strong>Ignore the sub-unit entirely</strong> when comparing prices across shops.
4. <strong>If a shop insists on lal or pawa</strong>, ask for the gram equivalent of that exact unit before you agree anything.

This site's own gold price tools use 12 aana and 96 lal per tola, while our hallmark guide uses the 100-lal convention widely quoted in shop talk — which is precisely why the safe answer to "how many grams is a lal?" is "ask for grams".

<h2 id="conversions">Conversion Formulas</h2>

The core conversion, and the two variants you will actually use:

```text
Grams = tola × 11.6638
```

```text
Tola = grams ÷ 11.6638
```

```text
Price per gram = price per tola ÷ 11.6638
```

```text
Price per tola = price per gram × 11.6638
```

Three shortcuts worth remembering for mental arithmetic:

- <strong>Per tola to per 10 grams</strong> — divide the tola rate by 1.16638. A rate of Rs 2,92,000 per tola is about Rs 2,50,350 per 10 grams.
- <strong>Per gram to per tola</strong> — multiply by 11.6638. A Rs 25,035 per-gram rate is about Rs 2,92,000 per tola.
- <strong>Tola to grams, roughly</strong> — 1 tola is "about 11.66 grams", so ten tolas are about 116.6 grams. Good enough to sanity-check a bill; use the exact factor when money changes hands.

<h2 id="price">How Gold Price Works in Nepal</h2>

The rate you see quoted is a base metal rate, not the price you will pay for a finished ornament. The sequence is roughly this:

1. <strong>International spot price</strong> — the global benchmark for gold.
2. <strong>Currency conversion</strong> — converted into Nepali rupees using the reference exchange rate published by <a href="https://www.nrb.org.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Nepal Rastra Bank (nrb.org.np)</a>.
3. <strong>Duties and applicable taxes</strong> — import duty and tax measures applied on entry.
4. <strong>Purity adjustment</strong> — the rate is published for a stated purity, commonly fine gold (999) with a separate rate for 22K jewellery gold.
5. <strong>Per-tola quote</strong> — dealers publish the resulting rate per tola, and price per gram or per 10 grams is derived from it.
6. <strong>Shop additions</strong> — making or labour charges, sometimes wastage (jarti), stone and gem charges, and applicable taxes on the final bill.

Steps 1 to 4 are the market's business; steps 5 and 6 are where your bill is actually decided. Our <a href="/guides/buying-gold-nepal-hallmark-tola-purity-guide">hallmark and purity guide</a> walks through the rate factors and how a jewellery bill is assembled, and the <a href="/nepal/gold-price-nepal">gold price calculator</a> applies the current published rate to any weight.

Rate publication: Nepali gold and silver rates are published by dealer bodies for each working day, and the rate generally does not change on Saturdays or public holidays. A rate you remember from last week is not today's rate.

<h2 id="purity">Purity Changes the Price You Actually Pay</h2>

Gold is weighed in tola but priced by fineness — how many parts in a thousand are actually gold:

- <strong>24K / 999</strong> — fine gold, the benchmark for investment bars and coins.
- <strong>22K / 916</strong> — the common jewellery standard, about 916 parts per thousand gold.
- <strong>18K / 750</strong> — lighter, harder alloys used in stone-set jewellery.

The consequence is simple and often missed: a gram of 22K jewellery gold is not worth the same as a gram of 24K fine gold, even though both weigh a gram. A per-tola rate is meaningful only when you know which purity it refers to. When you compare quotes, compare three things together:

1. <strong>Weight in grams.</strong>
2. <strong>Fineness number</strong> (999, 916, 750).
3. <strong>Total bill</strong> including making, wastage and taxes.

Two shops quoting the same rate per tola can still be 10% apart on the finished price, because the rate is only the first line of the bill. Look for the hallmark stamp and, where available, NBSM certification — standards information is published by the <a href="https://www.nbsm.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Nepal Bureau of Standards and Metrology (nbsm.gov.np)</a>.

<h2 id="at-the-shop">What to Do at the Shop</h2>

- <strong>Ask for the weight in grams</strong>, and check the bill shows grams and tola.
- <strong>Check the fineness number</strong> and look for the hallmark stamp.
- <strong>Ask for an itemised bill</strong> — gold value, making charge, wastage and taxes shown separately.
- <strong>Ask the current rate and the rate basis</strong> — per tola, for which purity.
- <strong>Ask about buyback and exchange</strong> before you pay, not after.
- <strong>Keep the invoice.</strong> It is your only proof of weight, purity and price when you sell or exchange.
- <strong>Do the arithmetic in grams.</strong> Grams are unambiguous; lal and pawa are not.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes</h2>

- <strong>Confusing tola and gram.</strong> A "1,000" quote per 10 grams is not 1,000 per tola — the units differ by more than ten times.
- <strong>Assuming a lal is a fixed weight.</strong> 96 and 100 per tola are both in circulation.
- <strong>Pricing 22K jewellery at the 24K rate</strong> without a purity adjustment.
- <strong>Comparing shop totals when one bill hides wastage inside the making charge.</strong>
- <strong>Using last week's rate.</strong> Rates are published per working day.
- <strong>Not asking the buyback rate.</strong> The gap between buying and selling prices is where resale losses appear.
- <strong>Ignoring making charges when comparing shops.</strong> On small pieces, labour can be a large share of the bill.
- <strong>Trusting an informal pawa or kori quote.</strong> If it is not in grams, it is not checkable.
