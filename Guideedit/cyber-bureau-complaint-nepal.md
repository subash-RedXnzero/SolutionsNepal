<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: How to Report Cybercrime in Nepal</h2>

Report suspected cybercrime — online payment fraud, hacked or fake accounts, harassment, blackmail, or someone posting your private images — through <strong>Nepal Police Cyber Bureau</strong>. The official route is:

1. <strong>Pick the right form</strong> on the official complaint page: <a href="https://cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np/report-cyber-crime/">cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np/report-cyber-crime</a>
2. <strong>Attach the two mandatory items</strong>: a copy of your identity document and the relevant URLs plus clear screenshots
3. <strong>Submit through one of three officially listed channels</strong>: any district/area/ward police office or police circle or station, the Cyber Bureau itself at Bhotahity, Kathmandu, or email with complete details to <a href="mailto:cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np">cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np</a>
4. <strong>Keep your copy and registration number</strong>, then follow up using the official contact page: <a href="https://cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np/about-us/contact-us/">cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np/about-us/contact-us</a>

The Bureau publishes a 24×7 duty line: <strong>+977 9851286770</strong> or <strong>01-5319044 extension 100</strong> for general inquiries. All complaint steps, forms, contact details, and submission channels in this guide were checked against the official Cyber Bureau pages on <strong>October 17, 2026</strong>.

> **No online portal:** As of the last check, the Cyber Bureau site provides forms and instructions — not an online complaint-filing or case-tracking system. Anything claiming to "file your case online for a fee" is not the official process.

<h2 id="channels">Where to Report: Three Official Channels</h2>

The complaint page lists three ways to submit a completed complaint with its attachments:

- **Any local police office** — the nearest district police office, area or ward police office, or a police circle or station. This is the practical route anywhere in Nepal; you do not need to reach Kathmandu.
- **In person at the Cyber Bureau** — Nepal Police Headquarters, Cyber Bureau, Bhotahity, Kathmandu.
- **By email** — if you cannot attend in person, the official page says you can email the complete details to <a href="mailto:cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np">cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np</a>.

Reporting to a nearby police office and reporting to the Cyber Bureau are both valid. For case-specific or technical follow-up, the Bureau's own contact channels are the authority.

<h2 id="types">What Counts as a Cybercrime (and Which Form Fits)</h2>

The Bureau's complaint page categorizes reports into <strong>four downloadable forms</strong>, plus a separate track for offences under the <strong>Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2008)</strong>:

- **Obscene audio, video, or photo of you or someone you know, posted on social media** — a dedicated complaint form
- **Online financial fraud (electronic-medium fraud)** — a dedicated complaint form
- **Fake or hacked social media ID (to recover or close it)** — a dedicated complaint form
- **Anything not covered above** — the general "Other" complaint form
- **Source-code theft, unauthorized access, publication of illegal material, and related computer offences** — follow the ETA 2063 Section 9 branch, with organization documents (see the dedicated section below)

Open every form from the official page so you get the current version — the financial-fraud form on that page is a recent revision, which shows the Bureau updates these files. Forms can be filled by typing or by handwriting in the same format, in English, Nepali, or both.

<h2 id="process">Step-by-Step: Filing a Cyber Bureau Complaint</h2>

1. <strong>Identify the type of incident.</strong> Match it to one of the four categories above, or the ETA 2063 branch for computer-system offences.
2. <strong>Preserve evidence first.</strong> Before deleting anything or changing accounts, photograph or export chats, posts, listings, and payment records. Deleting material can remove exactly the proof the complaint needs.
3. <strong>Open the official reporting page</strong> and confirm the address ends in <strong>cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np</strong>.
4. <strong>Download the matching complaint form.</strong> Use the dedicated form for your category, or the general form if none fits.
5. <strong>Complete the complaint accurately.</strong> Write or type the details available to you: what happened, when, where, and with whom. Typed and handwritten applications are accepted; institutional complaints should use official letterhead with the organization's stamp.
6. <strong>Prepare the mandatory attachments:</strong> copy of the complainant's identity document, plus URLs and clear screenshots of the offending content or messages.
7. <strong>Submit through an accepted channel.</strong> Hand it in at the nearest police office, go to the Cyber Bureau at Bhotahity, or email the complete package to the official address.
8. <strong>Keep your own record.</strong> Save a copy of the application and every attachment, note the date and place of submission, and record the registration number if the receiving office issues one.
9. <strong>Follow up officially.</strong> Use the Bureau's contact page and hotline extensions — see the follow-up section below.

> **Same format matters:** The note on the complaint page confirms the printed forms can be reproduced by typing or handwriting — but details should be given as completely and accurately as possible; vague complaints are slower to act on.

<h2 id="documents">Documents and Evidence to Attach</h2>

The complaint page marks these as <strong>mandatory</strong> (attach with your application):

- **Copy of the complainant's identity document** — citizenship certificate, national ID, passport, or driving licence (for minors, a birth registration certificate)
- **Hyperlinks (URLs) and necessary screenshots** — clear enough to be understood

Evidence that is strongly recommended by the nature of the case (not officially mandatory):

- **For financial or payment fraud** — transaction receipts, bank or wallet statements, screenshots of the payment confirmation, the account or phone number you sent money to, and any links used
- **For hacked or fake accounts** — the profile URL, screenshots of the account or fake page, and screenshots showing your own ownership (profile photos, old posts, registered email)
- **For posted content or blackmail** — screenshots and links to every post or message, including dates and usernames
- **For organization cases (ETA 2063 branch)** — official letterhead application, company registration details, ownership or intellectual-property documents, and other relevant records

Good evidence practice:

- Take full screenshots that show usernames, dates or timestamps, and URLs, not cropped fragments
- Keep the original files; share only copies
- Record exact amounts, dates, times, account names, and mobile numbers in the application
- Store a backup copy of everything before handing over devices or changing accounts

> **Do not destroy or alter evidence:** Do not delete chats, block then delete the account, or edit screenshots. Altered material undermines a real complaint.

Minors are covered by the official document list itself — the birth registration certificate replaces the citizenship-style ID copy. If you were a victim of fraud through banking channels, also see <a href="/blog/dollar-card-nepal-500-limit-banks-documents-guide">how dollar and foreign-currency cards work in Nepal</a> for the records banks keep around online card payments.

<h2 id="by-incident">Reporting by Incident Type</h2>

<h3 id="finance-fraud">Online Financial Fraud and Payment Scams</h3>

Someone took money through an online trick — fake investment groups, phishing links, paid "jobs," romance-and-investment scams, or account-takeover payments. Use the <strong>Online Financial Fraud form</strong>, attach payment proof together with the mandatory ID copy, URLs, and screenshots.

- <strong>Contact your bank or wallet provider immediately</strong> through their official app, helpline, or branch — not through numbers or links sent by the person who took your money. Banks can sometimes stop or trace transfers when told quickly, but recovery is never guaranteed.
- <strong>Extension 108</strong> (01-5319044, office hours) is the official follow-up line for online-fraud complaints.
- Report the same to the Bureau even if you feel embarrassed — official arrest notices show the Bureau acts on these cases routinely, including scams targeting people through KYC-update calls and fake e-service appeals.

<h3 id="hacked-fake">Hacked, Fake, or Impersonating Accounts</h3>

Your account was taken over, or someone created a fake profile posing as you or someone you know. The Bureau lists one form covering <strong>Fake/Hacked IDs for recovery or closure</strong>, and extension <strong>112</strong> is the follow-up line.

Also use the platform side — it often acts faster than any complaint:

- Facebook: the Bureau's FAQ links Facebook's official <a href="https://www.facebook.com/help/contact/295309487309948">report an imposter account</a> page, with two practical notes: reports filed <strong>through the phone the victim usually logs in from</strong> tend to close faster, and in Facebook's form you can proceed by answering <strong>"No" to "Do you have a Facebook account?"</strong> (for example, when the hacker changed access details).
- Change the password and activate two-factor authentication as soon as you regain access, and warn contacts if the account has been misused.

<h3 id="obscene">Intimate Images, Obscene Content, and Blackmail</h3>

If intimate or obscene photos, videos, or audio of you — or a person you know — have been posted on social media, the Bureau provides a <strong>dedicated complaint form</strong> for exactly this situation.

- Preserve everything: post links, screenshots, attacker profiles, and any chat demanding money from you
- <strong>Do not pay the blackmailer, and do not keep negotiating.</strong> Payment has no guarantee of stopping the threats and often invites more demands
- If material has <strong>not</strong> yet been posted but you are being threatened with it, describe the facts in the application with your evidence; the <strong>"Other" form exists for any incident outside the named categories</strong>
- This is something done to you, not something you are at fault for. It is a police matter, and the complaint can be filed without discussing personal details with anyone except the authorities

<h3 id="harassment">Harassment, Threats, and Cyberbullying</h3>

Repeated abusive messages, threats of violence, or targeted humiliation online do not have a dedicated publicly listed form at the time of writing — file under the <strong>general ("Other") complaint form</strong> with chat evidence, usernames, profile URLs, and dates.

- Save the abuse before blocking anyone, and export chat backups where the platform allows it
- Report the account and messages to the platform's own tools, which can suspend offenders quickly
- If there is any <strong>credible threat to your physical safety</strong>, treat it as an emergency: go to the nearest police office or call the general police <strong>100</strong> line rather than waiting for a routine complaint flow

<h3 id="unauthorized">Unauthorized Access, Source-Code Theft, Organization Cases</h3>

Hacked company systems, stolen software code, or computer-related fraud beyond personal accounts fall under the <strong>Electronic Transactions Act, 2063, Section 9</strong> — the offences branch the Complaint page links directly (the printed list covers source-code theft, unauthorized access, publication of illegal materials, and computer-related fraud).

For these, the page's guidance differs from personal complaints:

- **Letterhead application** — for organization cases the page recommends drafting the application on official company letterhead, stamped to validate it
- **Ownership and registration records** — registration details, intellectual-property or ownership documents, and other relevant records are required with these applications

Use the general complaint form as the base for drafting, or follow the ETA branch instructions as printed on the complaint page.

<h3 id="shopping">Online Shopping Scams</h3>

You paid for goods that never arrived, or the "seller" disappeared after asking for advance payment. This is normally an electronic-medium (online) financial fraud: use the <strong>Online Financial Fraud form</strong> with payment receipts, the listing URL, seller profile, and chat history.

- Comparable platform reports: report the seller account on the marketplace or social network so it can be suspended for other buyers
- Never "verify" a purchase by sharing your OTP, card photos, or identification documents with a stranger online — legitimate sellers do not need these

<h2 id="online">Can You File a Cybercrime Complaint Online?</h2>

Partial. Be precise about what "online" means here, because three different ideas get mixed in public conversation:

- <strong>Downloading the complaint form online — yes, officially.</strong> All forms and the full instructions live on the official report page.
- <strong>Submitting a complaint by official email — yes, under a condition.</strong> The page explicitly authorizes emailing complete details to <a href="mailto:cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np">cyberbureau@nepalpolice.gov.np</a> <strong>when you cannot attend in person</strong>. Include the completed form (or details in that format), your ID copy, URLs, and screenshots in one clear message.
- <strong>A fully online filing or case-tracking portal — not confirmed.</strong> No official online submission system is published on the Bureau's complaint or follow-up pages as of October 17, 2026. A downloadable form is not an online portal, and reporting an account to Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok is a platform process, not a police complaint — you can and often should do both.
- <strong>Contacting the Bureau online — yes.</strong> The published Facebook page (facebook.com/cyberbureaunepal), the 24×7 duty number, and the official email address are legitimate contact channels.

<h2 id="followup">How to Follow Up on a Complaint</h2>

There is no public case-status portal, so follow-up means keeping good records and using the official lines:

- **Registration number** — if the office issued one, write it down permanently; it is the key your case is known by
- **Copy of everything** — application, attachments, and the submission channel and date; if you emailed, keep the sent message
- **Caller details** — expect to identify yourself and describe your case when calling; put the complainant's name on the application consistently
- **Which extension** — match your type: <strong>108</strong> (online fraud), <strong>109</strong> (is it registered?), <strong>112</strong> (hacked/fake), <strong>114</strong> (emailed but no registration number came), <strong>100</strong> for general questions, or the 24×7 duty number <strong>+977 9851286770</strong>
- **No timeline promises** — processing speed varies with the case; neither the Bureau nor the complaint pages publish a guaranteed duration, so treat any promised "fast case" service as a red flag

> **Emailed and heard nothing?** Extension 114 exists specifically for complaints submitted by email whose registration number has not arrived.

<h2 id="immediate">What to Do Immediately After the Incident</h2>

The Bureau's own <a href="https://cyberbureau.nepalpolice.gov.np/cyber-security-tips/">cyber-safety page</a> and the complaint instructions point to practical first actions:

1. <strong>Move fast if money is involved.</strong> Contact the bank or wallet provider through official channels right away and ask what they can do; report to the Bureau in parallel
2. <strong>Secure the attacked accounts.</strong> Change passwords from a trusted, uncompromised device, log out other sessions where the platform offers it, and enable two-factor authentication (2FA/MFA) on everything important
3. <strong>Preserve, do not purge.</strong> Save chats, URLs, screenshots, transaction records, and account names before resetting or wiping your phone
4. <strong>Never share secret codes.</strong> Nobody legitimate asks for your OTP, password, PIN, or recovery code — never absolutely, not even someone claiming to be from the bank, police, or a mobile operator
5. <strong>Report the platform too.</strong> Impersonating accounts, fake posts, and abusive content violate every major platform's rules — their rapid suspension can limit continued damage while your complaint proceeds
6. <strong>Do not keep paying.</strong> Further payments or "fees to unlock a withdrawal" rarely change the outcome; stop and report instead
7. <strong>Share verified prevention advice</strong> — the safety page warns against clicking unexpected links, trusting foreign-gift and customs-fee scams, fake online "jobs" asking for investment, and money requests over chat from a friend's "hacked" account (call to confirm identity first)
8. <strong>Call 100 if you are in danger.</strong> Safety threats are immediate police telephone territory, not something to wait out while a written complaint is processed

Also talk to children and family members about online risks — the Bureau's tips explicitly recommend this at home.
