<h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>

When you take a loan in Nepal — whether for a home, a car, or personal use — you typically repay it in equal monthly installments. These installments are called <strong>EMI</strong>, or Equated Monthly Installments. Understanding how EMI works helps you make smarter financial decisions and compare loan offers effectively.

In this guide, we'll walk you through the EMI formula, show you how to calculate it step by step, and share practical tips to reduce your monthly payments.

<h2 id="what-is-emi">What is EMI?</h2>

EMI stands for <strong>Equated Monthly Installment</strong>. It is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender on a specified date each month. An EMI payment consists of two parts:

- <strong>Principal amount</strong> — the original loan amount
- <strong>Interest</strong> — the cost of borrowing the money

In the early years of your loan, a larger portion of each EMI goes toward paying interest. Over time, more of your payment goes toward reducing the principal balance.

> **Key takeaway:** EMI is not just interest — it's a combination of principal and interest spread evenly across your loan tenure.

<h2 id="emi-formula">The EMI Formula</h2>

The standard EMI formula used by banks and financial institutions in Nepal is:

```text
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ / ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)
```

Where:

- <strong>P</strong> = Principal loan amount
- <strong>r</strong> = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
- <strong>n</strong> = Total number of monthly installments (loan tenure in months)

This formula applies to <strong>reducing balance</strong> loans, which is the standard method used by most banks in Nepal.

<h2 id="factors">Factors That Affect Your EMI</h2>

Several factors determine how much you pay each month:

1. <strong>Loan amount (Principal)</strong> — Higher principal means higher EMI
2. <strong>Interest rate</strong> — Even a 0.5% difference can significantly change your total payment
3. <strong>Loan tenure</strong> — Longer tenure reduces EMI but increases total interest paid
4. <strong>Down payment</strong> — A larger down payment reduces the principal and thus the EMI

<h2 id="how-to-calculate">How to Calculate EMI in Nepal</h2>

Let's walk through a practical calculation step by step.

<h3 id="example">Practical Example</h3>

Suppose you want to take a home loan in Nepal with the following terms:

- **Loan Amount** — NPR 50,00,000
- **Annual Interest Rate** — 11%
- **Loan Tenure** — 20 years (240 months)

<strong>Step 1:</strong> Convert annual rate to monthly rate

```text
r = 11% / 12 = 0.9167% = 0.009167
```

<strong>Step 2:</strong> Apply the EMI formula

```text
EMI = 50,00,000 × 0.009167 × (1.009167)²⁴⁰ / ((1.009167)²⁴⁰ − 1)
```

<strong>Step 3:</strong> Calculate (r = 0.009167, n = 240)

```text
EMI ≈ NPR 51,610 per month
```

Cross-check: ≈ Rs 1,032 per lakh per month (51,610 ÷ 50), consistent with standard EMI tables for 11% over 20 years.

Over 20 years, you'll pay a total of approximately <strong>NPR 1,23,86,400</strong> (51,610 × 240), meaning you'll pay about <strong>NPR 73,86,400</strong> in interest alone.

<h2 id="tips">Tips to Reduce Your EMI</h2>

Here are practical strategies to lower your monthly EMI burden:

- <strong>Increase your down payment</strong> — Paying 20-30% upfront reduces the principal significantly
- <strong>Choose a longer tenure</strong> — While total interest increases, monthly EMI becomes more manageable
- <strong>Negotiate the interest rate</strong> — Compare offers from multiple banks before deciding
- <strong>Make prepayments</strong> — Even small extra payments toward principal can reduce tenure and total interest
- <strong>Opt for a balance transfer</strong> — If another bank offers a lower rate, transferring your loan can save lakhs
