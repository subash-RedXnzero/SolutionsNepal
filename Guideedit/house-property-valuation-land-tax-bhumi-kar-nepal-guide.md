<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: Sarkari Mulyankan and Bhumi Kar</h2>

<strong>Sarkari Mulyankan</strong> is the official minimum valuation used for land registration, capital-gains calculations, and other legal purposes. <strong>Bhumi Kar</strong> is an annual land or property tax set and collected by your municipality or rural municipality. Neither is automatically the same as the market price.

- **Sarkari Mulyankan / Malpot valuation** — Official minimum value used for registration, CGT, and legal reference.
- **Bhumi Kar / municipal property tax** — Annual local tax based on the municipality's Economic Act or tax schedule.
- **Market value** — Negotiated open-market price influenced by demand, location, access, and property features.
- **Purchase price** — The amount agreed between buyer and seller; for registration it generally cannot be below the official minimum.

> **Do not use one number for every purpose:** Registration fees, CGT, annual municipal tax, and market negotiations can each use different bases and authorities.

<h2 id="sarkari">What Is Sarkari Mulyankan?</h2>

<strong>Sarkari Mulyankan (सरकारी मूल्यांकन)</strong> is the official minimum valuation assigned to land or property for tax, registration, and legal purposes. It helps prevent artificial under-declaration and gives offices a consistent floor for government calculations.

- It is published through district-level valuation schedules and relevant Land Revenue Offices.
- Schedules are location-specific and commonly updated for a new fiscal year.
- Rates can differ by ward, road access, land classification, and municipality.
- It may be expressed per aana, ropani, kattha, dhur, square metre, or another official unit.

<h2 id="malpot">What Is Malpot Land Valuation?</h2>

The Malpot or Land Revenue Office maintains ownership records, registers transfers, collects land-revenue charges, and publishes or applies minimum valuation schedules for its jurisdiction.

- Land transfers are recorded through deeds such as Rajinama, gift, partition, and inheritance documents.
- The office maintains Lalpurja and other ownership records.
- Registration fees are generally based on the higher of the declared value and minimum valuation.
- Seller-side CGT may be collected at registration even though tax policy is administered under IRD rules.

<h2 id="value-types">Government Valuation vs Market Value vs Purchase Price</h2>

- **Government valuation** — Official minimum floor for defined tax and registration purposes; not a promise of market value.
- **Market value** — What comparable properties may command in the open market.
- **Transaction/purchase price** — The amount negotiated by buyer and seller; it must comply with the minimum-valuation rules for registration.

For example, a plot may have an official minimum of Rs 40 lakh per aana, a market estimate of Rs 1.2 crore per aana, and a negotiated price somewhere between or above those figures. The applicable number depends on the transaction or tax question being answered.

<h2 id="factors">How Official Valuation Is Determined</h2>

- District, province, municipality, and ward
- Main-road, secondary-road, narrow-lane, or no-road access
- Road width and commercial-corridor classification
- Residential, commercial, agricultural, industrial, or mixed-use classification
- Urban or rural setting and proximity to markets, schools, hospitals, and transport
- Corner-plot, frontage, topography, and development characteristics
- Current fiscal-year policy and infrastructure changes

Two nearby plots can have different government valuations when they fall in different wards, road categories, zones, or land classifications. Always match the schedule to the exact parcel.

<h2 id="units">Land Measurement Units Used in Valuation</h2>

- **Hill/Kathmandu Valley** — 1 ropani = 16 aana = 256 paisa = 4,096 daam; 1 aana is approximately 342.25 square feet.
- **Terai** — 1 bigha = 20 kattha = 400 dhur; 1 kattha is approximately 3,645 square feet.
- **Metric** — Square metre and hectare are increasingly used in official documents.

Valuation schedules specify the unit to which a rate applies. Do not confuse a land-area conversion with a valuation rate.

<h2 id="bhumi-kar">What Is Bhumi Kar?</h2>

<strong>Bhumi Kar (भूमि कर)</strong> is local land or property tax collected by municipalities, rural municipalities, and metropolitan cities. It is distinct from Malpot land-revenue charges and from the registration fee paid during a transfer.

- Local governments set the rate through their Economic Act or annual tax schedule.
- A municipality may tax land, buildings, or both together as property tax.
- Rates can vary by ward, land area, building area, classification, valuation basis, and local exemptions.
- Payment deadlines and late penalties are also local-government matters.

<h2 id="local-tax">How Local Economic Acts Set Property Tax</h2>

There is no single nationwide Bhumi Kar formula. A local tax schedule may use one of several structures:

- Land-area based: a rate per aana, kattha, square metre, or another unit.
- Valuation based: a percentage or bracket applied to a local valuation base.
- Building-area based: a rate per square foot or square metre.
- Combined property tax: land and building assessed together.
- Hybrid: land area, building area, classification, and valuation used together.

The current FY 2083/84 Economic Act or tax schedule for your municipality is the authoritative source. An old tax receipt or a rate from another municipality is only a reference, not a current answer.

<h2 id="comparison">Malpot Valuation vs Municipal Property Tax</h2>

- **Sarkari/Malpot valuation** — DOLMA and Land Revenue Office framework; used for registration, CGT, land-revenue, and legal reference.
- **Bhumi Kar** — Municipality or rural municipality; annual local land/property tax under its Economic Act.
- **House/building tax** — Local tax on the structure where the municipality applies it; may be separate or combined with land tax.
- **Market value** — Private market estimate and negotiation; not a local-government tax schedule.

> **Separate authorities:** Malpot handles transfers and land records. Survey Office handles cadastral maps and kitta boundaries. Municipalities handle local property taxes and building permits. IRD administers income-tax policy.

<h2 id="find-rate">How to Find the Current Official Rate</h2>

1. Identify the property district and the Malpot office with jurisdiction.
2. Identify municipality or rural municipality, ward, road access, and land classification.
3. Request the current FY 2083/84 Malpot minimum-valuation schedule from the Land Revenue Office or official local source.
4. Ask the municipality revenue section for the current Economic Act or Bhumi Kar tax schedule.
5. Confirm which unit and tax base apply: area, building area, valuation, or a combination.
6. Check exemptions, concessions, payment deadlines, and late penalties.
7. Keep the written schedule or official receipt that supports the calculation.

DOLMA can help identify relevant offices, but minimum valuation schedules are often published or administered locally. Some municipalities offer online services; many still require an in-person revenue-office visit.

<h2 id="calculate">How to Estimate Bhumi Kar and House Tax</h2>

```text
Estimated local property tax = Applicable local tax base × Current local rate
```

The formula is intentionally generic because the tax base differs by local government. Before calculating, collect municipality, ward, land area, property classification, building area, construction year, and the current local rate.

- **Area-based example** — If a local schedule says Rs 500 per aana and the property is 4 aana, the illustrative annual tax is Rs 2,000.
- **Building-area example** — If a local schedule says Rs 10 per square foot and a house is 2,000 square feet, the illustrative base is Rs 20,000 before local adjustments.
- **Valuation-based example** — If a local schedule uses a percentage of valuation, apply the exact local percentage and definition of valuation.

> **Illustrative only:** The examples teach the calculation concept and are not current nationwide rates. Use your municipality's Economic Act for the actual amount.

<h2 id="documents">Documents and Property Information Needed</h2>

- Lalpurja or land-ownership certificate
- Previous municipal property-tax receipt
- Citizenship or National ID of the owner
- District, municipality, ward, kitta number, and land area
- Road access and land classification
- Building permit, completion certificate, building area, floors, materials, and construction year where relevant
- Authorization letter or power of attorney when someone else pays on the owner's behalf

The exact document list varies by municipality. Carry originals and copies and ask the local revenue section before visiting.

<h2 id="payment">Property Tax Payment Process</h2>

1. Identify the municipality, ward, and revenue section.
2. Bring the property record and previous tax receipt.
3. Ask for the current fiscal-year assessment and any applicable exemption.
4. Pay at the revenue office, ward, authorized bank, or online portal where the municipality supports it.
5. Collect a receipt showing owner, property, fiscal year, amount, date, and receipt number.
6. Keep the receipt for future transactions, tax clearance, and dispute resolution.

Not every municipality supports online tax checking or payment. Do not assume a Kathmandu or metropolitan-city process is available in every rural municipality.

<h2 id="owners-abroad">Owners Abroad and Authorized Representatives</h2>

- A property owner abroad can authorize a family member or representative to pay local tax where the municipality permits it.
- The representative may need an authorization letter, owner identification, and property documents.
- A notarized power of attorney may be necessary for formal transactions rather than routine tax payment.
- Keep digital copies of Lalpurja, receipts, tax schedules, and local-office correspondence.
- Confirm whether the municipality offers online payment before relying on remote payment.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Valuation and Tax Mistakes</h2>

- Using an old FY 2082/83 valuation or tax schedule for FY 2083/84
- Treating market price as government valuation
- Assuming one Bhumi Kar rate applies across Nepal
- Using another ward's road or land-classification rate
- Confusing Malpot valuation with annual municipal property tax
- Assuming house tax is always included in Bhumi Kar
- Ignoring local exemptions or senior-citizen concessions
- Relying on real-estate listings or social-media figures as official rates
- Failing to keep tax receipts and property records

<h2 id="worksheet">Property Tax Estimation Worksheet</h2>

- **Location** — District, municipality/rural municipality, ward, kitta number
- **Land** — Area, unit, road access, classification, current valuation rate
- **Building** — Area, type, floors, construction year, materials, depreciation if local rules use it
- **Tax schedule** — Current FY 2083/84 Economic Act or local tax schedule
- **Calculation** — Applicable tax base × local rate, plus or minus local adjustments
- **Payment** — Deadline, penalty, exemption, payment method, receipt number

Use this as a planning worksheet only. The municipality's official assessment and receipt determine the final payable amount.

<h2 id="checklist">Final Verification Checklist</h2>

- Use the current FY 2083/84 Malpot valuation schedule.
- Use the current FY 2083/84 municipal Economic Act or tax schedule.
- Confirm district, municipality, ward, kitta, road category, and land classification.
- Separate registration fee, CGT, Malpot land revenue, Bhumi Kar, and house tax.
- Check exemptions or concessions.
- Confirm whether online payment is actually available in your municipality.
- Keep Lalpurja, prior receipts, identity documents, and building records.
- Get a written calculation or receipt from the relevant office before relying on the amount.

<h2 id="sources">Official Sources and Final Note</h2>

Use the <a href="https://dolma.gov.np">Department of Land Management and Archives</a>, the relevant Land Revenue Office, your municipality or rural municipality, Ministry of Land Management, and current local Economic Acts for official valuation and tax information.

Property valuation, Bhumi Kar, house tax, registration charges, exemptions, and payment processes vary by local government and fiscal year. This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Confirm the current rate with the relevant Malpot office and municipal revenue section before paying or transacting.
