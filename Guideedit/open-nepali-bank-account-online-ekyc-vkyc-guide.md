<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: Can You Open a Bank Account Online?</h2>

Yes, many Nepali commercial banks support online account opening through <strong>eKYC</strong> and, in some cases, <strong>Video KYC (V-KYC)</strong>. You may fill the application, upload documents, verify your mobile, and complete identity checks without visiting a branch.

However, online application does not always mean fully branchless banking. A bank may still require a branch visit for final KYC, specialized or high-limit accounts, failed digital verification, or debit-card collection.

> **The practical rule:** Check the selected bank's current terms for the exact account type, residency status, KYC method, transaction limits, and branch requirements before applying.

<h2 id="ekyc">What Is eKYC?</h2>

<strong>eKYC</strong> means electronic Know Your Customer. It is the digital version of the identity and customer-due-diligence process banks use before opening an account.

- Citizenship or National ID details may be checked against government records.
- An OTP verifies the mobile number.
- A photograph or live selfie can confirm identity and liveness.
- Document scans or photographs are uploaded through the bank app or website.
- Digital declarations and consent replace some paper forms.

Banks use eKYC to reduce paperwork and speed up onboarding, but each bank uses different technology, limits, and verification rules.

<h2 id="vkyc">What Is Video KYC?</h2>

<strong>Video KYC</strong> is a live video session with a bank officer. It adds human verification to the electronic document and OTP process and can allow eligible customers to complete onboarding remotely.

- Show your original Citizenship, NID, or passport to the camera.
- Answer identity questions such as name, date of birth, address, and document number.
- Confirm account purpose, occupation, and source of funds.
- Use a clear camera, good lighting, stable internet, working microphone, and quiet room.

V-KYC availability is bank- and account-specific. Some banks support applicants abroad; others restrict live verification to applicants physically in Nepal.

<h2 id="eligibility">Who Can Apply Online?</h2>

- **Nepali resident** — Usually applies for a standard savings or salary account with Citizenship or NID and a Nepali mobile number.
- **Foreign employment worker** — May use a remittance-account route with work permit, visa, passport, and remittance evidence.
- **Student abroad** — Remote onboarding is bank-specific; passport, foreign address, visa, and proof of study or residence may be requested.
- **NRN** — Uses a separate NRN account route with NRN ID, passport, foreign residency, and Nepali-origin documents.

Do not apply for an ordinary resident account when your bank requires a remittance or NRN product. The correct route affects eligibility, documents, currency options, transaction limits, and repatriation rules.

<h2 id="account-types">Types of Accounts You May Open Online</h2>

- **Ordinary savings** — For day-to-day deposits, payments, cards, and digital banking; commonly offered through eKYC or V-KYC.
- **Salary account** — Employer-linked account that may offer reduced balance requirements or bundled services.
- **Remittance savings** — For foreign employment workers and remittance recipients; eligibility and benefits vary by bank.
- **Current account** — Business-oriented account with more documentation; may not be fully digital.
- **Fixed deposit** — Often opened digitally after an existing savings relationship is active.
- **NRN account** — Special resident-abroad account with separate status, currency, transaction, and repatriation rules.
- **Foreign-currency account** — Available only where NRB rules and bank policy permit it for your status.

<h2 id="remittance">Remittance Account Online in Nepal</h2>

A remittance account is designed for Nepali citizens working abroad and, in some products, their family members receiving formal remittances. It may offer special interest, fee, or investment benefits, but these are bank-specific.

- A valid foreign-employment work permit or Shram Swikriti may be requested.
- The bank may ask for visa, passport, employment, or overseas-residence information.
- The bank may require evidence of formal remittance inflows for a benefit or product condition.
- Some banks support digital onboarding with V-KYC; others require a branch visit.
- Use licensed banks and remittance companies for foreign transfers.

A remittance account is not automatically the same as an ordinary savings account. Compare eligibility, interest, IPO or investment benefits, fees, and how family members can access funds.

<h2 id="documents">Documents Required</h2>

- **Nepali resident** — Citizenship or NID, recent photograph, selfie, Nepali mobile, email, permanent/current address, occupation, source of income, nominee details, and PAN where required.
- **Applicant abroad** — Citizenship and passport, foreign address proof, visa or residence permit, photograph/selfie, contact details, occupation, and source of funds.
- **Foreign employment worker** — Passport, work permit or Shram Swikriti, visa, employment information, and remittance evidence where requested.
- **NRN** — NRN ID card, passport, foreign-residency proof, evidence of Nepali origin, photograph, address, nominee, and source-of-funds information.

Some banks may request National ID or allow a temporary self-declaration where current regulatory guidance permits it. Follow the exact checklist shown by the chosen bank.

<h2 id="process">How to Open a Bank Account Online</h2>

1. <strong>Choose the bank and account.</strong> Compare eKYC/V-KYC, account type, minimum balance, card fees, mobile banking, branch network, and remittance support.
2. <strong>Use the official bank website or app.</strong> Confirm the domain or download the app from Google Play or the Apple App Store.
3. <strong>Select digital account opening.</strong> Look for Open Account Online, Digital Onboarding, eKYC, Online Savings, or Video KYC.
4. <strong>Enter personal details.</strong> Match name, date of birth, Citizenship or passport number, address, occupation, income source, and nominee details exactly.
5. <strong>Verify mobile and email.</strong> Enter OTPs only in the official bank flow and use your own active number.
6. <strong>Upload documents.</strong> Add clear, full-page files in the formats and sizes shown by the bank.
7. <strong>Complete eKYC.</strong> Confirm identity, take the requested selfie, and sign digital declarations.
8. <strong>Complete V-KYC if requested.</strong> Join the officer call with original documents and a stable connection.
9. <strong>Review and submit.</strong> Check account type, nominee, contact information, and source-of-funds details before submission.
10. <strong>Save the reference number.</strong> Keep the confirmation email, SMS, or application ID until the account is fully active.

<h2 id="timeline">How Long Does Online Account Opening Take?</h2>

- **Application submitted** — Usually immediate acknowledgement
- **Digital KYC approved** — From a few hours to several working days
- **Manual review** — Longer when details, documents, residency, or source of funds need checking
- **Activation** — After KYC approval, initial deposit, and any account-specific activation steps
- **Debit card** — Mailed or collected at a branch depending on bank policy

A bank may create an account number quickly but keep transactions or debit access restricted until KYC is complete. Account creation and full operational activation are not always the same event.

<h2 id="branch">Do You Need to Visit the Bank Branch?</h2>

- **Branch may not be required** — Bank explicitly supports fully digital eKYC plus V-KYC for your account and residency status.
- **Branch may be required** — KYC mismatch, failed selfie, incomplete records, high-limit or specialized account, or bank-specific policy.
- **Card collection** — Some banks mail a debit card; others require branch pickup even when onboarding was digital.
- **Debit restriction** — An online account may have limited transactions until physical KYC is completed.

Read the bank's current terms before assuming a branchless experience. The answer depends on bank, account type, verification result, and applicant status.

<h2 id="abroad">Opening a Nepali Bank Account From Abroad</h2>

Some banks support remote onboarding for Nepalis working or studying overseas, especially for remittance and NRN products. Others require a branch visit or an approved representative for specific steps.

- You may need a Nepali mobile number for OTP, although some banks accept foreign numbers under their own rules.
- Keep passport, Citizenship, visa or residence permit, overseas address proof, and employment or study details ready.
- V-KYC may be available from abroad only for selected banks and products.
- A representative may assist with document or card collection if the bank allows it, but the account holder usually must personally complete identity verification.
- Confirm where the debit card will be delivered and what activates mobile or internet banking.

<h2 id="nrn">NRN Bank Account Online in Nepal</h2>

An NRN account is a specialized account for eligible Non-Resident Nepalis. It is not simply an ordinary savings account opened while living abroad.

- Eligibility can involve Nepali origin, foreign residence, and a valid NRN Identity Card.
- Banks may request passport, foreign citizenship or residence permit, overseas address proof, and parents' or grandparents' Nepali Citizenship evidence.
- Products may include NPR savings and, where permitted, foreign-currency accounts.
- Transaction, investment, conversion, and repatriation rules differ from resident accounts.
- Some banks support online application and V-KYC; others require in-person or representative-assisted onboarding.

> **Check NRB rules:** Currency holding, remittance, repatriation, and account eligibility are regulated. Confirm current NRB and bank rules rather than relying on a generic NRN checklist.

<h2 id="activation">Activating the Account After Approval</h2>

1. Receive the account number by email, SMS, or the bank app.
2. Complete any remaining KYC or initial deposit requirement.
3. Set up mobile banking and internet banking through the official bank flow.
4. Request or collect the debit card according to the bank's delivery policy.
5. Confirm transaction limits and whether the account is fully activated or still debit-restricted.
6. Save the bank's official support details and account-opening reference.

<h2 id="fees">Minimum Balance, Initial Deposit, and Fees</h2>

- **Account opening** — Often free, but bank-specific processing charges may apply.
- **Minimum balance** — Some accounts are zero-balance; ordinary savings can require a bank-specific minimum.
- **Initial deposit** — May range from zero to a bank-specific amount before activation.
- **Debit card** — Commonly an annual charge; verify the current card and replacement fee.
- **Mobile or internet banking** — May be free or carry annual/service charges depending on bank and product.
- **SMS alerts** — Often a recurring charge or optional service; check the current schedule.
- **Transfers and other services** — Fees vary by channel, transaction, card type, and account package.

These are categories, not universal rates. Read the selected bank's current fee schedule and account terms before submitting.

<h2 id="failure">What to Do if eKYC or V-KYC Fails</h2>

- **OTP not received** — Check signal, confirm the number, avoid repeated rapid requests, and retry through the official app.
- **Citizenship or NID mismatch** — Match spelling, number, district, and date of birth exactly; correct the source record if it is wrong.
- **Selfie or liveness failure** — Use good lighting, clean the camera, show your full face, remove glare, and follow the on-screen movement instructions.
- **Document upload failure** — Use a clear full-page scan, correct file format, readable text, and the allowed size.
- **Video KYC failure** — Test camera and microphone, use stable internet, prepare original documents, and join from a quiet well-lit place.
- **Application pending** — Allow the bank's stated review period, then contact official support with the reference number.
- **Application rejected** — Read the reason, correct the identified problem, and resubmit without creating conflicting applications.
- **Branch visit requested** — This can be a normal final-KYC step rather than proof that the online application failed.

<h2 id="security">Online Banking Security Tips</h2>

- Use only the official bank website or app from the bank's website, Google Play, or the Apple App Store.
- Never share OTPs, card PINs, mobile-banking passwords, or internet-banking credentials.
- A legitimate bank will not ask you to send an OTP or password through social media or an unsolicited call.
- Do not upload Citizenship or passport files to unknown websites or unofficial agents.
- Do not install unofficial APK files or give strangers remote access to your phone.
- Check the domain before entering documents or banking information.
- Contact the bank using the phone number or email listed on its official website.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Choosing an ordinary savings account when you need a remittance or NRN product
- Entering a name, address, Citizenship number, or passport number differently from the source document
- Using another person's mobile number for OTP
- Uploading blurry, cropped, or outdated documents
- Missing a scheduled V-KYC call
- Ignoring minimum-balance, card, SMS, and mobile-banking fees
- Assuming account-number generation means full activation
- Assuming every bank supports branchless onboarding
- Treating an NRN account as an ordinary resident account
- Using an unofficial agent or website that requests passwords or OTPs

<h2 id="comparison">eKYC vs V-KYC vs Traditional KYC</h2>

- **Traditional KYC** — Physical branch visit where staff inspect original documents. Usually not remote.
- **eKYC** — Electronic document, identity, OTP, photo/selfie, and consent checks. Often remote.
- **V-KYC** — Live video call with a bank officer to inspect the person and original documents remotely.

<h2 id="choose">How to Choose the Right Account</h2>

- <strong>Resident:</strong> prioritize eKYC/V-KYC, low balance requirements, nearby branches and ATMs, and reliable mobile banking.
- <strong>Foreign worker:</strong> compare remittance products, formal-inflow benefits, V-KYC availability, and international transfer fees.
- <strong>Student abroad:</strong> check remote onboarding, foreign-address documentation, low-balance requirements, and card delivery.
- <strong>NRN:</strong> compare dedicated NRN products, currency options, repatriation rules, and the bank's NRN document checklist.
- <strong>Fully digital customer:</strong> choose a bank that explicitly states V-KYC and no branch visit for your exact account and status.

<h2 id="checklist">Final Checklist</h2>

- Choose the correct bank and account type.
- Confirm eKYC and V-KYC availability for your residency status.
- Use the official bank website or app only.
- Prepare clear Citizenship, passport, NID, photo, and selfie files.
- Use your own active mobile number and email.
- Match all personal details to official documents.
- Complete OTP, eKYC, and V-KYC steps carefully.
- Check minimum balance, initial deposit, card, SMS, and mobile-banking fees.
- Confirm whether a branch visit or card collection is required.
- Save the application reference and account-opening confirmation.
- Activate mobile and internet banking after approval.
- Never share OTP, PIN, or banking passwords.

<h2 id="sources">Official Sources and Final Note</h2>

Confirm current digital-onboarding and KYC rules with <a href="https://www.nrb.org.np">Nepal Rastra Bank</a> and the official website of your chosen bank. Banks such as commercial, development, and state-owned institutions publish their own account, remittance, NRN, and fee terms.

Online bank-account opening in Nepal is increasingly practical, but the final experience depends on bank, account type, residency, KYC result, and regulatory requirements. Verify the latest terms before uploading identity documents or sending money.
