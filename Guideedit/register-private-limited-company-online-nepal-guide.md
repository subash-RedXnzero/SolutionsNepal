<h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>

> **Important information:** Government procedures, fees, tax rules, portal features, and local registration requirements can change. Verify the latest instructions with the <a href="https://www.ocr.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Office of the Company Registrar</a>, <a href="https://camis.ocr.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CAMIS</a>, the <a href="https://ird.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Inland Revenue Department</a>, and the relevant municipality or rural municipality before submitting an application. This SolutionsNepal article is educational information, not legal, tax, accounting, or business-specific advice.

If you want to establish a startup, trading business, software company, consultancy, manufacturing business, or other formal venture in Nepal, a <strong>Private Limited company</strong> is one of the common structures to consider. The incorporation process is handled by the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), with online services available through its CAMIS system.

The general journey is:

<strong>Choose Structure → Reserve Company Name → Prepare MOA/AOA → Submit Application → Pay Fees → OCR Review → Receive Registration Certificate → PAN/VAT → Local Ward Registration → Sector-Specific Licenses</strong>

Company incorporation is not the same as permission to operate every type of business. After receiving the company certificate, you may still need tax registration, local business registration, and industry-specific licenses.

<h2 id="what-is-private-limited">What Is a Private Limited Company in Nepal?</h2>

A Private Limited company is a legally registered organization with its own identity separate from its shareholders. It can own property, enter contracts, maintain bank accounts, employ people, and conduct business in its registered name.

A private company generally has:

- <strong>Shareholders</strong> who own shares in the company.
- <strong>Directors</strong> who manage or oversee the company.
- A <strong>registered office</strong> in Nepal.
- A <strong>Memorandum of Association (MOA)</strong>.
- <strong>Articles of Association (AOA)</strong>.
- <strong>Share capital</strong> and ownership records.
- Continuing <strong>reporting and compliance</strong> responsibilities.

A shareholder’s financial exposure is generally connected to the shares or commitments they hold, subject to the Companies Act, company documents, and other applicable laws. This is different from treating the business and the owner as the same person.

<h3 id="structure-comparison">Business Structure Comparison</h3>

- **Sole proprietorship** — Owner-based legal identity. Individual ownership. Typical use: small individual business.
- **Partnership** — Partnership structure. Partners as owners. Typical use: multiple-owner business.
- **Private Limited** — Separate company identity. Shareholders as owners. Typical use: startups and growing businesses.
- **Public Limited** — Separate company identity. Shareholders as owners. Typical use: larger or public companies.

OCR’s current information states that a private company may have one shareholder and may have up to 101 shareholders. Always confirm the applicable Companies Act and OCR requirements for your proposed structure before filing.

This guide focuses on private limited company registration in Nepal, not sole proprietorship or partnership-firm registration.

<h2 id="who-can-register">Who Can Register a Private Limited Company?</h2>

The founders of a private company may include:

- Individual Nepali citizens.
- Multiple Nepali shareholders.
- A Nepali company or other legal entity where permitted.
- Foreign individuals or companies, subject to foreign-investment rules and sector restrictions.
- A combination of Nepali and foreign investors where the required approvals are obtained.

The exact documents depend on the founder’s status. For example, a Nepali individual normally uses citizenship documents, while a foreign individual generally needs a passport and evidence of the required investment approval.

> **Foreign investment:** It may involve additional approval from the relevant authority, such as the Department of Industry, and may be restricted in certain sectors. Do not assume that every business activity is open to foreign investment.

The proposed directors must also meet the applicable legal and filing requirements. The number, eligibility, and supporting documents can vary according to the company type and current OCR requirements.

Prepare clear, readable electronic copies before starting the OCR online application.

<h3 id="docs-company">Company documents</h3>

The application normally requires:

- Memorandum of Association.

- Company objectives.

- Directors’ and shareholders’ details.

<h3 id="docs-situational">Situational documents</h3>

Additional documents may be required if:

- A foreign person or company is investing.
- Another company is a shareholder.

- A representative is filing on behalf of a founder.
- A founder is a legal entity rather than an individual.

OCR’s published registration guidance lists different requirements for Nepali founders, foreign founders, legal-entity shareholders, and firms converted into companies. Use the current checklist shown by OCR rather than relying on a general online list.

<h2 id="ocr-camis">What Are CAMIS and OCR?</h2>

- <strong>OCR</strong> means the <strong>Office of the Company Registrar</strong>. It is the government authority responsible for company registration and company-administration services in Nepal.
- <strong>CAMIS</strong> means the <strong>Company Administration Management Information System</strong>. It is the online platform used for company-related services, including company registration and administrative filings.

In simple terms: OCR is the government office. CAMIS is the online system operated for OCR services.

The official OCR website links to CAMIS and identifies company registration and other services under its CAMIS system. The CAMIS portal address currently shown by OCR is <a href="https://camis.ocr.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">camis.ocr.gov.np</a>. Check the OCR website if the portal address or login route changes.

<h3 id="camis-functions">What CAMIS is used for</h3>

- Creating or accessing an applicant account.
- Proposing and reserving a company name.
- Entering company information.
- Uploading incorporation documents.
- Submitting an application.
- Paying applicable government charges.
- Responding to corrections or observations where supported.
- Tracking an application or company-related request.

The interface and field names can change. Follow the instructions currently displayed in the portal.

<h2 id="registration-process">How to Register a Private Limited Company Online</h2>

The following workflow explains the usual company registration Nepal online 2026 process. Treat it as a practical roadmap, not a substitute for the current CAMIS instructions.

<h3 id="step-structure">Step 1: Decide your company structure</h3>

Before opening CAMIS, decide:

- Whether Private Limited is appropriate.
- Who the shareholders will be.
- How many shares each shareholder will hold.
- Who will serve as directors.
- The proposed company objectives.
- The registered office address.
- The expected capital structure.
- Whether any founder is a foreign person or legal entity.
- Whether the business activity is regulated.

The share distribution should match the founders’ agreement. For example, if two founders agree to own 60% and 40%, the share details entered in the application and reflected in the company documents should be consistent.

> **Do not pick a number from an old blog post:** Capital, fee calculations, business requirements, and sector rules should be checked against the current law and OCR process.

<h3 id="step-name">Step 2: Choose a company name</h3>

Prepare a name that is distinctive and suitable for the proposed business. Check for:

- Similarity to an existing company name.
- Confusing or misleading wording.
- Restricted government or regulatory terms.
- Words that imply an approval or status the company does not have.
- Conflict with registered trademarks or well-known brands.
- A name that does not match the proposed business purpose.
- Correct Nepali and English spelling.

Company-name approval is controlled by OCR. A name that sounds available is not automatically approved. Prepare an alternative name in case the first proposal is rejected or considered too similar to an existing name.

You can use the official OCR company-search service to review registered-company information, but a search result is not a guarantee of approval.

<h3 id="step-account">Step 3: Create an account and reserve the name</h3>

The exact screens may change, but the general process is:

1. Open the official OCR/CAMIS portal.
2. Create an applicant or company-user account if you do not already have one.
3. Verify the contact details requested by the system.
4. Log in to the dashboard.
5. Select the company-registration or name-reservation service.
6. Enter the proposed company name and required company information.
7. Submit the name request.
8. Monitor the application status.
9. Correct or resubmit the name if OCR returns an observation.

Keep the username, password, application number, and messages from the portal safely. Do not use unofficial websites that ask for unnecessary identity information or payment.

A proposed name may require correction because of similarity, restricted wording, inaccurate information, or other OCR requirements. The actual reason should be taken from the portal or OCR communication.

<h2 id="moa-aoa">How to Draft the MOA and AOA</h2>

The MOA and AOA are core incorporation documents. They should not be copied blindly from an unrelated company.

<h3 id="moa">MOA: Memorandum of Association</h3>

The Memorandum of Association sets out the company’s fundamental information. Depending on the applicable format, it may cover:

- Company name.
- Registered office.
- Main objectives.
- Authorized or issued capital information.
- Share details.
- Founder or subscriber information.
- The nature and scope of the proposed business.

The objectives should be broad enough to cover the activities you genuinely plan to conduct, but they should not include unrelated or regulated activities without considering the relevant approvals.

<h3 id="aoa">AOA: Articles of Association</h3>

The Articles of Association contain the company’s internal governance and operating rules. They may address:

- Share transfers.
- Meetings and notice.
- Director powers and responsibilities.
- Voting arrangements.
- Share certificates and records.
- Appointment or removal of officers.
- Decision-making procedures.
- Internal administration.

<h3 id="moa-vs-aoa">MOA and AOA comparison</h3>

- **MOA** — Defines fundamental company information and objectives. Describes the company’s basic purpose and structure. Important for incorporation. Filed as part of incorporation.
- **AOA** — Defines internal governance and operating rules. Explains how the company is administered. Important for continuing administration. Filed as part of incorporation.

> **Get advice for complex structures:** If the company will have foreign investors, multiple share classes, special voting rights, investor agreements, complex transfers, or regulated activities, obtain professional legal advice before finalizing the documents.

<h2 id="submit-application">Submit the Registration Application</h2>

After the name is approved, complete the incorporation application in CAMIS. Information commonly entered includes:

- Approved company name.
- Company type.
- Registered office.
- Business objectives.
- Shareholders and their identity details.
- Directors and their details.
- Capital information.
- Share allocation.
- Contact information.
- MOA and AOA.
- Supporting documents.
- Required declarations or consents.

Review every field carefully before submitting. A mismatch between a citizenship certificate, passport, MOA, AOA, and online form can lead to an observation.

Pay particular attention to:

- Name spelling in English and Nepali.
- Citizenship number and issue district.
- Date of birth.
- Permanent and current address.
- Shareholder names and ownership percentages.
- Director details.
- Company objectives.
- Uploaded document quality.

The portal’s current fields and upload requirements take priority over the general list above.

<h2 id="ocr-review">OCR Review and Company Approval</h2>

After submission and payment:

1. OCR reviews the application.
2. It may accept the documents or return them with an observation.
3. You respond to corrections through the applicable process.
4. OCR reviews the corrected information.
5. The application proceeds if the requirements are satisfied.
6. The company registration certificate is issued after approval.

Processing time depends on factors such as:

- Name approval.
- Document completeness.
- Identity verification.
- Application volume.
- Corrections and resubmission.
- Foreign-investment or sector approvals.
- Portal or payment issues.

> **No guaranteed timeline:** Online submission does not necessarily mean instant approval. Do not rely on a guaranteed number of hours or days unless OCR has officially provided that service standard.

<h2 id="certificate">Receive the Company Registration Certificate</h2>

The certificate confirms that the company has been incorporated and registered with OCR. Check the certificate for:

- Legal company name.
- Company registration number.
- Registration date.
- Company type.
- Other identifying details.

Store the certificate, approved MOA, AOA, shareholder information, and OCR correspondence in secure digital and physical records. Banks, tax offices, investors, payment providers, landlords, and regulators may request these documents.

<h2 id="after-registration">What to Do After Registration</h2>

Incorporation is the beginning of compliance, not the end. A company certificate does not automatically provide every permission required to operate.

<h3 id="pan">1. Register for PAN</h3>

<strong>PAN</strong> means Permanent Account Number. It identifies a taxpayer for dealings with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD).

The IRD provides taxpayer-registration services and information for entities. The company may need to use the IRD Taxpayer Portal or the relevant linked government process, depending on the current system.

OCR currently highlights a system in which PAN may be automatically generated through the CAMIS process after company registration. Confirm whether the PAN has actually been created, whether verification is required, and how to obtain the certificate before beginning transactions.

Typical information may include:

- Company registration certificate.
- Company name and registration number.
- Registered address.
- Directors or authorized representative.
- Contact details.
- Business activity.
- Bank or tax-office information where required.

<h3 id="vat">2. Check VAT registration</h3>

VAT registration is not automatically required for every company merely because it is incorporated. It depends on applicable tax rules, the business activity, taxable transactions, turnover or other conditions.

- Whether your goods or services fall within VAT rules.
- Whether a registration threshold or compulsory category applies.
- Whether voluntary registration is suitable.
- Whether invoices and accounting systems must meet additional requirements.

The IRD’s registration system includes PAN, VAT, and excise-related services. Use current IRD guidance rather than relying on an old threshold or tax-rate article.

<h3 id="bank">3. Open a corporate bank account</h3>

A bank may request:

- Company registration certificate.
- PAN certificate or PAN details.
- MOA and AOA.
- Board decision or authorization.
- Director and authorized-signatory identity documents.
- Company stamp or other bank-specific documents.
- Beneficial-owner or KYC information.

Requirements differ between banks. Ask the selected bank for its current corporate-account checklist.

<h3 id="ward">4. Check local ward or municipality registration</h3>

A company registered by OCR may still need local registration or a business operating permission from the ward or municipality where it operates. Local requirements can depend on:

- Municipality.
- Business activity.
- Office or shop location.
- Signboard.
- Rental or ownership documents.
- Local tax or business-registration rules.

The terminology may be “business registration,” “business operating license,” “local registration,” or another term. Confirm directly with the relevant local government.

<h3 id="licenses">5. Obtain sector-specific licenses</h3>

Additional approvals may apply to businesses operating in areas such as:

- Education.
- Health.
- Food and hospitality.
- Travel and trekking.
- Construction.
- Finance and lending.
- Manufacturing.
- Import and export.
- Pharmaceuticals.
- Telecommunications.
- Technology services involving regulated activities.

A software company may have different requirements from a school, restaurant, travel agency, or finance company. Company registration alone does not replace sector licensing.

<h2 id="vs-ward">Company Registration vs Ward Registration</h2>

- **Company registration** — Central company incorporation. Handled through OCR. Creates the legal company. Produces a company registration certificate. Applies to the company as a legal entity.
- **Ward/municipality registration** — Local business registration or operating permission. Handled by the relevant local government. Relates to operating at a local place. Produces a local certificate, license, or record depending on the municipality. May apply to a particular business location or activity.

A Private Limited company can exist legally after OCR incorporation, but it may still need local registration before operating from a particular municipality. Always check the local authority’s current process.

<h2 id="how-long">How Long Does Company Registration Take?</h2>

The process may involve several stages:

- Name review.
- Name approval or correction.
- MOA/AOA preparation.
- Application review.
- Identity and document verification.
- Correction or resubmission.
- Fee confirmation.
- Certificate issuance.

A complete, straightforward application may move faster than an application requiring corrections or additional approvals. Foreign investment, regulated activities, unclear objectives, and document mismatches may add steps.

> **Not same-day:** Do not treat online filing as a promise of same-day incorporation. Check the current status inside CAMIS or contact OCR through its official channels if the application remains pending.

<h2 id="corrections">Common Reasons for Corrections</h2>

Possible issues include:

- Proposed name is unavailable or unsuitable.
- Shareholder information does not match identity documents.
- Director details are incomplete.
- Uploaded documents are unclear.
- Documents are missing signatures or required pages.
- MOA objectives are incomplete or inconsistent.
- AOA provisions do not match the proposed structure.
- Registered-office information is incomplete.
- Share distribution does not add up.
- A required approval is missing.
- A foreign investor’s documents are not properly certified or translated.

Use the exact observation provided by OCR. Do not change unrelated information without checking whether it affects the application.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Choosing a name without checking existing companies.
- Using a business objective that is too narrow.
- Adding regulated activities without checking approval requirements.
- Entering incorrect citizenship or passport details.
- Uploading blurry documents.
- Confusing the registered office with a future branch or operating location.
- Assuming OCR registration automatically creates every tax registration.
- Assuming company registration replaces ward registration.
- Ignoring VAT analysis.
- Starting a regulated business before obtaining the required license.
- Failing to keep MOA, AOA, certificates, and shareholder records.
- Missing annual filings, audit, tax, or renewal obligations.
