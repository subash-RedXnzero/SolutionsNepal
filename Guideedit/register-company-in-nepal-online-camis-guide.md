<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: Can You Register a Company Online?</h2>

Yes. A Private Limited company can generally be registered online through the Office of Company Registrar (OCR) <strong>CAMIS</strong> portal at <a href="https://camis.ocr.gov.np">camis.ocr.gov.np</a>. The digital workflow covers name reservation, incorporation application, document upload, payment, status tracking, correction queries, and electronic Certificate of Incorporation.

Online incorporation creates the legal company, but it does not automatically make the business fully operational. You may still need Business PAN, VAT if applicable, local municipality registration, industry or sector licenses, Social Security Fund registration for employees, and foreign-investment approval where relevant.

> **First compliance event, not the last:** OCR incorporation creates the entity. Tax, local, industry, sector, employment, and foreign-exchange compliance are separate steps.

<h2 id="ocr-camis">What Are OCR and CAMIS?</h2>

The <strong>Office of Company Registrar (OCR)</strong> is the government authority responsible for incorporating companies and maintaining the company register under Nepal's Companies Act, 2063. It also administers corporate records, amendments, and ongoing company filings.

<strong>CAMIS</strong> means Company Administration Management Information System. It is OCR's official online portal for:

- Company-name search and reservation
- Online incorporation applications
- MOA, AOA, identity, and supporting-document upload
- Government-fee payment and receipts
- Application status and OCR-query responses
- Digital Certificate of Incorporation download
- Selected post-registration filings and amendments

> **Use the official portal:** Start at <a href="https://camis.ocr.gov.np">camis.ocr.gov.np</a>. Avoid unofficial registration websites or agents asking for unnecessary fees or account credentials.

<h2 id="company-types">Types of Companies in Nepal</h2>

- **Private Limited** — Common for startups, SMEs, consulting, trading, and most founder-led businesses; generally 1–101 shareholders.
- **Public Limited** — For larger businesses or companies planning a public offering; higher founder and capital requirements.
- **Single-shareholder company** — A simplified private-company structure for one founder where the prescribed rules and format apply.
- **Profit-not-distributing company** — For nonprofit or social purposes; profits cannot be distributed to members.
- **Holding company** — A company whose principal purpose is holding shares or interests in subsidiaries.
- **Foreign branch or liaison office** — For foreign companies operating or representing themselves in Nepal; additional approval and documentation apply.

For most first-time founders, a Private Limited company is the default structure. Sole proprietorships and partnership firms follow different local or sector registration routes and are not the same as OCR incorporation.

<h2 id="private-company">What Is a Private Limited Company?</h2>

- <strong>Separate legal entity:</strong> The company can own property, sign contracts, sue, and be sued in its own name.
- <strong>Limited liability:</strong> Shareholders' liability is generally limited to their share contribution.
- <strong>Shareholders:</strong> A private company commonly has at least 1 and no more than 101 shareholders under the Companies Act framework.
- <strong>Directors:</strong> At least one director is required; a shareholder may also be a director.
- <strong>Registered office:</strong> The company must have an official office address in Nepal.
- <strong>Restricted transfer:</strong> Shares cannot simply be offered to the general public and transfers must follow the company's constitutional rules.

After incorporation, private companies must track annual returns, AGM obligations, accounting records, tax filings, audits where applicable, and changes to shareholders, directors, or the registered office.

<h2 id="eligibility">Who Can Register a Company?</h2>

- Nepali citizens who meet the age and identity requirements can form a company individually or with co-founders.
- A private company can have individual or corporate shareholders subject to the applicable rules.
- Existing sole proprietorships or partnerships may convert or restructure, but that is not always the same as a fresh CAMIS incorporation.
- Foreign nationals and foreign companies can participate only through the foreign-investment and sector-approval framework.

Before filing, decide the shareholders, share percentages, directors, registered office, business objectives, and authorized, issued, and paid-up capital. Changing these later may require amendments and additional filings.

<h2 id="requirements">Requirements Before Starting CAMIS</h2>

- Two or three proposed company names in Nepali and English
- Specific business objectives that accurately describe the intended activity
- Registered office address with ward, municipality, district, and contact details
- Shareholder list, ownership percentages, and share allocation
- Director list and contact details
- Authorized, issued, and paid-up capital decisions
- Clear Citizenship and NID information for Nepali shareholders and directors
- MOA and AOA prepared for the chosen company structure
- Witness details and Citizenship copies where required
- Sectoral or foreign-investment approvals where applicable

<h2 id="name">How to Reserve a Company Name</h2>

1. Create or log in to a CAMIS company-user account.
2. Search the company database for similar names.
3. Submit alternative Nepali and English names with the intended business objectives.
4. Pay the current name-reservation fee, commonly listed as Rs 100.
5. Wait for OCR review, often around one to three working days for a clean request.
6. Use the approved name and reference number to start the incorporation application before the reservation expires.

Names may be rejected when they conflict with existing companies or trademarks, use restricted words without sector approval, misrepresent the business activity, or appear unsuitable. Submit alternatives to reduce delay.

> **Validity can change:** Published guidance differs on the exact reservation window. Follow the expiry date shown inside CAMIS rather than relying on an older number.

<h2 id="documents">Documents Required</h2>

- **MOA** — Defines company name, office, objectives, capital, liability, subscribers, and share commitments.
- **AOA** — Sets internal rules for directors, meetings, voting, dividends, records, and share transfers.
- **Citizenship certificates** — Front and back copies for Nepali shareholders and directors.
- **NID details** — National ID numbers where the current CAMIS flow requires them.
- **Director photographs** — Passport-size photographs in the current portal format.
- **Witness Citizenship** — For witnesses who sign the MOA where required.
- **Registered-office information** — Ward, municipality, district, address, and premises details.
- **Additional approvals** — DOI approval, passport, corporate documents, power of attorney, or sector approval when applicable.

- Upload clear, complete, readable files in CAMIS-supported formats and sizes.
- Make sure names, capital, objectives, and share allocations match across every document and form field.
- Ensure shareholders and witnesses sign the documents where required.

<h2 id="moa-aoa">MOA and AOA Explained</h2>

The <strong>Memorandum of Association (MOA)</strong> defines the company's external scope and legal foundation. The <strong>Articles of Association (AOA)</strong> govern how the company operates internally.

- **MOA name clause** — Approved company name in the required Nepali and English form.
- **MOA office clause** — District and registered-office address.
- **MOA object clause** — Specific business activities the company will conduct.
- **MOA capital clause** — Authorized capital, share count, face value, and subscriber commitments.
- **AOA governance** — Director powers, appointments, meetings, voting, dividends, records, and share transfers.

Avoid copying an outdated template without updating names, objectives, capital, and shareholding. Vague objectives or discrepancies between CAMIS and the MOA/AOA commonly trigger OCR queries. A single-shareholder company may use a prescribed AOA format where the current rules allow it.

<h2 id="workflow">How to Register Through CAMIS</h2>

1. <strong>Create or log in:</strong> Register a company-user account with an active email and phone number.
2. <strong>Reserve the name:</strong> Submit alternatives, objectives, and the name-reservation fee.
3. <strong>Enter company details:</strong> Add company type, registered office, objectives, contact information, and capital structure.
4. <strong>Add shareholders:</strong> Enter names, Citizenship, NID, addresses, shares, percentages, and upload identity documents.
5. <strong>Add directors:</strong> Select or add directors, enter their details, and upload photographs.
6. <strong>Upload MOA and AOA:</strong> Submit signed constitutional documents that match the application fields.
7. <strong>Upload supporting documents:</strong> Add witness and case-specific approvals or agreements.
8. <strong>Pay the OCR fee:</strong> Use the integrated payment route and save the receipt.
9. <strong>Review and submit:</strong> Check the full application preview before final submission.
10. <strong>Respond to OCR:</strong> Monitor status and correct only the issues noted in a returned application.
11. <strong>Download the certificate:</strong> After approval, download the electronic Certificate of Incorporation and verify its QR code.

- **Submitted** — Application received by OCR.
- **Under review** — OCR examiner is checking documents and details.
- **Returned for correction** — OCR has listed issues; correct and resubmit.
- **Approved** — Incorporation is complete and certificate download becomes available.
- **Rejected** — Application was refused; follow the stated reason and current resubmission route.

<h2 id="fees">OCR Company Registration Fees</h2>

For private and public companies, the OCR registration fee is calculated from <strong>authorized capital</strong>, not paid-up capital. The following planning table reflects commonly published 2026 slabs; confirm the final amount in CAMIS before payment.

- **Up to Rs 1,00,000** — Rs 1,000
- **Rs 1,00,001–5,00,000** — Rs 4,500
- **Rs 5,00,001–25,00,000** — Rs 9,500
- **Rs 25,00,001–1,00,00,000** — Rs 16,000
- **Rs 1,00,00,001–5,00,00,000** — Rs 19,000–28,000 across the published slabs
- **Rs 5,00,00,001–10,00,00,000** — Rs 31,000–43,000 across the published slabs
- **Above the top slab** — Additional per-unit amount under the current OCR schedule; confirm in CAMIS.

- Name reservation is commonly Rs 100 and non-refundable.
- Business PAN registration generally has no government registration fee, but IRD procedures still apply.
- VAT registration may be free where applicable.
- Local registration, industry registration, sector licenses, professional drafting, notary, stamps, and accounting are separate costs.

> **Estimate before filing:** Use SolutionsNepal's <a href="/business-registration-cost-nepal">Business Registration Cost Estimator</a> for a planning estimate, then follow the fee shown by the official CAMIS payment screen.

<h2 id="capital">Authorized, Issued, and Paid-Up Capital</h2>

- **Authorized capital** — Maximum share capital the company is allowed to issue; OCR registration fee is calculated on this amount.
- **Issued capital** — The portion of authorized capital actually allotted to shareholders.
- **Paid-up capital** — The amount shareholders have paid or committed as paid for their shares.

A commonly used planning minimum for a private company is Rs 1,00,000 authorized capital. Public companies have higher founder and capital requirements. Banking, insurance, finance, and other regulated sectors may impose much higher sector-specific minimums.

<h2 id="timeline">Processing Time</h2>

- **Name reservation** — Often 1–3 working days
- **Document preparation** — Depends on founders and MOA/AOA readiness
- **OCR review** — Often 3–7 working days for a clean application
- **Correction query** — Adds time depending on how quickly the applicant responds
- **Overall clean application** — Roughly 7–14 days is a practical planning range
- **Foreign or regulated company** — Longer because DOI or sector approvals may come first

Statutory administrative timelines and real-world completion times are not always identical. Do not promise same-day incorporation unless the current official portal explicitly does so for your case.

<h2 id="pan">Business PAN After Incorporation</h2>

OCR incorporation and IRD Business PAN registration are separate. CAMIS may let you initiate PAN registration alongside incorporation, but the PAN is issued by the Inland Revenue Department and may still require director biometrics or office verification.

1. Use the CAMIS PAN integration if it is available for your application, or open the IRD Taxpayer Portal.
2. Select Business PAN and provide the incorporation number, registered-office details, directors, and company documents.
3. Upload Certificate of Incorporation, MOA/AOA, director documents, office proof, and photographs as requested.
4. Complete IRD biometric verification where required.
5. Receive and save the company PAN certificate before starting formal operations.

A company needs its tax identity for corporate banking, invoicing, hiring, tax returns, and other formal activity. Confirm the current deadline and procedure with IRD rather than relying on an old fixed number.

<h2 id="separate-registrations">Company Registration vs PAN, VAT, and Licenses</h2>

- **OCR / CAMIS** — Incorporates the legal company under the Companies Act.
- **IRD PAN** — Registers the company as a taxpayer and enables tax records and invoicing.
- **VAT** — Applies when turnover, activity, or law requires registration.
- **Local municipality** — Local business operation registration, trade permission, and local charges.
- **Department of Industry** — Industry registration and foreign-investment approval where applicable.
- **Sector regulator** — Special permissions for banking, insurance, education, health, media, telecom, tourism, and other regulated activities.
- **SSF** — Relevant employer and employee registration when the company hires covered staff.

<h2 id="after">What to Do After Registration</h2>

1. Download and securely store the Certificate of Incorporation, MOA, AOA, and payment receipts.
2. Obtain Business PAN from IRD.
3. Open a corporate bank account using incorporation and PAN documents.
4. Complete municipality or local-business registration where required.
5. Check VAT, industry, and sector-license requirements.
6. Set up accounting records, invoices, and a document-retention system.
7. Register employees for SSF and other employment obligations where applicable.
8. Track AGM, annual returns, audit, and corporate tax deadlines.
9. Update OCR when directors, shareholders, objectives, capital, or registered office changes.

<h2 id="foreign">Foreign Investors and Foreign-Owned Companies</h2>

Foreign shareholders should not assume that the Nepali-founder CAMIS workflow applies unchanged. Foreign investment is governed by FITTA and related DOI, sector, and Nepal Rastra Bank requirements.

1. Check whether the proposed sector is open, restricted, or closed to foreign investment.
2. Obtain Department of Industry approval before OCR incorporation where required.
3. Meet the current minimum investment or sector threshold, noting that exemptions and rules can change.
4. Prepare passport, foreign corporate, board-resolution, financial-credibility, and attestation documents.
5. Arrange compliant foreign-exchange capital injection and NRB documentation.
6. Reserve the name and file the OCR application with DOI and foreign-shareholder evidence.
7. Complete PAN and sector-specific registrations after incorporation.

> **Professional review is prudent:** Foreign ownership, joint ventures, regulated activities, repatriation, and complex corporate structures need current legal and tax advice.

<h2 id="problems">Common CAMIS Problems and Solutions</h2>

- **Company name rejected** — Use alternatives, remove restricted or misleading terms, and align the name with business objectives.
- **Application returned** — Read OCR remarks, correct the listed issues, and resubmit without changing unrelated information.
- **Shareholder mismatch** — Match names, Citizenship, NID, addresses, share counts, and percentages across all files.
- **Document upload failure** — Use clear files in the current format and size limits; upload the correct document to each field.
- **Payment not reflected** — Save the transaction ID, check the gateway, and contact official CAMIS or payment support before paying again.
- **Status not updating** — Check email and phone for queries, allow the stated review window, then contact OCR through official channels.
- **Login problem** — Use CAMIS password recovery and confirm the official URL.
- **MOA/AOA query** — Make objectives, names, capital, shares, signatures, and witness details match the application exactly.
- **PAN confusion** — Remember that CAMIS may initiate PAN, but IRD issues it and can require separate verification.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Registration Mistakes</h2>

- Choosing a name without searching CAMIS first
- Using an old MOA/AOA template without updating it
- Writing vague business objectives that do not match the activity
- Entering shareholder names or NID numbers incorrectly
- Confusing authorized capital with paid-up capital
- Uploading unreadable or cropped documents
- Assuming submission means approval
- Paying an unofficial agent unnecessarily
- Assuming OCR incorporation automatically provides PAN, VAT, or licenses
- Forgetting AGM, annual-return, audit, and tax compliance after incorporation

<h2 id="checklist">Complete Registration Checklist</h2>

- <strong>Preparation:</strong> name alternatives, founders, directors, office, objectives, capital, share structure, and documents ready.
- <strong>CAMIS:</strong> account created, name reserved, company details entered, shareholders and directors added, MOA/AOA uploaded, supporting files uploaded, fee paid, application submitted.
- <strong>Review:</strong> application number saved, status monitored, OCR queries answered, certificate downloaded and QR checked.
- <strong>After incorporation:</strong> Business PAN, corporate bank account, municipality registration, VAT check, industry and sector licenses, accounting, SSF, AGM, annual returns, audit, and tax filings.
