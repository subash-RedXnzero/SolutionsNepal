<h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>

Getting a driving license in Nepal is a two-exam rite of passage: a written (likhit) test and a practical trial. Since 1 Shrawan 2083 (17 July 2026), applications, exam scheduling and results run through the province-based <strong>eDLVRS</strong> system — each province operates its own portal — rather than one central DOTM website (see our <a href="/guides/nepal-online-driving-license-edlvrs-portal-guide">EDLVRS portal guide</a> for the provincial links). Plenty of myths about quotas and "guaranteed" trial slots persist. Here is how it actually works.

<h2 id="process">The process from application to card</h2>

1. <strong>Online application</strong> — fill the form on your province's official eDLVRS portal for your category (motorcycle, scooter, car, etc.). You choose an exam date from available slots.
2. <strong>Learn and pass the written exam</strong> — traffic signs, rules and basics. Pass and you move to the trial phase; the written result is valid for the window your office gives you, so confirm it on the portal or at your TMO and do not let it lapse.
3. <strong>Book the trial</strong> — the practical exam at your office's ground. This is the step with the longest queues in busy offices.
4. <strong>Pass the trial</strong> — for two-wheelers: the figure-eight track, the stop line, the bridge maneuver. For four-wheelers: reversing, parking and track control.
5. <strong>Medical fitness declaration</strong> — the form certifying vision and fitness, part of the file.
6. <strong>Pay and collect</strong> — after passing, the printed license is processed against the fee schedule; you collect it from the office (or through the delivery channel currently offered).

> **Learner phase:** If you have never ridden or driven, the system expects you to learn before the trial — many people practice at the actual exam ground during off hours. There is no shortcut around the trial itself.

<h2 id="trial">The trial: what they actually test</h2>

For a two-wheeler license, the trial is commonly described as four classic stations — but exact layouts, station names, and scoring differ by TMO, so confirm your office's current course rather than assuming these match it:

- <strong>The figure-eight</strong> — ride the pattern without putting a foot down. Nerves fail more people than skill.
- <strong>The line stop</strong> — come to a controlled stop with the front wheel on the line, not over it.
- <strong>The narrow bridge</strong> — ride the raised plank without dropping a wheel.
- <strong>Slow control</strong> — demonstrating clutch-throttle balance at walking pace.

Four-wheelers face reversing into a box, hill starts and track discipline. The universal truth: examiners fail balance and hesitation, not engine size. Ten sessions of deliberate practice beat months of casual riding.

<h2 id="fees">Fees and the payment flow</h2>

The license journey has several fee touchpoints:

- Application
- Written exam
- Trial
- The printed license itself

Each is modest individually. Since FY 2083/84, amounts are set by province under eDLVRS and differ by vehicle category and province (indicative ranges as of October 2026; confirm on your provincial portal). The reliable approach:

1. Check the current fee schedule on your province's eDLVRS portal when you apply (national policy notices at <a href="https://dotm.gov.np">dotm.gov.np</a>)
2. Pay through the channel the portal assigns (typically bank vouchers or digital payment)
3. Keep every voucher — name mismatches between voucher and application cause counter delays

<h2 id="rejections">Why applications get rejected</h2>

- <strong>Age or eligibility mismatch</strong> — the category you applied for does not match your age or document trail
- <strong>Name inconsistencies</strong> — citizenship, application and medical form must agree exactly
- <strong>Missed exam windows</strong> — skipping the scheduled written or trial date forfeits the attempt; rebooking means new queues
- <strong>Unpaid or mismatched vouchers</strong> — the classic silent killer
- <strong>Photo/biometric issues</strong> — poor captures at registration cause identity-check failures later
- <strong>Trying to skip stages</strong> — the system enforces the written → trial → license sequence strictly

<h2 id="takeaway">The takeaway</h2>

The Nepali license process rewards exactly two things: a consistent document trail and genuine trial practice. Apply on your province's eDLVRS portal, take the written seriously, rehearse your own TMO's stations until they are boring, and keep every payment voucher matched to your name. Everyone who does this gets a license; the stories about endless rejection almost always trace back to one of the six reasons above.

Station layouts and scoring details vary by TMO — confirm locally.
