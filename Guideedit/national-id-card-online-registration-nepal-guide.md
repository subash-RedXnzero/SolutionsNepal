<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

To get Nepal’s National Identity Card (Rastriya Parichayapatra / NID), you normally complete online pre-registration, then visit an enrollment center for document verification and biometrics. After processing, you receive your National Identity Number (NIN), then later collect your physical NID card. <strong>Online pre-registration alone does not usually issue the card.</strong>

<h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>

If you’re trying to get Nepal’s National Identity Card (NID), you may be confused by terms like “online registration”, “biometrics”, and “NIN”. The process has multiple stages.

At a high level, Nepal’s National Identity system works like this:

<strong>Online Pre-Registration → Biometric Enrollment → NIN Assignment → Application Processing → NID Card Issuance/Collection</strong>

> **Important:** Online pre-registration usually does not mean the whole process is completed online. Biometrics typically require a physical visit to an enrollment center.

This guide is written for complete beginners. You’ll learn what to do before biometrics, what happens during biometrics, and what to do after you’ve enrolled — especially for NIN checking and National ID status tracking.

<h2 id="what-is-nid">What is National ID?</h2>

A National Identity Card (in Nepali, often called <strong>Rastriya Parichayapatra</strong>) is Nepal’s government-issued digital-and-biometric identity system. It is designed to identify citizens using both:

- <strong>Demographic information</strong> (like your name, date of birth, address, family details where required)
- <strong>Biometric information</strong> (like fingerprints and other biometric traits collected during enrollment)

<h3 id="nid-included">What is included?</h3>

A National Identity record is built using:

- Your citizenship details (from your citizenship certificate records)
- Your personal/demographic information
- Biometrics captured at the enrollment center

<h3 id="nid-replaces">What does it replace?</h3>

It does not automatically replace every other document in every situation. For example:

- <strong>Citizenship certificate</strong> establishes citizenship
- <strong>NID</strong> provides government-issued identity credential details based on the national identity system

<h2 id="what-is-nin">What is NIN?</h2>

<strong>NIN</strong> stands for <strong>National Identity Number</strong>. It is the unique number linked to your National Identity record.

You may need NIN for government services before you receive your physical card, depending on the service’s requirements and how records are integrated.

> **Accuracy note:** The exact NIN format (for example, number of digits) can vary or be described differently across sources. Before relying on any “format details”, verify the official NIN format instructions from the Department of National ID and Civil Registration (DoNIDCR) portal or official guidance.

<h2 id="eligibility">Eligibility</h2>

Who can apply for a National ID card?

In general, Nepal’s National ID system is for Nepali citizens who are eligible under the applicable rules. Eligibility and age-related rules may vary by program cycles and issuance phases. Before you start:

- Use your official citizenship details
- Follow the eligibility and “who can enroll” guidance shown in the official portal during pre-registration

If the portal shows category-specific options (for students, special cases, or other groups), choose the correct option instead of guessing.

> **Who should prioritize enrollment:** social-security benefit recipients, anyone opening bank accounts or updating KYC, passport applicants, people correcting address/name details, and anyone near an active mobile enrollment camp. If none of these apply yet, there is no hard deadline — but enrolling during a quiet period beats queuing during a service rush.

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

Before you begin online pre-registration and biometric enrollment, prepare your information and documents carefully. Requirements can change depending on your situation (new application vs correction, special categories, etc.).

<h3 id="docs-basic">Basic requirements (most applicants)</h3>

- Citizenship certificate details (including citizenship number)
- Personal details (name, date of birth, gender)
- Address details (permanent and current, as requested)
- A mobile number you can access for OTP/verification (as the portal requires)

<h3 id="docs-corrections">Documents you may need if you are correcting details</h3>

Supporting documents for the correction you want to make (for example, documents that support a corrected address, spelling, or other information).

<h3 id="docs-biometrics">Documents for biometric appointment</h3>

Usually you must bring:

- Original citizenship certificate
- Your pre-registration reference details (or whatever confirmation the portal provides)
- Any supporting documents needed for corrections (if applicable)

For exact document lists, follow the enrollment center instructions and the checklist shown in your official appointment/confirmation.

<h2 id="online-vs-physical">Online vs Physical Process</h2>

One of the most confusing parts for first-time applicants is: “Is everything online?”

Typically:

- <strong>Online pre-registration</strong> collects your application information
- <strong>Biometrics</strong> requires physical attendance at an enrollment center

Here is the practical distinction:

- **Pre-registration** — Online (application details entered digitally); no physical visit for this step
- **Application information entry** — Done online
- **Biometric capture** — Physical — at the enrollment center
- **Fingerprints** — Physical — captured at center
- **Photograph (if required)** — Physical — often captured/verified at center
- **Document verification** — Some steps online, but verification at center
- **NIN assignment / processing** — After center submission — a processing step that occurs after biometrics
- **Card issuance and collection** — Status tracking may be online; card collection typically requires physical collection or as per official instructions

Only mark something “fully online” if the official portal explicitly says so for your case. (For a parallel example of a “status vs collection” workflow, see our <a href="/guides/nepal-online-driving-license-edlvrs-portal-guide">Nepal online driving license EDLVRS portal guide</a>.)

<h2 id="portal">Official Pre-Registration Portal</h2>

You should use the official portal operated by Nepal’s Department of National ID and Civil Registration. Multiple sources reporting the official process indicate the pre-registration portal is on:

- <a href="https://donidcr.gov.np/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">donidcr.gov.np</a> (department site)
- <a href="https://enrollment.donidcr.gov.np/PreEnrollment/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">enrollment.donidcr.gov.np/PreEnrollment</a> (pre-enrollment portal)

> **Accuracy note:** Always confirm you are on the correct official domain. There are many fake “NID registration” websites online that attempt to collect personal information or charge unofficial fees. If a website looks unofficial, or asks for money outside official channels, do not use it — exit and restart from the official domain.

<h2 id="pre-registration">Complete Online Pre-Registration Tutorial (Step-by-Step)</h2>

Before you start, keep these “inputs” ready:

- Your citizenship number and the issuing district as per your citizenship certificate
- Your exact name spelling (and Nepali/English spelling if asked)
- Your correct date of birth
- Your address details (permanent and current)
- A phone number you can receive OTP on

<h3 id="step-portal">Step 1: Open the official pre-registration portal</h3>

Open the official portal: <a href="https://enrollment.donidcr.gov.np/PreEnrollment/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">enrollment.donidcr.gov.np/PreEnrollment</a>. Before clicking anything, verify:

- The domain is correct
- The page language and layout match the official portal
- There is no warning sign that suggests the site is untrusted

<strong>What to do if you can’t open the portal:</strong> try again later, avoid re-entering your personal details on unofficial mirrors, and use a stable internet connection.

<h3 id="step-option">Step 2: Select the correct registration option</h3>

In the portal, you usually need to choose the option for individual applicants (not an agency). Choose the “individual” flow unless you are registering as something else and the portal provides that option.

<h3 id="step-login">Step 3: Login using your mobile number</h3>

1. Enter your mobile number.
2. Submit and wait for the OTP (One-Time Password).
3. Enter the OTP in the portal.

<strong>Common mistake:</strong> typing the wrong mobile number. Use your active number you can receive OTPs on.

<h3 id="step-enrollment">Step 4: Start a new enrollment</h3>

After login, look for something like “New Enrollment” or “Start pre-registration” and begin a new application for your NID enrollment.

<h3 id="step-personal">Step 5: Enter personal information</h3>

Fill in each section carefully. Typical fields include:

- Full name
- Date of birth
- Gender
- Citizenship number
- Issuing district

For each field:

- Understand what the field asks
- Copy data exactly from your citizenship certificate
- Avoid shortcuts and guesswork
- Re-check spelling and date

> **Mistake to avoid:** Your name must match your citizenship record. Small differences can cause verification problems later.

<h3 id="step-citizenship">Step 6: Enter citizenship information</h3>

This usually includes:

- Citizenship number
- Citizenship issuing district
- Citizenship details matching your certificate

Double-check the citizenship number digits and issuing district spelling.

<h3 id="step-address">Step 7: Enter address information</h3>

You may be asked for:

- Permanent address (district/municipality/ward)
- Current address (district/municipality/ward) if different

<strong>Why it matters:</strong> your NID record must match official identity data. Wrong addresses can lead to mismatch during verification.

<h3 id="step-family">Step 8: Add family/personal details (if requested)</h3>

Many systems collect information about parents or family details. If the portal includes family fields, enter them exactly as required and use supporting documents if your records need correction.

<strong>If you are unsure:</strong> do not invent details. Wait, check your citizenship documents, or follow the portal instructions.

<h3 id="step-review">Step 9: Review everything (before submission)</h3>

Create a quick review checklist:

- Name spelling matches citizenship
- Date of birth matches citizenship
- Citizenship number matches citizenship certificate
- Issuing district is correct
- Permanent address is correct
- Current address (if asked) is correct
- Mobile number is correct

<h3 id="step-submit">Step 10: Submit pre-registration</h3>

Submit your online pre-registration. After submission, you should receive:

- A reference/application number (important)
- Confirmation details (how you receive it depends on the portal)

<h3 id="step-save">Step 11: Save your confirmation</h3>

Save:

- Reference/application number
- Screenshot or printed copy (as allowed)
- Any instructions for biometrics and enrollment center

This reference is often needed later to book biometrics or to track NIN/status.

<h2 id="appointment">Biometric Appointment Booking</h2>

In most cases, biometrics require a physical visit. The portal may offer appointment scheduling and enrollment-center selection. Because appointment availability can change, follow what the official portal shows for your account.

<h3 id="appointment-portal">What the portal may ask you to do</h3>

- Select an enrollment location (center)
- Choose a date/time slot (if available)
- Confirm the appointment
- Save your appointment confirmation

<h3 id="appointment-unavailable">If appointments are unavailable</h3>

- Try again later
- Select another nearby enrollment center (if the portal allows)
- Avoid using middlemen websites

<h3 id="appointment-change">Can you change/cancel?</h3>

Some systems allow changes through the portal. Others require center-specific rescheduling rules. Use the options shown inside your official portal account.

<h2 id="centers">Where Can You Complete NID Biometrics?</h2>

Biometric enrollment is typically done at designated enrollment centers. These may include:

- District administrative offices
- Appointed/assigned enrollment centers
- Other government enrollment sites/camps (depending on current policy)

Availability changes by location and by time. Your pre-registration portal and appointment confirmation are the most reliable sources for where you must go.

<h2 id="during-biometrics">What Happens During Biometric Enrollment?</h2>

This section answers the “what exactly will I do there?” question. A typical sequence is:

1. Arrival
2. Token/appointment verification (if required)
3. Document check
4. Personal details verification
5. Photograph (if collected)
6. Fingerprints and other biometrics
7. Final confirmation/acknowledgement

<h3 id="biometrics-bring">What you should bring</h3>

- Original citizenship certificate
- Your pre-registration reference and appointment confirmation
- Any supporting documents for corrections
- Any other document the center requests (if they show a checklist for your appointment)

<h3 id="biometrics-collected">What information is collected?</h3>

During biometrics, you may be asked to confirm the information you entered online and then capture biometrics such as fingerprints, photograph, and other biometric data as required by the system at that center.

<h3 id="biometrics-incorrect">What if information is incorrect?</h3>

If your personal information differs, the center may request you to correct based on official rules and your record may be updated after verification. Follow official instructions from the enrollment staff and do not share documents with unofficial agents.

<h3 id="biometrics-time">How long does it take?</h3>

Processing time varies by center workload. Expect it may take longer on busy days. Do not assume same-day completion of NIN assignment.

<h2 id="appointment-docs">Documents for Appointment (Detailed Checklist)</h2>

<h3 id="docs-universal">Universally required (most likely)</h3>

- Original citizenship certificate
- Pre-registration reference/application number
- Appointment confirmation (if appointment booking is used)

<h3 id="docs-situation">Situation-dependent (bring if relevant)</h3>

- Supporting documents for corrections (if you are correcting name/address/etc.)
- Other identity documents requested by the center (only if the portal or center checklist asks)

<strong>If you forget originals:</strong> the center may refuse biometric capture until you provide them (follow the center rules).

<h2 id="after-biometrics">What Happens After Biometric Enrollment?</h2>

After you complete biometrics, your data goes through processing. Typical next stages:

1. Data processing at the department
2. Record verification
3. NIN assignment
4. Card production/printing (if required)
5. Card distribution/collection according to official instructions

> **Important:** In many identity systems, NIN becomes available before your physical card arrives. So you can sometimes use NIN for government services even if the physical card is not collected yet (depending on integration and official guidance).

<h2 id="check-nin">How to Find or Check Your NIN</h2>

After processing, you need a method to check whether NIN has been assigned to your record. Because official “NIN check” options (web portal lookup vs SMS) can change, use the official “NIN check” method inside the official department systems.

<h3 id="nin-online">Steps for online lookup (general)</h3>

1. Go to the official NID systems/portal from the department website
2. Choose the option for NIN check / NID lookup
3. Enter the information requested (commonly your pre-registration reference and/or citizenship details)
4. Submit and verify results
5. If not found, follow the official “not found” instruction (often linked to pending processing)

The current Citizen Portal lookup lives at <a href="https://citizenportal.donidcr.gov.np/ne/check-national-id-number" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">citizenportal.donidcr.gov.np/ne/check-national-id-number</a> (the "NID Search" link published on donidcr.gov.np; last verified October 2026), where you enter a request number or NIN and complete a CAPTCHA. If that address moves, start from donidcr.gov.np and follow its current NID Search link rather than trusting a forwarded URL.

<strong>SMS checking note:</strong> some systems may offer SMS checking, but the SMS format/keyword and SMS number must be verified from the official portal. Do not guess SMS keywords.

<h2 id="check-status">How to Check National ID Application Status</h2>

Status checking usually uses your application/reference number and citizenship details. Status labels can vary — use only the status meanings shown in the official portal for your application.

- **“No record found”** — Re-check your reference number spelling and digits; ensure you are using the correct application; wait for official synchronization time and re-check later as instructed. An SMS format ("N [space] your 16-digit application number" to 30001) is commonly reported online but could not be confirmed on the official DoNIDCR portal (last checked October 2026) — use the portal lookup, not an unverified SMS short code.
- **“Processing/delayed”** — Follow the official next step — some systems require no action; some require resubmission/correction only through official steps.

<h2 id="card-collection">Physical Card Collection</h2>

After your record is processed, you will need to collect the physical National ID card (or arrange collection as per official rules).

<h3 id="collection-bring">Bring</h3>

- Original citizenship certificate (or whatever identity documents the center asks for)
- Your NIN/application reference confirmation
- Any collection instruction letter or message

<h3 id="collection-representative">Someone else collecting on your behalf</h3>

Some processes allow authorization/representative collection; the official instructions must be followed. Do not assume it’s always allowed.

<h3 id="collection-not-ready">If the card is not available yet</h3>

1. Check status first
2. Then contact the relevant enrollment authority/department helpline as instructed on the official site
3. While waiting on printing, check whether you can use the eNID download through the Citizen Portal (after verifying details + OTP) or the digital National ID in the Nagarik App once a NIN has been generated

<h2 id="nid-vs-citizenship">Citizenship vs NID (Clear Difference)</h2>

- **National ID (NID)** — National identity system record. Uses NIN (National Identity Number). A government identity credential.
- **Citizenship Certificate** — Proof of Nepali citizenship. Uses citizenship certificate number/details. A government citizenship document.

National ID does not automatically eliminate the need for citizenship documents in every situation. You should keep your citizenship certificate safe even after receiving your NID, until official rules for each service clearly show it’s no longer required.

<h2 id="passport">Passport Relationship</h2>

- You may need NIN for passport-related processes or updates, depending on the current Department of Passports requirement.
- If you do not yet have the physical NID card, use official passport guidance to see whether NIN (number) is acceptable or whether the physical card is required.
- Typically, a department may ask for NIN or identity verification proof in its own system.

Do not assume that “passport requires the card” without checking current requirements. For the passport process itself, see our <a href="/guides/nepali-passport-renewal-process-documents-fees">Nepali passport renewal guide</a>.

<h2 id="corrections">Corrections</h2>

If your details are wrong (name spelling, DOB, address, photograph, or demographic details), you need an official correction process. A practical “correction workflow”:

1. Identify exactly what is wrong (compare online entries vs citizenship certificate vs received record)
2. Collect supporting documents that prove correct details
3. Request correction through the official correction mechanism mentioned in the department portal or instructions
4. Follow biometric re-verification rules if the system requests it

Do not submit multiple applications repeatedly unless the official process explicitly tells you to.

<h2 id="delays">Delays / Problems</h2>

<h3 id="delay-patterns">Common problem patterns</h3>

- **NIN not found after biometrics** — Possible cause: record mismatch or pending assignment. Next step: use official “NIN check” and follow not-found instructions.
- **Card not received after some time** — Next step: check application/card status first, then contact the enrollment authority.

<h2 id="troubleshooting">Troubleshooting (Big Table)</h2>

- **Pre-registration not submitting** — System error or incorrect entry → retry on stable internet; check each field; do not use unofficial websites
- **Citizenship details rejected** — Mismatch with citizenship certificate → correct entries using the exact citizenship certificate details
- **Appointment not available** — Slot unavailable / capacity → try again later or select another center if portal allows
- **Biometrics failed** — Document mismatch or biometric capture issue → bring originals next time; follow official staff instructions
- **NIN not found** — Not processed yet / mismatch → use official NIN check; follow not-found guidance
- **Application status not updating** — Sync delay → wait and re-check per portal instructions
- **Card not received** — Production/distribution delay → check status; contact official enrollment authority
- **Details incorrect** — Data entry or record error → request correction through official correction route with supporting docs
- **Portal login issues** — Mobile/OTP issues → use correct active mobile number; avoid repeatedly requesting OTP if limits exist

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

1. Using an unofficial “NID registration” website.
2. Entering citizenship number or spelling incorrectly.
3. Entering an address that doesn’t match documents.
4. Using a mobile number that you cannot access (OTP problems).
5. Not saving the pre-registration reference number.
6. Forgetting original citizenship certificate during biometric appointment.
7. Going to the wrong enrollment center.
8. Assuming pre-registration automatically completes biometrics.
9. Assuming NIN is generated immediately after pre-registration (it usually depends on biometrics and processing).
10. Re-applying multiple times without an official instruction.
11. Sharing personal documents with unknown agents.
12. Not checking status after biometrics.
13. Losing NIN/reference details and not knowing how to recover.
14. Thinking NID replaces citizenship certificate automatically for all services.

<h2 id="safety">Is National ID Safe?</h2>

Security and privacy are important because National ID records can include biometric data. Practical safety rules:

- Use only official portals and official enrollment centers
- Never share OTPs or verification codes
- Avoid unofficial agents and websites promising “fast updates”
- Store your pre-registration reference and documents safely
- Keep your phone secure (screen lock)
- Be careful when sending screenshots that show personal details

No identity system is “100% secure” in every scenario. But you can greatly reduce risk by using only official channels and not sharing credentials.
