<h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>

Nepal's traffic fine system was overhauled a few years ago from flat token fines to a schedule of substantial, violation-specific penalties, plus a point system that can suspend your license. If you ride or drive in Kathmandu, knowing the current landscape is cheaper than learning it from a challan.

> **Figures move:** Fine amounts are set by regulation and have been revised before. The structure below is stable; the exact rupee figures should be confirmed against the current official schedule (Metropolitan Traffic Police Division publishes it) before you rely on them.

<h2 id="schedule">The fine schedule — and why ranges exist</h2>

Under the current framework, penalties scale by vehicle category — a car or van pays more than a motorcycle for the same violation — and some violations carry fixed amounts while others carry ranges. The categories that generate the most challans in the Valley:

- <strong>Riding without a helmet</strong> — one of the most-issued fines for two-wheelers
- <strong>Lane violations</strong> — crossing the lane line, using the wrong lane for your vehicle class
- <strong>Running red lights</strong> — increasingly enforced through camera footage
- <strong>Using a phone while driving</strong> — a flat penalty under the schedule
- <strong>No license / expired license</strong> — heavier than most people expect
- <strong>Drunk driving</strong> — carries both fines and court-process exposure; the tolerance is effectively zero

Beyond the rupee cost, remember the <strong>points</strong>: many violations add points to your license, and accumulating enough within the lookback window can suspend it. The fine is the cheap part.

<h2 id="points">The point system on your license</h2>

Each violation type carries a point value. Points accrue against your license, and at defined thresholds the department can suspend it for a period. The practical consequence: repeated small violations — the "just this once" lane cuts and helmet-less short rides — are what actually put licenses at risk, not one unlucky big fine.

> **Thresholds unverified:** The exact points-per-violation values and suspension thresholds change by notice and could not be verified against a live Metropolitan Traffic Police Division schedule as of October 2026 — confirm the current point table at <a href="https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np</a> / <a href="https://dotm.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">dotm.gov.np</a> before relying on any number.

<h2 id="paying">How to pay a challan online</h2>

1. <strong>Get your challan reference.</strong> Camera-issued fines are commonly reported as reaching you by SMS to the registered mobile number (SMS delivery could not be verified as universal as of October 2026 — also check the portal/office directly); on-spot fines come with a paper slip.
2. <strong>Open the official payment channel.</strong> The traffic police payment system (accessible via the designated portal/app flow linked from <a href="https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np</a> and <a href="https://dotm.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">dotm.gov.np</a>) lets you look up a fine by vehicle plate or challan number — verify the live link on those official sites, as portal URLs change.
3. <strong>Verify the violation details</strong> — vehicle, date, violation type. This is where camera errors get caught.
4. <strong>Pay</strong> through the connected digital-wallet/banking options and keep the receipt.
5. <strong>For on-spot e-challans</strong>, officers increasingly complete the process digitally on the spot; if you were handed a paper process, follow its payment instructions exactly.

> **Deadline matters:** Unpaid challans can block license renewal and vehicle-related services. Pay within the stated window rather than letting it become a renewal-day surprise.

<h2 id="disputing">If you think the fine is wrong</h2>

You can dispute a challan — especially camera-issued ones where the vehicle might be misread or sold without transfer. The route is through the traffic office stated on the notice, with your documents (blue book, license, payment receipts). This is also where the vehicle ownership transfer discipline from our namsari guide pays off: if the vehicle is registered in your name, its challans are your problem by default.

<h2 id="takeaway">The takeaway</h2>

The current fine regime is designed so that violations are no longer rounding errors — and the point system punishes repetition, not just severity. Check the current official schedule once, keep your challan payments current, and treat the license points as the real currency at stake.

> **No dated rate table published here:** A rupee-by-rupee "current rates" table could not be verified against a dated Metropolitan Traffic Police Division schedule as of October 2026, so this guide describes the structure and payment flow instead of quoting figures — read the current schedule at <a href="https://traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">traffic.nepalpolice.gov.np</a> (Metropolitan Traffic Police Division) and <a href="https://dotm.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">dotm.gov.np</a> before relying on any amount.
