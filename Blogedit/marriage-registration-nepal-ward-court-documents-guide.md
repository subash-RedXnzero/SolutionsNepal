  - q: Where do I register my marriage in Nepal?
    a: "Two Nepali citizens who already had a social/religious ceremony register at the ward office (either spouse’s permanent-address ward, within 35 days). Court marriage at the District Court solemnises the marriage itself — no prior ceremony — and is mandatory when a foreign national is involved. Both are valid under Muluki Civil Code 2074, Part 3 (Sec 67–84)."
  - q: What is the 35-day rule?
    a: "Ward registration of a socially-performed marriage must be done within 35 days of the ceremony under the vital-events registration framework (see DONIDCR registration instructions at donidcr.gov.np and your ward's posted notice). Within 35 days it is free and usually same-day; after 35 days a late fee of ~Rs 500–1,000 applies (municipality-dependent — confirm at your ward, as amounts vary) and extra verification may be needed."
  - q: What is the court marriage 15-day notice?
    a: "Some District Courts publish a public notice (commonly reported as ~15 days) before the hearing — practice varies by court and case type, so confirm at the filing court. Both-Nepali court marriages are commonly reported as completing in ~2–4 working days where the court does not require a full notice wait; the ~16–22-day window applies mainly to foreign-spouse cases with residence verification and embassy NOC checks. The notice cannot be assumed waivable — follow the posted notice at your court."
  - q: Can I register marriage completely online?
    a: No. DONIDCR lets you pre-fill/download the Anusuchi form and check/verify the certificate online at donidcr.gov.np, but identity and witness verification and certificate issuance are in-person at the ward (or at court for court marriage).
  - q: What documents are needed for ward registration?
    a: Citizenship of both spouses, 4 passport-size photos each, completed ward form, proof of ceremony (invitation/photos/letter from officiant), and 2 witnesses with citizenship. Add migration certificate (Basai Sarai) if registering outside permanent-address ward, and divorce/death certificate if previously married.
  - q: What about a foreign spouse?
    a: "Mandatory District Court route: both passports + visas, NOC from the foreign embassy, unmarried certificate, divorce/death proof if applicable, 2 witnesses, translations/notarization, and 15-day residence for the foreign party in many cases. Court fee ~Rs 500 plus ward fees where applicable."
  - q: How much does ward registration cost?

<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

<strong>Ward:</strong> for an <strong>already-performed ceremony</strong> — register at either spouse’s <strong>permanent-address ward within 35 days</strong> (free, usually same-day) with <strong>citizenship + photos + ceremony proof + 2 witnesses</strong>. <strong>Court:</strong> the <strong>court solemnises the marriage itself</strong> (no ceremony needed) — file at <strong>District Court</strong>; both-Nepali cases commonly complete in <strong>~2–4 working days</strong>, while foreign-spouse cases take <strong>~16–22 days</strong> with residence verification and embassy NOC checks (as of October 2026 — confirm the notice period posted at your court, as practice varies). Court route is <strong>mandatory for foreign-spouse cases</strong>. Online pre-fill via DONIDCR helps but <strong>witnesses + spouses must appear in person</strong>.

> **Pick the right office:** Ward office for two Nepali citizens after a ceremony; District Court for any foreign-national marriage or when you want the court to create the marriage without a social ceremony. Wrong office = rejection.

<h2 id="two-routes">Two Legal Routes — Ward vs Court</h2>

- **What it is** — Ward: registration of an already-performed social/traditional marriage | Court: standalone legal marriage solemnised by a judge
- **Requires ceremony?** — Ward: yes (Hindu/Buddhist/Christian/Nikah etc.) | Court: no
- **15-day notice** — Ward: not required | Court: some courts post a public notice (commonly ~15 days, mainly enforced in foreign-spouse cases) — follow the notice posted at your court
- **Where** — Ward: municipality/rural municipality ward office (Local Registrar) | Court: District Court of either party’s residence
- **Deadline** — Ward: within 35 days of ceremony (late fee after) | Court: none (court creates the marriage)
- **Validity** — Both fully valid in Nepal under Muluki Civil Code 2074; court certificate is more standardised for visas/abroad

<h2 id="legal-basis">Legal Basis</h2>

- <strong>Muluki Civil Code 2074, Part 3 (Sec 67–84)</strong> — conditions for a valid marriage (20+ years, free consent, sound mind, not within prohibited degrees, single status) and Sec 73 obligation to register. Verify the section text (including Sec 76–78 on registration procedure) at <a href="https://lawcommission.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">lawcommission.gov.np</a> (Nepal Law Commission) — the authoritative statute source.
- <strong>Birth, Death and Other Personal Events (Registration) Act 2033 / National ID and Civil Registration Act 2076</strong> — registration mechanics, with DoNIDCR administering and ward offices as Local Registrars (Local Government Operation Act 2074)
- <strong>District Court</strong> authority for court solemnisation; for foreign nationals embassy NOC / unmarried-certificate / translation requirements follow court circular and immigration practice — confirm the current document list at the filing District Court (no separate "Marriage Registration Act 2028" or "Passport Act 2079" marriage-registration procedure could be verified — those citations have been removed; rely on the Civil Code + court notice).

<h2 id="who-uses-which">Who Should Use Which Route?</h2>

- <strong>Use ward:</strong> you already held a social/religious ceremony as two Nepali citizens and want the quick ward certificate
- <strong>Use court:</strong> inter-caste/inter-religion wanting a self-contained legal process, privacy, or any case where <strong>one spouse is a foreign national</strong> (ward will refuse foreigners)
- <strong>Online donidcr.gov.np</strong> helps both routes for form download/pre-fill, but court-foreigner cases still route through District Court with embassy docs

<h2 id="ward-process">Ward Office Registration (After Ceremony)</h2>

<h3 id="ward-documents">Ward Documents</h3>

- **Both spouses** — Original citizenship + copy, 4 passport-size photos each, completed ward registration form
- **Proof of marriage** — Invitation card, photos/video, or letter from the officiant (lama/purohit/pastor) — proves the ceremony occurred
- **Witnesses** — 2 adult witnesses with original citizenship + copy, present in person
- **Where different ward** — Migration certificate (Basai Sarai) from the permanent-address ward
- **If previously married** — Divorce decree or death certificate of former spouse
- **Parents’ citizenship** — Copies sometimes asked by some wards — carry them

<h3 id="ward-steps">Ward Steps</h3>

1. <strong>Choose the ward:</strong> either bride’s or groom’s permanent-address ward (or new ward with Basai Sarai).
2. <strong>Get the form:</strong> at the ward or downloaded from donidcr.gov.np / municipal e-services (pre-fill online where offered).
3. <strong>Appear together with witnesses:</strong> both spouses +2 witnesses present with originals + copies.
4. <strong>Verification:</strong> ward checks age (20+), single status, prohibited degrees, free consent, and documents.
5. <strong>Pay:</strong> free within 35 days; Rs 500–1,000 late fee after (municipality-dependent).
6. <strong>Certificate issued:</strong> Bihe Darta Praman Patra, usually same day or within a few days — check all spellings before leaving.

<h2 id="court-process">Court Marriage (District Court)</h2>

<h3 id="court-documents">Court Documents</h3>

- **Both Nepali** — Citizenship, unmarried certificate from ward, 4 photos each, 2 witnesses with citizenship, application + court fee
- **Foreign spouse** — Passport + valid visa, NOC from embassy (mandatory), unmarried certificate from home country (apostilled/legalized + translated), divorce/death proof if applicable, 2 witnesses, translations + notarization; MoFA attestation is an outbound step only, not a prerequisite

<h3 id="court-steps">Court Steps &amp; 15-Day Notice</h3>

1. <strong>File joint application</strong> at the District Court where either party resides (names, addresses, citizenship/passport numbers, DOB, marital declaration, consent, witnesses).
2. <strong>Public notice (where the court requires it):</strong> some courts post a notice (commonly ~15 days); anyone may raise a valid objection within the posted window. Both-Nepali cases at courts that do not enforce a full wait are commonly reported as completing in ~2–4 working days — confirm at filing.
3. <strong>No objection → hearing:</strong> both parties +2 witnesses appear before the judge; judge verifies identity, age, consent, and absence of prior marriage/prohibited degrees.
4. <strong>Judge solemnises:</strong> marriage created and entered in the court registry.
5. <strong>Certificate issued:</strong> court marriage certificate with case number, court seal and judge’s signature (same day or 1–2 days after hearing). Optional extra registration at DAO for administrative convenience.

<h2 id="online-donidcr">Online DONIDCR Form (Pre-registration Only)</h2>

At <strong>donidcr.gov.np → Online Ghatana Darta → Marriage Registration</strong> you can:

- download/pre-fill the Anusuchi form
- track status
- verify a certificate

This saves counter time but <strong>does not issue the certificate online</strong> — the Local Registrar (ward) or the District Court still does in-person verification and issuance. A ward certificate can be verified at donidcr.gov.np → Verification (with registration number); a court certificate is verified via the Supreme Court/District Court portal by date + number.

> **Abroad?** Two Nepalis abroad can pre-fill online, but an in-person visit to Nepal is still expected for the registration itself, or a Power of Attorney authenticated at the Nepali embassy where the local office accepts it. For a foreign-national marriage the District Court route (with 15-day residence for the foreign party in many courts) is mandatory and cannot be done fully online.

<h2 id="fees-timeline">Fees &amp; Timeline</h2>

- **Ward — within 35 days** — Free — certificate same day to a few days (if documents complete)
- **Ward — after 35 days** — ~Rs 500–1,000 late fee + 1–3 days verification
- **Court — both Nepali** — ~Rs 500 court fee — commonly ~2–4 working days where no extended notice wait applies (confirm at filing; courts that post a ~15-day notice take longer)
- **Court — with foreigner** — ~Rs 500 court + ward/translation costs — ~16–22 days including residence verification and NOC checks (as of October 2026)
- **Lawyer (optional)** — Rs 5,000–30,000 — useful for POA, foreign-spouse paperwork and single-visit execution

<h2 id="certificate-difference">Certificate Differences</h2>

- **Ward certificate** — Issued by municipality/ward; format varies by local government; in Nepali; signed by ward chair/secretary; basic details + witnesses
- **Court certificate** — Issued by District Court; standardised nationwide; court seal + judge signature; detailed case number, nationalities, passport/citizenship numbers, witness details
- **For visas/abroad** — Court certificate is more widely accepted by embassies and for MoFA attestation chains

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Filing at the wrong office (ward for a foreign-spouse marriage, or court where a simple ward registration would do)
- Missing the 35-day ward window and then paying an avoidable late fee
- Bringing only one witness or a witness without citizenship
- Assuming the DONIDCR online pre-fill = registration (it is not — you still attend in person)
- Neglecting invitation/photos as ceremony proof for the ward
- For a foreign spouse, arriving without an embassy NOC or without apostilled + translated unmarried certificate

<h2 id="checklist">Checklist</h2>

- Decide route: ward (ceremony already done, both Nepali) vs court (solemnised by judge / any foreign spouse)
- Collect: citizenship/passport + 4 photos each + 2 witnesses with IDs + ceremony proof (ward) or NOC/translations (court)
- Pre-fill the Anusuchi form on donidcr.gov.np if offered
- Both spouses + witnesses appear in person (or authenticated POA where accepted)
- Ward: file within 35 days (free); Court: file and wait the 15-day notice
- Collect certificate and verify all spellings before leaving
