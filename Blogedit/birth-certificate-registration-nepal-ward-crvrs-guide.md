<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

In Nepal a birth must be registered at the <strong>ward office within 35 days</strong> under the <strong>Birth, Death and Other Personal Events (Registration) Act 2033</strong>. The informant is usually <strong>father or mother</strong> (or an 18+ family member). Within 35 days it is <strong>free and usually same-day</strong>. After 35 days add a <strong>late fee (NPR 100–300)</strong> and extra verification. Bring the <strong>hospital birth letter + both parents’ citizenship copies + marriage certificate where applicable</strong>.

> **Act quickly:** The 35-day window is statutory (Section 6, Act 2033). Hospitals issue the birth notification/discharge letter at discharge — keep it safe; it is the primary evidence at the ward.

<h2 id="what-is">What Is a Birth Certificate in Nepal?</h2>

<strong>Janma Darta Praman Patra (जन्म दर्ता प्रमाणपत्र)</strong> is the first legal identity document issued under Nepal’s vital-events (CRVS) system. It proves name, date/place of birth, sex and parentage, and is the base document for citizenship, passport, NID (for minors), school admission and vital-statistics planning.

The five registrable vital events in Nepal are:

- birth
- death
- marriage (ward registration)
- divorce
- migration

Birth is the most common and is handled entirely at the ward level.

<h2 id="law">Legal Basis</h2>

- <strong>Birth, Death and Other Personal Events (Registration) Act 2033 (1976)</strong> + Rules — statutory requirement to register every birth within 35 days
- <strong>Local Government Operation Act 2074 (2017)</strong> — delegates registration to municipalities/rural municipalities; ward chairperson/secretary is the local registrar
- <strong>National ID and Civil Registration Act 2076 + Rules 2077</strong> — DONIDCR maintains the central civil-registration database and links vital events to the NID system (citizenportal.donidcr.gov.np)

<h2 id="where">Where to Register</h2>

- **Standard** — Ward office of the municipality/rural municipality where the birth occurred, or the parents’ permanent-address ward (confirm which your municipality uses)
- **Government employee informant** — With office recommendation, may register at the ward of the office location (migration excluded)
- **753 local levels** — Every municipality/rural municipality has a designated vital-events registrar at the ward

<h2 id="who-informant">Who Can Be the Informant?</h2>

- <strong>Father or mother</strong> — preferred
- If parents are absent: an <strong>18+ family member</strong>
- The informant must present his/her <strong>citizenship</strong>, be clearly identifiable and responsible for the accuracy of the form

<h2 id="deadline">35-Day Deadline &amp; Late Fee</h2>

- **Within 35 days** — Free — same-day certificate in most wards
- **After 35 days** — A late fee commonly applies (often reported as a flat ~Rs 200; graduated 100/200/300-by-years tables circulate but could not be verified against a DONIDCR circular as of October 2026 — confirm at your ward) + written reason for delay + deeper verification (ward-member recommendation, supporting evidence such as school/immunization record, and where asked, affidavit / muchulka or police report for very late cases)

All fees are paid at the ward. After 35 days the ward does deeper verification — bring more evidence than the minimum.

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

- **Hospital letter** — Original birth notification / discharge letter from the hospital/birthing centre stating child’s name (if given), DOB/time, sex, mother’s name
- **For home birth** — Health-post letter / FCHV attestation + khop (immunization) card + ward attestation
- **Father’s citizenship** — Original + photocopy
- **Mother’s citizenship** — Original + photocopy
- **Marriage certificate** — Where applicable — important for establishing parentage; bring original + copy
- **Application form** — Birth registration form — available at the ward or municipal website (fill name in Nepali & English, DOB in BS & AD, place of birth, sex, parents’ details)
- **Informant’s citizenship** — If the informant is not the father/mother
- **If father missing** — Police report stating father is untraceable / missing — confirm with ward first

<h2 id="process">How to Register (Step-by-Step)</h2>

1. <strong>Collect documents immediately after birth:</strong> get the hospital birth letter before discharge; keep the immunization card.
2. <strong>Go to the ward within 35 days:</strong> bring originals + photocopies. Ask for the birth registration form if you did not download it.
3. <strong>Fill the form:</strong> child name (Nepali & English), date/time of birth, sex, place of birth, parents’ names, citizenship numbers, permanent & temporary addresses, informant details. Write names exactly as on citizenship.
4. <strong>Verification:</strong> ward secretary/registrar checks documents, enters details in the vital register, and may ask a ward member to witness.
5. <strong>Certificate issued:</strong> printed on the government form with municipal seal and registrar signature — check spelling, DOB, sex and parents’ names before leaving.
6. <strong>Keep originals safe:</strong> make a scanned backup; you will need certified copies for passport/NID later.

<h2 id="online">Online Filing (Where Available)</h2>

As of April 2026, major urban municipalities (Kathmandu Valley, Pokhara, Chitwan, etc.) allow filing via the municipal e-services portal or DONIDCR-integrated platform. The flow is:

1. Open your municipality website or DONIDCR citizen portal and go to vital-events registration
2. Fill the online birth form and upload scanned documents
3. Submit and note the SMS reference/token
4. Visit the same ward with originals to verify and collect the sealed certificate

> **Not everywhere:** Many rural municipalities are still in-person only. If your municipality site has no vital-events portal, go directly to the ward — do not use a third-party “online birth certificate” site.

<h2 id="late">Late Registration (After 35 Days)</h2>

Beyond 35 days you are in the late-registration track:

- Add a written application explaining the delay
- Add ward-member recommendation (1–5 years) and supporting evidence such as school admission record, immunization book, or ward muchulka for 5+ years
- A sworn affidavit may be asked for very late cases
- Pay the late fee at the ward (confirm the current amount on the ward's posted notice — see table above)

<h2 id="duplicate">Duplicate / Certified Copy</h2>

1. Visit the same ward that issued the original
2. Submit a written request + your citizenship/ID
3. The ward pulls the original register entry and prints a certified copy (usually same day; older archived records may take longer)
4. Pay nominal fee (municipality-dependent estimate, commonly ~Rs 50–100 per copy — confirm at your ward)

Keep 2–3 certified copies — passport, NID and many embassy processes ask for an original or certified copy, not a plain photocopy. Some municipalities now issue QR-verifiable digital certified copies.

<h2 id="uses">Where You Need It</h2>

- **Citizenship** — Base proof of DOB and parentage when applying at the DAO via the ward
- **Passport & NID (minor)** — Certified birth certificate required for minor passport/NID
- **School / scholarship** — Admission and age verification
- **Vital statistics** — Foundation for the CRVS system linked to NID (citizenportal.donidcr.gov.np)

<h2 id="abroad">For Use Abroad (Attestation)</h2>

For use outside Nepal:

1. Get a fresh certified copy from the ward
2. Verify at the District Administration Office (DAO) of the issuing district
3. Authenticate at the Department of Consular Services, MoFA (doc.nepalconsular.gov.np) — and then embassy legalization where the destination requires it

See also our <a href="/guides/mofa-document-attestation-nepal-online-guide">MOFA attestation (NEATS) guide</a> for the full chain.

<h2 id="problems">Common Problems &amp; Fixes</h2>

- **Hospital letter missing / home birth** — Use khop card + health-worker/FCHV letter + ward attestation; ward may do a field muchulka
- **Name spelling mismatch vs citizenship** — Correct the citizenship/NID source first — the ward copies what is on the parents’ citizenship
- **Father unknown / absent** — Mother registers with her citizenship; add police report if father is declared missing/untraceable
- **Record not found / old register destroyed** — If any other copy exists elsewhere, that can rebuild the record; otherwise fill a new information form with a witness attestation and the ward will re-establish the record and issue a certificate
- **Birth before 2033 Act (pre-1976)** — Cannot newly register — get a ward-chairperson verification/attestation instead

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Waiting past 35 days — you pay a late fee and need more evidence
- Registering in the wrong ward (use birth-place ward or parents’ permanent-address ward as your municipality directs)
- Writing the child’s name differently in Nepali vs English on the form
- Laminating the certificate before making scanned backups
- Using a hospital card photocopy instead of the original birth notification letter
- Assuming online submission alone is enough — you still need the ward visit for the sealed certificate

<h2 id="checklist">Complete Checklist</h2>

- Hospital birth letter / health-post attestation + khop card
- Father’s & mother’s citizenship originals + photocopies
- Marriage certificate (if applicable)
- Informant’s citizenship (if informant ≠ parent)
- Birth registration form filled in Nepali & English, BS & AD dates
- Visit ward within 35 days — or prepare late-fee + extra evidence if late
- Check printed certificate (name, DOB, sex, parents) before leaving
- Make 2–3 certified copies + scanned backup for passport/NID
