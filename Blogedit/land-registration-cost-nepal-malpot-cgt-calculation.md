<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

Buying land in Nepal produces <strong>two separate tax bills at the same counter</strong>: the <strong>buyer pays a province-set registration duty</strong> (in Kathmandu Valley metro wards of Bagmati this is <strong>commonly cited as 5.3% of the higher of deed vs minimum value, as of October 2026</strong> — always confirm the current Finance Act schedule at your Malpot), and the <strong>seller pays capital-gains tax only on the profit</strong> (<strong>10% if held 5 years or less, 7.5% if held longer</strong>, on deals above Rs 10 lakh — the post-16-July-2026 federal pair). Together they land around <strong>7–8.5% of the headline price</strong> on a typical Valley deal. Women / senior buyers are reported to get a <strong>fee reduction (commonly cited as 25%, higher in some cases)</strong> — confirm the exact concession clause in the current Bagmati Finance Act text before relying on it.

> **What changed in July 2026:** Multiple practitioner summaries report that both the provincial duty schedule and the federal CGT pair moved with the Finance Acts effective Shrawan 1, 2083 (16 July 2026). Older explainers quoting a 7.5%/5% CGT pair describe the prior regime — treat 10%/7.5% as the current working figure <strong>as of October 2026</strong>, and have the counter confirm it for your deed date.

<h2 id="two-bills">The Two Separate Bills: Buyer vs Seller</h2>

- **Buyer-side duty (malpot dastur)** — A percentage of the taxable value (deed figure or official minimum, whichever is larger). Set by the province, collected at the Land Revenue / Bhumi Prashasan office.
- **Seller-side gains tax (labh kar / CGT)** — A percentage of the <em>profit only</em>, collected at the same sitting by withholding and treated as final for that gain — not via a later annual return.
- **Counter extras** — Ward recommendation letter plus independent deed-drafting cost (commonly ~Rs 1,000 at the ward and ~Rs 5,000–25,000 for drafting, varying by office and lawyer).

The two levies never offset each other: the buyer cannot deduct the seller's CGT, and the seller does not share the buyer's duty.

<h2 id="registration-fee">Registration Fee (Malpot Dastur) 2026 — Province-wise</h2>

Each province writes its own duty table into its yearly Finance Act. The Valley numbers below are the <strong>widely reported Bagmati Finance Act 2083 figures, effective 1 Shrawan 2083 (16 July 2026), as of October 2026</strong> — ask the counter for the printed Schedule and read the rate off that paper, not off any blog (including this one). If the parcel sits outside Bagmati, that province's Act controls.

<h3 id="bagmati-rates">Bagmati Rates in Detail</h3>

- **Metro wards inside Kathmandu Valley (KMC, Lalitpur)** — commonly cited 5.3% of taxable value
- **Municipal wards inside the Valley (Bhaktapur, Kirtipur, Tokha, Budhanilkantha, Madhyapur Thimi etc.)** — commonly cited 4.8%
- **Metro wards outside the Valley (Hetauda, Bharatpur)** — commonly cited ~5.15%
- **Sub-metropolitan wards** — commonly cited ~4.65%
- **Municipal wards outside the Valley** — commonly cited ~4.5%
- **Rural (gaupalika) wards** — commonly cited 3.0%
- **Flats / apartments in group housing** — commonly reported as charged at the land rate (no verified 1% apartment concession found — confirm at Malpot)

The small gap between the Valley metro figure and the outside-Valley metro figure is the "Valley premium" — practitioner write-ups treat it as built into the rate itself. A separate "Sabhyata Kosh" surcharge line could not be verified in the published schedule excerpts available as of October 2026 — do not budget a separate 5% surcharge unless your Malpot's printed schedule shows one.

<h3 id="other-provinces">Other Provinces</h3>

Lumbini, Gandaki, Koshi, Madhesh, Karnali and Sudurpaschim each publish their own table with the same metro-high / rural-low shape but different percentages. Always read the local Malpot / Bhumi Prashasan Karyalaya notice — people still say "Malpot" in conversation.

> **How to verify:** Ask for the current "Province Finance Act, registration-duty schedule" at the counter and read your ward class off that sheet.

<h2 id="concession">Women &amp; Senior Discount</h2>

Practitioner summaries describe a Bagmati reduction where the title passes to a woman, a senior citizen 70+, or a parentless minor — commonly quoted as 25% off the duty, with higher figures (e.g., ~35% for single women or rural-municipality transfers) in some write-ups, and joint husband-wife deeds still qualifying once. These summaries usually cite Finance Act section numbers (reported as Sec 9 / Sec 13), but the section text could not be independently verified against the gazetted Act as of October 2026 — <strong>treat the 25%/35% figures as unconfirmed planning figures and read the concession clause off the printed Act schedule at your Malpot before discounting your budget</strong>. Only one concession is reported to apply per deed.

On a Rs 1 crore Valley metro deed, a 25% reduction (if confirmed) would save <strong>Rs 1,32,500</strong> (5,30,000 → 3,97,500).

<h2 id="cgt">Capital Gains Tax (CGT) 2026 — Seller</h2>

- **Held 5 years or less** — 10% on the profit (working figure as of October 2026 — confirm the federal Finance Act line at Malpot/IRD)
- **Held over 5 years** — 7.5% on the profit
- **Applies only if** — Deal value above Rs 10 lakh (commonly cited floor — confirm)
- **Collection** — Withheld at the counter when the deed passes; described as final for that gain

Through FY 2082/83 practitioner archives quote 7.5% / 5% — many older posts still carry that pair. Treat 10% / 7.5% as the current working pair <strong>as of October 2026</strong>.

> **10-year exemption:** A primary residence held 10+ years and sold for under Rs 10 lakh is fully exempt. Transfers to spouse/children or by inheritance are also exempt. CGT for entities (companies/firms) is materially higher (10–25%) — not covered in this individual seller guide.

<h3 id="gain-calc">How the Gain Is Calculated</h3>

```text
Gain = Selling Price − (Purchase Price + Allowable Expenses)
```

Allowable expenses = documented renovation, brokerage, legal fees related to the transaction. Keep invoices/receipts — without proof the ward cannot deduct them.

- **Example** — Sell Rs 1.00 crore − Purchase Rs 70 lakh − Rs 50,000 brokerage = Rs 29,50,000 gain
- **CGT ≤5 years (10%)** — Rs 2,95,000
- **CGT >5 years (7.5%)** — Rs 2,21,250

<h2 id="example-1cr">Worked Example: Rs 1 Crore Deal (Kathmandu Metro)</h2>

Assumptions: sale inside KMC/Lalitpur, held 3 years, male buyer, gain Rs 29.5 lakh after documented expenses.

- **Registration fee (5.3% of 1 crore)** — Rs 5,30,000
- **Ward recommendation letter** — Rs 1,000
- **Lawyer / deed drafting** — Rs 10,000
- **Buyer total** — Rs 5,41,000
- **Seller CGT (10% of 29.5 lakh)** — Rs 2,95,000
- **Combined transaction cost** — Rs 8,36,000 = 8.4% of headline price

<h3 id="example-variants">What Changes the Total</h3>

- **Held >5 years (7.5% CGT)** — CGT → Rs 2,21,250 → combined ~7.6% (Rs 7,62,250)
- **Woman buyer (25% off)** — Registration → Rs 3,97,500 → combined ~7.0% (Rs 7,03,500)
- **Woman + held >5 years** — Combined ~6.3% (~Rs 6,29,750)
- **Rural municipality (3% fee)** — Registration → Rs 3,00,000 → combined ~6.1% despite same CGT

<h2 id="minimum-valuation">Government Minimum Valuation</h2>

Every district's Minimum Price Determination Committee (CDO-chaired) publishes a per-aana floor per ward and road type. As a <strong>prior-year illustration only (FY 2082/83 figures, not current)</strong>, Kathmandu write-ups quoted roughly Rs 4 lakh/aana in outer wards up to ~Rs 50 lakh/aana on Durbar Marg/New Road. Malpot taxes the <strong>higher of your declared deed figure vs the current minimum</strong>. Current-year minima sit well below market in most wards (commonly described as ~30–40% of market — confirm your ward's current sheet), so declaring under the floor saves nothing.

<h2 id="process">Registration Process at Malpot</h2>

1. <strong>Agree deed value:</strong> reconcile your negotiated price with the ward minimum valuation.
2. <strong>Get ward letter:</strong> recommendation from the relevant ward office (municipality).
3. <strong>Draft deed (likhat):</strong> via a licensed deed writer/lawyer; include seller/buyer citizenship/NID, kitta number, area, and transaction value.
4. <strong>Calculate fees:</strong> ask the counter for the current provincial schedule + CDO minimum sheet and have the counter run the duty + CGT math for your ward class.
5. <strong>Pay:</strong> via malpot.gov.np portal (connectIPS/eSewa) or bank deposit voucher — keep the payment reference; buyer’s PAN required.
6. <strong>Register:</strong> both parties + witnesses at the Land Revenue (Bhumi Prashasan) Office with originals; Malpot withholds CGT, registers the deed, and issues the new Lalpurja (land ownership certificate).

<h2 id="documents">Documents Required</h2>

- **Both parties** — Original citizenship + NID where available, PAN (buyer), 2 witnesses with citizenship
- **Seller** — Original Lalpurja, recent land-tax (malpot) receipt, CGT calculation sheet, prior deed copy
- **Buyer** — Ward recommendation letter, citizenship/NID, PAN, fee payment voucher
- **For concession** — Proof for 25%/35% — single-woman certificate / 70+ citizenship
- **Company seller** — Company registration, board decision, tax clearance certificate, authorized signatory citizenship

<h2 id="mistakes">Costly Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Copying a rate from the wrong province’s blog — Bagmati 5.3% ≠ Gandaki/Lumbini schedule
- Using the old 5%/7.5% CGT pair from FY 2082/83 — current is 10%/7.5% since 16 July 2026
- Declaring below the government minimum valuation — Malpot will recalculate on the higher minimum anyway
- Forgetting the women’s/senior 25%/35% concession on the registration fee
- No receipts for renovation/brokerage — CGT gain cannot be reduced without documented expenses
- Assuming “flats = 1%” or adding a fake 5% Sabhyata Kosh — neither exists in the Act
- Forgetting the Rs 10 lakh floor — small transactions at/below Rs 10 lakh attract no CGT

<h2 id="checklist">Pre-Registration Checklist</h2>

- Check current Bagmati Finance Act 2083 rate for your metro/municipality/gaupalika
- Pull the CDO minimum per-aana valuation for your ward/road type
- Take max(deed value, govt minimum) as your tax base
- Calculate registration fee (base × province rate) minus women/senior concession
- Calculate CGT gain (sell − buy − documented expenses); apply 10% or 7.5% by holding period
- Add ward letter (~Rs 1k) + drafting (~Rs 10k)
- Arrange PAN (buyer) + Lalpurja/tax receipt (seller)
- Pay via malpot.gov.np portal and keep the payment reference
