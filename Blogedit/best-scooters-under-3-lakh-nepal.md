The all-new Jupiter is the headline of this segment right now. On Jagdamba Motors' official product page, the 2025-generation Jupiter 110 sits at <strong>Rs 264,900</strong> — under the line by a comfortable Rs 35,000 — and it is the least expensive verified scooter here that still gives you a real front disc.

- **Engine** — <strong>113.3cc</strong> single-cylinder CVTi FI
- **Power and torque** — <strong>5.9 kW at 6,500 rpm</strong>, <strong>9.8 Nm at 5,000 rpm</strong>
- **Brakes** — <strong>220 mm front disc</strong>, 130 mm rear drum
- **Wheels and lights** — 12-inch tubeless (90/90 both ends), LED headlamp and tail lamp
- **Suspension** — telescopic front fork, 3-step adjustable rear

The disc-plus-LED-plus-large-boot combination at this price is why the new Jupiter anchors the value story. TVS markets its under-seat storage as the largest in the class — wording you should verify at the dealership with the luggage you actually carry — and the official page highlights the big under-seat space, the emergency brake warning and a modern ECU-managed ignition.

One festive-market note: during Dashain 2083, Jagdamba's own banner advertised an introductory <strong>Rs 2,57,900</strong> price for the all-new Jupiter with 0% financing and a lifetime-warranty claim, while the product page still showed Rs 264,900. <h2 id="dio-110">Honda Dio 110 — Rs 284,900</h2>

Honda's 110cc scooter in Nepal is the Dio, sold by Syakar Trading Company through honda.com.np. The official page lists the Dio 110 BS6 at <strong>Rs 284,900</strong> in both STD and DLX trims, and it remains the most polished small scooter on sale in Nepal.

- **Engine** — <strong>109.51cc</strong> single-cylinder PGM-Fi
- **Power and torque** — <strong>5.78 kW at 8,000 rpm</strong>, <strong>9.03 Nm at 5,250 rpm</strong>
- **Brakes** — 130 mm drum front and rear
- **Weight and tank** — <strong>103 kg</strong>, 5.3-litre tank
- **Suspension** — telescopic front fork, 130 mm drum geometry at both ends
- **Extras** — engine start-stop switch, ACG silent start, external fuel lid, front pocket, intelligent meter with real-time and average mileage plus distance-to-empty

The Dio's identity is refinement: fuel injection, an ACG silent starter, a start-stop switch that conserves fuel at long lights, and an intelligent multi-information meter that shows live and average economy. Its drawbacks are honest ones at this budget — drum brakes at both ends and no Bluetooth connectivity, in a segment where TVS now offers both on cheaper paper. What you are paying for is badge confidence, a light 103 kg mass, strong resale familiarity and a finished cockpit feel.

If your heart is set on a Honda but the budget has room, note the <strong>Dio 125 at Rs 331,900</strong> sits well above the line — it adds a 123.92cc engine, a front disc, idling stop and a DLX smart key. It appears in our above-budget list below.

<h2 id="ntorq-disc">TVS Ntorq 125 Disc Fi — Rs 293,900</h2>

This is the most powerful verified scooter in the table, and the only one whose official page quotes a 0-to-60 km/h time around nine seconds and a top speed of about 95 km/h — figures published by the distributor as marketing material, not our measurement. The Bluetooth console is the genuinely differentiating feature at this price, warning you of incoming calls, storing the last-parked position and logging rides.

Consider its trims honestly: the <strong>Disc Fi at Rs 293,900</strong> is the full-featured value point, while the <strong>RTFi</strong> (Rs 315,900), <strong>XP</strong> (Rs 334,900) and <strong>Ntorq 150</strong> (Rs 369,900) play above the line. - **Colours listed** — Neon Sunburst, Martian Copper, Cosmic Titanium

Be equally honest about the limits: a claimed 158 km is a test-condition figure; charging needs a reliable parking-point socket; EV resale is still maturing; and availability is city-by-city. If those swing your way, the Orbiter V2 is, on official pricing alone, the most interesting scooter under Rs 3 lakh today.

<h2 id="expectations">What a Scooter Under Rs 3 Lakh Gets You in 2026</h2>

The sub-3-lakh class has quietly arrived in 2026:

- <strong>Fuel injection everywhere.</strong> All verified scooters here use FI engines with ECU control and BS6/OBD II emission hardware — carburettor budgets are gone.
- <strong>110 to 125cc dominance.</strong> The engine band runs 109.51cc (Dio 110) to 124.8cc (Ntorq), which covers city speeds safely without climbing into the above-line 125+ trims.
- <strong>LED is common but not universal.</strong> The new Jupiter and Dio 110 carry LED lighting; the RayZR 125 Hybrid Drum still ships a 12V 35/35W halogen headlight, so check the trim you are quoted.
- <strong>Front discs remain premium.</strong> Only the new Jupiter (220 mm) and the Ntorq Disc Fi (220 mm roto-petal) officially bring front discs under the line; the Dio 110 and RayZR drum are drum-braked at both ends.
- <strong>Hybrid finally has a price point.</strong> Yamaha's RayZR Hybrid puts silent stop-start motoring under Rs 3 lakh — with the LED/cluster caveat — at 99 kg, the lightest of any verified scooter here.
- <strong>Connected consoles exist below the line.</strong> TVS's Ntorq brings Bluetooth calling and navigation to Rs 293,900; the Fascino would even add a TFT with turn-by-turn, but without a published price in Nepal.
- <strong>Storage is a real differentiator.</strong> TVS markets the new Jupiter's under-seat boot as class-leading; the RayZR carries 21 litres and the Dio 110 adds a front pocket and an external fuel lid for filler access.
- <strong>An EV entry under the line exists.</strong> The Orbiter V2 makes petrol-free commuting officially available at less than the Dio 110's price — with range, charging and availability caveats applied.

Weights of 99–118 kg, 5.2–5.8-litre tanks and 12-inch tubeless wheel specs across the field tie the class together: these are world-city scooters intended for Kathmandu's realities rather than for sustained touring speeds.

<h2 id="official-vs-market">Official Price vs Showroom Price in Nepal</h2>

The official tag (which this guide verifies) is the anchor, not the final bill. Nepal showrooms routinely quote:

- <strong>Over the official tag</strong> when registration, insurance and number-plate charges are bundled in — Hero's FAQ openly says its ex-showroom figures can vary with those add-ons.
- <strong>Under the official tag</strong> during festive stock pushes, old-colour clearances or when dealer incentives flow — a Dashain 2083 example: Jagdamba's banner advertised the all-new Jupiter at an <strong>introductory Rs 2,57,900</strong> with 0% financing while the product page held at Rs 264,900.
- <strong>Loosely with older batches</strong> — OBD I versus OBD II emission cycles, model-year stock and equipment lists can shift without a page update, so match the exact variant on the order.

What you should insist on is a written quotation that separates the showroom price, registration, insurance, plate and accessory charges. Recurring annual costs matter just as much for a buyer choosing between the Xoom VX and the Dio 110 — our vehicle-tax explainer and bluebook renewal walkthrough cover those, and embossed number-plate work is part of the registration journey after a new purchase. If any shop quotes dramatically below an official tag like Rs 264,900, ask which batch, variant or import channel produced the number.

<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to Choose the Right Scooter</h2>

Work through four questions in order:

1. <strong>What is your real budget?</strong> Rs 255,900 (Xoom VX ex-showroom) and Rs 264,900 (Jupiter) anchor the field's low end. Budget for registration, insurance and days of paperwork before setting the ceiling — otherwise the Rs 3 lakh line you were planning becomes a Rs 3.2–3.4 lakh reality.
2. <strong>Which braking and safety package do you need?</strong> If a front disc matters for your daily route, the Jupiter 110 and Ntorq Disc Fi are the only verified sub-3-lakh choices with one; everything else here is drum-braked on paper. Long-route or two-up riders also get the most from the Ntorq's 124.8cc performance.
3. <strong>How much does economy or electrification matter?</strong> The RayZR Hybrid Drum is the economy champion with clear trim honesty; the Orbiter V2 is the running-cost reset, provided you can charge where the scooter lives.
4. <strong>What does your local service network support best?</strong> TVS, Honda and Suzuki have dense dealer webs, Yamaha's support rides on MAW's network, and Hero's Xoom is sold through its own channel. Also confirm the exact trim — especially RayZR drum versus disc, and Ntorq Disc Fi versus XP — because trims carry meaningful hardware gaps.

Then follow the process that prevents regret at any price point: check the official page for the latest tag, book a test ride (Jagdamba runs one on its TVS pages; most showrooms arrange them), and only then sign for the written on-road figure. Take the vehicle-tax rates and bluebook documents into account in the same week, before the sticker price turns into ownership.
