<h2 id="quick-answer">Quick Answer: Can Foreigners Buy Property in Nepal?</h2>

The answer depends on the buyer's legal status, the property, and the purpose of the transaction. This is the starting point for understanding <strong>foreigner property rights Nepal</strong>: an ordinary foreign national should not assume they can buy Nepalese land or a house with land in personal freehold ownership. NRNs and foreign citizens of Nepali origin may have specific statutory rights. Foreign-invested businesses may use property for legitimate business purposes through structures permitted by current investment and land law, but FITTA is not a blanket personal land-purchase permission.

- **Ordinary foreign national** — Do not assume direct freehold land or house ownership is permitted; verify status and transaction-specific law.
- **NRN / eligible person of Nepali origin** — May have statutory residential-property rights subject to current Act, Rules, documentation, purpose, location, and applicable limits or approvals.
- **Nepal-registered foreign-invested business** — May use or lease business premises where permitted and approved; incorporation does not automatically create unrestricted land ownership.
- **Diplomatic mission or international organization** — May operate under government-to-government or treaty-based arrangements that do not apply to ordinary buyers.
- **Nepali citizen** — Uses the ordinary domestic land, registration, ceiling, tax, and transfer framework.

> **Legal answer first:** Before signing an agreement or paying a deposit, obtain written confirmation from the relevant authority and advice from a qualified Nepalese property lawyer.

<h2 id="land">Foreign Land Ownership in Nepal</h2>

The starting question is whether the buyer can hold registered land ownership, not whether they can occupy a building. Land ownership, use rights, leasehold rights, and a contractual right to occupy property are different legal interests.

- Ordinary foreign nationals should not treat a residential lease as freehold ownership.
- Buying a house often involves transferring rights in the underlying land as well as the structure.
- A business may need a lawful lease, approved use arrangement, or another structure rather than personal land ownership.
- Special status, government arrangements, inheritance, apartment rules, and investment approvals require transaction-specific verification.

<h2 id="house">Can Foreigners Buy a House in Nepal?</h2>

“Buying a house” is not always legally separate from acquiring the land underneath it. A foreign national may be able to rent or lease a residence, but that does not mean they can purchase the building and land in the same way as a Nepali citizen.

Do not use a friend's name, spouse's name, nominee, informal partnership, or shell company to bypass an ownership restriction. Such arrangements can leave the paying party without enforceable ownership and create serious legal and financial risk.

<h2 id="nrn">NRN Property Rights in Nepal</h2>

The Non-Resident Nepali Act, 2064 and related Rules create a framework for Non-Resident Nepalis. “NRN” is not simply a synonym for any foreigner interested in Nepal. Citizenship, Nepali origin, NRN registration or card, residence abroad, and the exact transaction must be checked under current law.

- NRN status is different from Nepali citizenship.
- A foreign citizen of Nepali origin should not assume they have every right of a Nepali citizen.
- Residential and investment purposes may be treated differently.
- Any area, value, regional, approval, or registration condition must be confirmed from the current Act, Rules, and authority.
- Property registration and sale-proceeds repatriation must use the prescribed process and banking channels.

Online summaries often repeat area ceilings without proving that the cited text is current or applicable to the buyer. Obtain the current written rule from the Department of Land Management and Archives, the relevant Malpot office, or qualified counsel before relying on any number.

<h2 id="comparison">NRN vs Foreign National vs Nepali Citizen</h2>

- **Nepali citizen** — Ordinary domestic ownership and transfer rules apply, subject to land ceilings, classification, registration, and tax law.
- **NRN / eligible foreign citizen of Nepali origin** — Specific statutory rights may apply, but status, purpose, location, conditions, registration, and current limits must be verified.
- **Ordinary foreign national** — Should not assume direct freehold land or house ownership. Lawful lease/use options may be available.

<h2 id="fitta">Does FITTA Allow Foreigners to Buy Property?</h2>

The Foreign Investment and Technology Transfer Act (FITTA), 2075 is an investment framework. It addresses foreign investment approval, registration, technology transfer, foreign-invested businesses, and repatriation within applicable rules. It does not automatically give an ordinary foreign individual the right to buy residential land in Nepal.

- A foreign-invested business must have a genuine permitted business purpose.
- Company registration and foreign-investment approval do not by themselves erase land-law restrictions.
- Business premises may be obtained through lease, use, or another approved structure where current law permits.
- The business must also comply with company, sector, DOLMA/Malpot, and NRB foreign-exchange rules.

> **Do not overread FITTA:** Foreign investment law is not the same as personal residential land ownership law. A company is not a loophole.

<h2 id="company">Can a Foreign Company Own Property in Nepal?</h2>

Distinguish an overseas company, a Nepal-registered company with foreign investment, a joint venture, and an individual foreign national. The entity's registration does not decide the question by itself; purpose, sector, investment approval, land rules, and transaction documents also matter.

- A foreign company not registered or approved to operate in Nepal should not be treated as an ordinary Nepalese landowner.
- A Nepal-registered foreign-invested company may require Department of Industry approval, company registration, and lawful business premises.
- A joint venture remains subject to investment, land, sector, and foreign-exchange rules.
- Leasehold or use rights are not the same as freehold title.

<h2 id="options">Property Options Available to Foreigners</h2>

- **Residential lease** — Use or occupancy for living; the lessor retains ownership.
- **Commercial lease** — Office, shop, or operating premises under a written lawful lease.
- **Business premises** — A foreign-invested business may use an approved lease or other permitted structure for office, hotel, factory, or similar operations.
- **NRN residential acquisition** — May be available only where the buyer qualifies and current NRN law, registration, conditions, and limits are satisfied.
- **Apartment or condominium** — Requires project-specific verification; do not rely on generic claims about floors or foreign eligibility.

Every option must be checked against current land, apartment, company, investment, tax, and foreign-exchange rules. A long lease can be valuable, but it remains a lease.

<h2 id="diplomatic">Diplomatic and International Organization Property</h2>

Embassies, consulates, and international organizations may operate under government-to-government arrangements, treaties, host-country agreements, or approvals involving the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and other authorities. Those arrangements cannot be generalized to ordinary foreign nationals buying a home or land.

<h2 id="inheritance">Can Foreigners Inherit Property in Nepal?</h2>

Inheritance is not the same as a voluntary purchase. A succession case may depend on the deceased owner, heir relationship, citizenship, NRN status, property type, and current land restrictions.

- Nepali-citizen inheritance is governed by the Civil Code and related succession rules.
- NRN inheritance may be treated under the NRN framework, subject to current conditions.
- An ordinary foreign-national heir should not assume automatic freehold land ownership.
- Death certificates, relationship or heirship documents, tax clearance, and registration may be required.

> **Case-specific area:** Inheritance by an ordinary foreign national requires written advice from a Nepalese lawyer and confirmation from the relevant authority.

<h2 id="tax">Taxes, Registration, and Foreign Exchange</h2>

For a legally permitted transaction, the buyer or entity may face registration fees, local charges, documentation costs, property taxes, and bank or valuation charges. The seller may have a separate capital-gains obligation. Rates depend on location, property, transaction value, fiscal year, buyer status, seller status, and transfer type.

See SolutionsNepal's <a href="/guides/land-registration-fees-malpot-nepal-guide">Land Registration Fees in Nepal guide</a> for the general Malpot calculation framework. It does not replace a status-specific legal and tax review.

- Bring investment funds through authorized banking and foreign-exchange channels.
- Confirm documents proving the source and route of funds.
- Check NRB rules for conversion, reporting, repatriation, and sale proceeds.
- Foreign-invested businesses should confirm Department of Industry approval and repatriation documentation.
- Do not pay a deposit until the bank and lawyer confirm the proposed payment structure.

<h2 id="due-diligence">Due Diligence Before Any Property Transaction</h2>

1. Confirm your legal status: ordinary foreign national, NRN, Nepali citizen, company, or diplomatic/international entity.
2. Confirm that the intended ownership, lease, or use transaction is legally permitted.
3. Verify Lalpurja, title, seller authority, co-owners, and land records at the relevant Malpot office.
4. Check kitta or parcel number, area, ward, land classification, and permitted use.
5. Search for mortgages, restrictions, disputes, easements, and existing claims.
6. Confirm required investment, company, land, sector, and government approvals.
7. Calculate registration, local, tax, legal, bank, valuation, and foreign-exchange costs.
8. Have the agreement, deed, lease, and payment structure reviewed by qualified Nepalese counsel.
9. Obtain written confirmation from the relevant authority before paying a material deposit.

<h2 id="myths">Common Myths About Foreign Property Ownership</h2>

- **“Any foreigner can buy a house.”** — Not a safe assumption. House transactions can include underlying land and must be checked by status and structure.
- **“Buying only the building avoids land rules.”** — Do not rely on this without a legally reviewed structure and registration position.
- **“FITTA lets every foreigner buy land.”** — FITTA regulates investment; it is not automatic personal residential land permission.
- **“NRNs have exactly citizen rights.”** — NRN rights are statutory and conditional; citizenship and NRN status are not interchangeable.
- **“A Nepali friend can hold it safely.”** — Nominee or proxy ownership can leave the foreign payer without enforceable rights and creates serious risk.
- **“A company solves everything.”** — A company needs genuine purpose, approvals, lawful property arrangements, and continuing compliance.

<h2 id="steps">What Should a Foreigner Do Before Buying?</h2>

1. Identify the buyer or entity status.
2. Determine whether the intended ownership or use is permitted.
3. Identify the controlling Acts, Rules, and government authorities.
4. Verify title, kitta, land classification, restrictions, and seller authority.
5. Confirm approvals, taxes, registration, payment, and repatriation requirements.
6. Get a qualified Nepalese property lawyer to review the transaction documents.
7. Register only through the prescribed Malpot, company, investment, and banking process.
