<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

Ansha Banda is the <strong>legal division of joint family property under Civil Code 2074 Sec 205–236</strong> — <strong>husband, wife, father, mother, son and daughter = equal shares</strong>. If everyone agrees: <strong>draft a deed (अंशबन्डा कागज) with inventory → sign with witnesses + thumb impressions → notarise → register at Malpot → mutate into separate Lalpurjas</strong>. If not: <strong>District Court Ansha Banda case</strong> (or mediation). Challenge an unfair partition within <strong>3 months</strong>.

> **No signature = no partition:** Every coparcener must sign before witnesses. An absent member abroad signs via a Power of Attorney — don’t draft a deed that omits someone.

<h2 id="what-is">What Is Ansha Banda?</h2>

<strong>Ansha Banda / Ansabanda (अंशबन्डा)</strong> is the partition of joint family property (land, house, cash, shares, vehicles, gold) among coparceners (अंशियार). Before partition the property is joint; after registration each former coparcener owns their share independently and can sell, gift or mortgage it without the others’ consent.

<h2 id="who-can-claim">Who Can Claim — Equal Shares (Civil Code 2074)</h2>

- **Coparceners (Sec 205)** — Husband, wife, father, mother, son, daughter — each one equal share
- **Daughters** — Married and unmarried daughters have the same right as sons (fully settled since 2074)
- **Not coparceners** — Do not assume extended relatives are coparceners — confirm under Sec 205 vs other heir classes

<h2 id="two-routes">Two Routes: Mutual Deed vs Court Case</h2>

- **Mutual (deed + Malpot)** — All agree → deed → notarise → register at Malpot → mutate. Fastest/cheapest: days–weeks if clean.
- **Mediation** — Negotiated with a mediator, recorded in a mediation deed, then registered at Malpot. Binding on signatories; 1–6 months.
- **Court case** — District Court Ansha Banda mudda when agreement fails; court surveys, orders a compulsory division; final order taken to Malpot for mutation. Litigation timeline.

<h2 id="deed-format">Partition Deed (अंशबन्डा कागज) — What It Must Contain</h2>

- <strong>Coparceners:</strong> full name, citizenship reference, relationship to head of family, current address of each
- <strong>Property inventory:</strong> for each parcel — kitta number, location, area, type/use, ownership chain, and valuation reference. For houses add rooms/storeys/materials; for movables add cash, bank, shares, bonds, vehicles, gold with values
- <strong>Allocation:</strong> which property or sub-parcel (with boundaries) goes to which coparcener — or proceeds share if sold and divided
- <strong>Debts/obligations:</strong> family debts settled among coparceners with amounts
- <strong>Acknowledgments:</strong> that the partition is fair/complete and no other joint property beyond the inventory is being hidden
- <strong>Witnesses:</strong> at least 2 competent witnesses signing before all coparceners
- <strong>Operative date:</strong> date from which the partition is effective; all coparceners sign with thumb impressions

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

- **Identity** — Citizenship of all coparceners (original + copy) + photos where asked
- **Property** — Lalpurja for each property + latest malpot tax receipt (clear dues)
- **Technical** — Survey/measurement report from a licensed technician for land division
- **Family proof** — Ward relationship verification (family register) — ward checks that named persons are truly the family claimed
- **Deed** — Drafted partition deed with inventory as above
- **If POA needed** — Power of Attorney for an absent coparcener (authenticated; if abroad, via Nepali Embassy)

<h2 id="steps-mutual">Mutual Partition — Step-by-Step</h2>

1. <strong>Agree the shares:</strong> family meeting settlements who gets what — include debts and any member continuing to cohabit.
2. <strong>Measure & value:</strong> licensed surveyor measures land; list movables/cash/share values with supporting docs.
3. <strong>Draft the deed:</strong> reduce the agreement to writing in the statutory format above; keep plot numbers/areas identical to Lalpurja and trace maps.
4. <strong>Sign before witnesses:</strong> all coparceners + 2 witnesses sign with thumb impressions; notarise.
5. <strong>Ward verification:</strong> submit deed + citizenship + Lalpurja + survey report to the ward; ward verifies family relationships.
6. <strong>Register at Malpot:</strong> file for registration; pay the partition registration fee; Malpot registers the partition.

<h2 id="mutation">Registration &amp; Mutation at Malpot</h2>

Registration at Malpot is what makes the partition operative against third parties. <strong>Mutation (dakhil kharej)</strong> then updates the land records: each former coparcener receives a <strong>fresh Lalpurja in their individual name</strong> for their allocated share. Only after mutation can a coparcener sell or mortgage their portion independently. Keep the stamped deed + new Lalpurjas safely — banks and future buyers will ask for them.

<h2 id="court-route">When Family Cannot Agree — Court / Mediation Route</h2>

- <strong>Mediation:</strong> a neutral mediator helps agree the split; settlement recorded in a mediation deed, then registered at Malpot like a mutual deed (binding; enforceable if later repudiated).
- <strong>District Court case:</strong> file an Ansha Banda mudda with citizenship, Lalpurja, family proof, property details and evidence of denial; court investigates, surveys, orders a compulsory division; the final order is presented at Malpot for implementation.
- <strong>Notary role:</strong> a notary advocate can draft/notarise a mutual deed and prepare certified copies, but cannot decide a contested partition — that is litigation decided by the court.

<h2 id="deadlines">Deadlines to Challenge</h2>

> **Verify in the statute:** Time limits below are commonly cited practitioner summaries of the Civil Code limitation (hadd-myad) chapter — confirm the exact article for your fact pattern in the Code text at <a href="https://lawcommission.gov.np" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">lawcommission.gov.np</a> or with counsel before acting.

- **No partition yet or shared-enjoyment deed** — Can file at any time (commonly cited — confirm)
- **Unhappy with a completed partition** — Generally 3 months from the partition date
- **Concealed/hidden property** — For the life of the coparcener who hid it
- **Other acts** — ~6 months from the act (confirm per Civil Code chapter for your fact pattern)

> **Watch the 3 months:** Missing the 3-month window to challenge an unfair partition usually loses the claim. Hidden property is the only long-lived exception.

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- A thin deed without a full inventory (kitta numbers, boundaries, values, debts) — creates the next dispute
- Omitting a daughter or other equal coparcener (Civil Code 2074 equality is settled law)
- One signature missing or an absent member not covered by a proper POA
- Not registering at Malpot — an unregistered deed does not transfer title for resale/mortgage
- Mismatched plot areas across Lalpurja vs trace map vs deed
- Relying on a verbal split — carries little legal weight

<h2 id="checklist">Checklist</h2>

- List all coparceners (husband, wife, father, mother, son, daughter) — no one omitted
- Inventory with kitta numbers, areas, boundaries and valuations for every parcel
- Deed drafted per statutory format with witnesses + thumb impressions
- Ward family-relationship verification obtained
- Malpot tax receipts cleared; survey report ready
- Notarised, registered at Malpot, and mutated into individual Lalpurjas
- Copies kept for future sale/mortgage; note the 3-month challenge window
