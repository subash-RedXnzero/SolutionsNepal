<h2 id="what-is-ftth">What Is NT Fiber (FTTH)?</h2>

FTTH — Fiber To The Home — brings an optical fiber cable all the way into your house instead of stopping at a street cabinet. Because light carries the signal, fiber comfortably delivers 50–200 Mbps on standard plans and up to 1–2 Gbps on business lines, and the same single cable carries internet, landline telephone and television together.

The three things bundled on that cable matter for choosing a package:

- <strong>Internet</strong> — the core service, with a speed tier (50/75/100/150/200 Mbps) and a daily Fair Usage Policy (FUP) cap on standard plans
- <strong>Telephone (voice)</strong> — a landline number with bundled NT-to-NT call minutes; extra off-net calls follow NT's standard voice rates
- <strong>NTTV</strong> — Nepal Telecom's IPTV television service, in Basic and Premium channel versions, delivered over the same fiber through a set-top box

Businesses get an <strong>XGPON</strong> line instead — the newest fiber standard with multi-gigabit speeds — under separate corporate tariffs. One more distinction to keep in mind: the <strong>CPE</strong> (customer premises equipment) is the router/ONU box installed in your home, and whether it is free, included in the price, or purchased separately depends on the plan you pick, as shown below.

<h2 id="fup">FUP: Daily Data Caps and Throttling Speeds</h2>

The Fair Usage Policy is the rule most buyers miss. On standard plans your speed is NOT actually unlimited: after you cross a daily data cap, the line is <strong>throttled</strong> to a lower speed until the cap resets.

| Speed | Data cap per day | Throttled to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 Mbps | 50 GB | 5 Mbps |
| 75 Mbps | 75 GB | 10 Mbps |
| 100 Mbps | 100 GB | 15 Mbps |
| 150 Mbps | 150 GB | 20 Mbps |
| 200 Mbps | 200 GB | 50 Mbps |

How to read it in practice: a 100 Mbps line still gives you 100 GB of full-speed data every day — roughly 25–30 hours of HD streaming — which a typical household never exhausts. The throttle kicks in only past the daily cap. If you run a home office, upload big files nightly, or stream constantly on many screens at full quality, the <strong>Premium plans below remove FUP entirely</strong>. On all bundle tiers the daily FUP applies; Premium internet and Wi-Fi 6 Premium are exempt.

The Wi-Fi 6 label describes the <strong>router</strong>, not your internet line — your line is still the 100–200 Mbps you subscribe to. And older devices that lack Wi-Fi 6 support connect to it like any router, so they will not see the full benefit.

<h2 id="choose">Which NTC Fiber Package Should You Choose?</h2>

- <strong>One person or a tight budget</strong> — the 50 Mbps monthly Internet + Telephone at Rs 815 keeps commitment at zero. If you stay a year, move to the Rs 8,499 yearly price.
- <strong>Lowest possible cost per month</strong> — the 2-year 50 Mbps pack (Rs 14,999, ~Rs 625/month) if you are settled at your address and ready to pay once.
- <strong>Students and online classes</strong> — 50 or 75 Mbps handles video classes fine; the daily FUP caps (50/75 GB) are generous for study use. Check that the free mobile-data bundle is confirmed when subscribing.
- <strong>Remote workers and video meetings</strong> — 100 Mbps at Rs 1,150/month, or jump to Premium (Rs 5,900/month) if nightly uploads or many simultaneous calls keep hitting the daily cap. The free public IP on Premium matters if visitors need to reach a server at home.
- <strong>Streaming families, multiple devices</strong> — 100–200 Mbps; a 200 Mbps line throttles to 50 Mbps after its 200 GB daily cap, which is still usable evening speed.
- <strong>Homes that watch TV</strong> — TV bundles. Compare first-year vs renewal pricing (Basic 100 Mbps: Rs 13,499 then Rs 12,499), and remember the extra set-top box is Rs 3,230 if a second room wants TV.
- <strong>Buyers inside the festive window</strong> — the Wi-Fi 6 festive prices (100 Mbps Rs 11,499) cost only Rs 2,000 more than the standard 100 Mbps yearly internet + telephone plan while adding the newer router tier.
- <strong>Offices</strong> — ignore individual plans; XGPON corporate tariffs govern, starting at Rs 69,691/year for 500 Mbps Basic.

<h2 id="apply">How to Apply for NT Fiber Internet</h2>

1. <strong>Confirm availability at your address.</strong> Dial 198 (NT quotes it as the number for new fiber connections and maintenance) or visit the NT office listed for your district on the <a href="https://www.ntc.net.np/services/fiber-to-the-home-ftth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official FTTH service page</a>. Offices are listed across dozens of districts including Kathmandu, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur, Kaski, Morang, Sunsari, Dang, Kailali and Jumla; your exact street depends on the local exchange.
2. <strong>Measure the cable distance informally.</strong> The first 100 meters of drop fiber from the street point are free; beyond that it is Rs 10 per meter. A house far from the road pays more.
3. <strong>Pick your package</strong> from this guide or the live tariff page — decide billing cycle (monthly vs yearly changes the effective cost significantly) and whether to attach voice and TV.
4. <strong>Fill the application and provide what NT asks for.</strong> The tariff page does not list document requirements, so ask the office when you book rather than assuming; subscriber identity verification is standard for Nepali telecom connections.
5. <strong>Schedule installation.</strong> Budget the one-times: Rs 500 installation (free on 2-year packs), Rs 665 Ownership Tax if a new voice number is issued, and the router/STB unless your plan includes them.
6. <strong>Test and keep records.</strong> After installation, confirm the router and (if ordered) the set-top box work, note the warranty period (2 years on the multi-year pack's terms), and keep the receipt showing that all charges were inclusive of tax.

<h2 id="renew">How to Renew or Change Your Package</h2>

Renewal pricing exists in the tariff for both TV-bundle tiers (for example, 150 Mbps + Premium NTTV renews at Rs 15,999 after a first year at Rs 16,499). Published channels to renew or get help:

1. <strong>Dial 198</strong> — the stated number for new fiber connections and maintenance requests alike
2. <strong>Visit the NT office</strong> that serves your area, or the one where you subscribed
3. <strong>Self Care portal</strong> — NT's website has a self-service login area for registered customers; whether your specific upgrade or downgrade is possible there depends on the package, so confirm rather than assume

What the official tariff page does NOT document: a self-service package switch mid-cycle (for example moving a monthly 100 Mbps line up to 150 Mbps). Before subscribing, ask 198 how tier changes are handled and whether a change resets your billing cycle. The 2-year multi-year pack is a single up-front purchase for 24 months, so treat that commitment accordingly.

<h2 id="compare-others">Comparing NTC Fiber With Other ISPs in Nepal</h2>

Private ISPs (WorldLink, Vianet, Subisu, WebSurf and others) sell fiber aggressively in Kathmandu Valley and big cities, and their promotional rates change monthly — we have not verified their current price cards here, so we will not quote them. Use NTC's published tariffs as your comparison baseline and weigh these factors rather than headline megabits alone:

- <strong>Total cost of ownership</strong> — installation, router and set-top box can add Rs 6,000+ upfront on monthly single-year plans; NT's multi-year and yearly bundles fold those in
- <strong>FUP exposure</strong> — standard NT tiers throttle after a daily cap; if an ISP's "unlimited" claim is genuinely unlimited, that is worth real money for heavy users
- <strong>Infrastructure ownership</strong> — NT owns its core network end-to-end; most private ISPs lease some backhaul, which affects outage ownership
- <strong>Reach</strong> — NT's listed offices stretch to far-flung districts (Jumla, Kalikot, Salyan) where private fiber presence is thinner
- <strong>Loyalty-proofing</strong> — bundled NT mobile data with yearly plans can offset a mobile recharge bill for NT SIM users

Then do the one thing that settles it locally: ask the houses next door which provider's line actually performs during evening hours in your lane.

<h2 id="verdict">Final Verdict: Are NTC Fiber Packages Good Value in 2026?</h2>

For a state-owned telecom, NT's fiber pricing is more competitive than its reputation suggests. Rs 815/month for a 50 Mbps fiber line with telephone, or roughly Rs 625/month on the 2-year pack, undercuts most people's expectations from "government internet". The yearly 100 Mbps price (Rs 11,499 — Rs 958/month by division) and the currently live festive Wi-Fi 6 offer make genuine household-grade plans available under Rs 1,000/month.

The trade-offs are the ones to enter with eyes open: a daily FUP cap on standard tiers (Premium fixes it from Rs 5,900/month), hardware purchases stacked onto monthly plans, and the mobile-data bundle figures NT's own page contradicts. Where you live decides more than the tariff sheet does — fiber stops at the exchange boundary.

Before you pay anything: recheck the <a href="https://www.ntc.net.np/tariff/ftth-tariff" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official FTTH tariff page</a>, confirm availability and cable distance with 198, and read today's offer windows — seasonal offers come and go.
