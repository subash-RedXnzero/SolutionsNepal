<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

Get <strong>Nata Pramanit</strong> at your <strong>permanent-address ward office</strong> with <strong>citizenship of both parties + proof of relationship + 2 witnesses</strong>. Usually <strong>Rs 500–1,000</strong>, often <strong>same-day</strong> if the ward’s family register verifies. You need it most for <strong>citizenship by descent, minor passport, inheritance/property transfer, and abroad family-link proofs</strong>.

<h2 id="what-is">What Is Nata Pramanit?</h2>

<strong>Nata Pramanit (नाता प्रमाणित)</strong> — also called relationship/kinship certificate — is a ward-issued letter that certifies a specific family relationship as recorded in the ward’s population/family register. It states names, citizenship numbers, ward, and the exact relationship (e.g., “Ram Bahadur is the father of Sita”). It is not a family-tree research — it confirms what the local register already shows or what witnesses + documents prove.

<h2 id="when-needed">When You Need It</h2>

- <strong>Citizenship:</strong> proving parent–child link for descent (tied to the “citizenship by case” checklist)
- <strong>Passport / minor ID:</strong> linking child to parents
- <strong>Inheritance / Ansha Banda:</strong> proving heir relationship at court / Malpot
- <strong>Land transfer – gift/partition between relatives:</strong> verifying kinship for concession or legality
- <strong>Abroad:</strong> embassy/consulate asking for family-link proof (PCC, visa, NOC chain)
- <strong>Ward services:</strong> migration, birth/marriage linkage where family entry is inconsistent

<h2 id="types">Common Types</h2>

- **Parent–child** — Most common — for citizenship/passport
- **Spouse** — Where marriage certificate alone is not enough for the asking office
- **Sibling** — Often via parent link — “both are children of X”
- **Extended** — Grandparent–grandchild etc. — ward may ask for intermediate links + witnesses

<h2 id="where">Where to Get It</h2>

At the <strong>ward office of your permanent address</strong> where your family is recorded in the population register. If the family has migrated, get a Basai Sarai (migration) certificate from the old ward first, or have the current ward pull the register entry after verification.

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

- **Both parties** — Citizenship (or birth certificate/minor ID for a child) — original + copy
- **Proof of relationship** — One of: citizenship showing same parents/family, birth certificate, marriage certificate for spouse link, old Nata Pramanit / ward family register entry
- **Witnesses** — 2 adults with citizenship, present in person at the ward
- **Form + photos** — Ward application form + passport-size photos where asked
- **If register is silent** — Additional: ward muchulka / field verification, neighbors’ statements where the ward requires it

<h2 id="process">Step-by-Step at the Ward</h2>

1. <strong>Confirm the exact relationship wording</strong> the asking office wants (e.g., “father–son” vs “son of” — offices are particular).
2. <strong>Gather proof:</strong> citizenship/birth/marriage certificate that already shows the link.
3. <strong>Visit the ward together</strong> with both parties (or their citizenship + authorized representative where the ward allows) and 2 witnesses.
4. <strong>Fill the form</strong> — names exactly as on citizenship (Nepali + English), citizenship numbers, issuing districts, ward, and the stated relationship.
5. <strong>Verification:</strong> secretary checks the family/population register and witness statements; may do a brief muchulka for extended links.
6. <strong>Pay at the revenue counter</strong> (Rs 500–1,000) and keep the receipt.
7. <strong>Collect the letter:</strong> signed/stamped by the ward chair/secretary — check spellings before leaving.

<h2 id="fee-timeline">Fee &amp; Timeline</h2>

- **Fee** — Commonly Rs 500–1,000 at the ward (municipality revenue, as of October 2026) — municipality-dependent; confirm the posted fee notice at your ward
- **Timeline** — Same day to a few days with complete documents and witnesses present; longer if the family register needs to be traced/updated
- **Validity / freshness** — No expiry is printed, but asking offices commonly want a certificate issued within the last 3–6 months — municipality/office-dependent; confirm with the asking office

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Spelling the names differently from citizenship in English/Nepali
- Bringing photocopies only — carry originals + copies
- Arriving without 2 witnesses present
- Requesting the wrong kinship wording (e.g., “brother” when the office needs “son of X, brother of Y”)
- Getting it from the wrong ward (not the permanent-address / family-register ward)
- Assuming a digital download alone is enough — the ward’s stamp/signature is what the asking office verifies

<h2 id="checklist">Checklist</h2>

- Exact relationship phrase confirmed with the asking office (DAO/passport/court/embassy)
- Citizenship/birth/marriage proof that shows the link
- 2 witnesses with citizenship available to attend
- Ward form + photos ready
- Rs 500–1,000 at the revenue counter
- Certificate checked (names, numbers, ward, relationship) before leaving

<h2 id="sources">Official Sources &amp; Final Note</h2>

Witness count, fee and freshness expectations are municipality-dependent — verify with your ward before visiting.
