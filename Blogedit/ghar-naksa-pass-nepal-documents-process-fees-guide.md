<h2 id="tldr">Quick Answer (TL;DR)</h2>

Get <strong>Ghar Naksa Pass</strong> from your <strong>plot’s municipality/ward</strong> before building — it approves your drawings against <strong>zoning, setbacks, ROW, FAR and the National Building Code</strong>. Bring <strong>Lalpurja + Char-killa + Thapad Napi + malpot clearance + citizenship</strong> and <strong>architect/structural drawings stamped by a NEC-registered professional</strong> (soil test for 3+ floors). <strong>Fee ~Rs 10–25/sq ft residential</strong>; timeline <strong>30–90 days</strong> for a clean file; issued as <strong>Asthai (plinth) → Sthai (full) → Completion Certificate</strong>.

> **Start with facts, not drawings:** Ask the ward for your plot’s zoning, FAR and road ROW before your architect draws. Designing first and checking setbacks later is the most expensive mistake.

<h2 id="what-is">What Is Ghar Naksa Pass?</h2>

Naksha = drawing, Pass = approval. The municipality’s sign-off that your proposed building complies with local bylaws (setbacks, height, coverage, FAR), road Right of Way (ROW), and seismic safety under the Building Act 2055. Construction without it is illegal — inspectors can impose a stop-work order, fine, or in serious cases demolition on the offending portion.

<h2 id="who-issues">Who Issues It?</h2>

Since federalism, your <strong>municipality / rural municipality</strong> (not a central ministry) reviews, inspects and issues Naksa Pass via its engineering/building-permit section. KMC, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur, Pokhara, Butwal etc. each publish their own bylaws and fee schedules — hence requirements differ slightly.

- <strong>KMC / Lalitpur / Butwal:</strong> Electronic Building Permit System (EBPS) online submission + tracking (e.g., lsmcebps.gov.np) — still needs physical verification
- <strong>Smaller municipalities:</strong> in-person paper filing at the ward

<h2 id="when-needed">When You Need It</h2>

- New construction of any size (even a one-room structure)
- Vertical addition (extra floor), horizontal extension, or major structural change
- Rebuilding after demolition (demolition papers required)
- Change of use (residential → commercial) where bylaws differ

<h2 id="documents">Required Documents</h2>

<h3 id="land-docs">Land &amp; Ownership</h3>

- **Lalpurja** — Land ownership certificate — original + copy (Land Revenue Office)
- **Char-killa** — Four-boundary certificate — confirms plot boundaries (Survey Office)
- **Thapad Napi** — Certified field measurement map / trace map (Survey Office)
- **Malpot clearance** — Current FY land-tax receipt — no outstanding dues
- **Citizenship** — Landowner’s nagrikta (or company PAN + registration for institutional owner)
- **Road/ROW note** — Ward road clearance where required for narrow access / shared wall

<h3 id="design-docs">Design &amp; Professional</h3>

- **Architectural drawings** — Site plan, floor plans, elevations, sections — at required scales (often 1:100 plans, 1:50 sections), stamped/signed by a NEC/CAAN-registered architect
- **Structural drawings** — Foundation, column-beam, reinforcement + calculation report — from a licensed structural engineer (mandatory for 3+ floors)
- **Soil test** — From a geotechnical firm — required for 3+ floors (takes 1–2 weeks; influences foundation design)
- **Plumbing/sanitation** — Where municipality requires it
- **POA (if needed)** — Notarised Power of Attorney if owner not applying; if abroad, attested at the Nepali Embassy

> **Avoid delays:** Update old Lalpurja/Char-killa data before applying — mismatches between Lalpurja plot area and the trace map vs drawings are the #1 correction reason.

<h2 id="process">Step-by-Step Process</h2>

1. <strong>Pre-consult at the ward:</strong> take Lalpurja to confirm zoning, FAR, setbacks, height limit and any planned road widening that could affect ROW.
2. <strong>Engage a registered professional:</strong> drawings by an unlicensed draughtsman are rejected outright.
3. <strong>Prepare drawings + documents:</strong> ensure title blocks, plot area, road width, setbacks, floor areas and structural grids match across every sheet.
4. <strong>Submit at the ward/municipality:</strong> via EBPS online where available (KMC/Bhaktapur/Lalitpur/Butwal) or in person; pay the initial application fee (built-up-area based) and get an acknowledgement slip with an application number.
5. <strong>Document & design review:</strong> municipal engineers check bylaws + National Building Code; you may receive a written correction notice.
6. <strong>Site inspection:</strong> engineer visits the plot to verify boundaries, access and existing structures against the survey map.
7. <strong>Approval → collection:</strong> collect the stamped approved drawing set + permit (keep originals on site — inspectors check them).

<h3 id="asthai-sthai">Asthai vs Sthai Permit</h3>

- **Asthai Ijazat (Temporary)** — Issued after initial approval — authorises construction up to plinth / DPC level only
- **Plinth inspection** — Municipality confirms the plinth matches approved drawings
- **Sthai Ijazat (Permanent)** — Clears the superstructure; you may then complete the building
- **Nirman Sampanna (Completion)** — Final inspection after construction → Completion Certificate (needed for final bank disbursement, utility connections, future sale/insurance)

<h2 id="fees">Fees</h2>

- **Residential** — Commonly quoted ~Rs 10–25 per sq ft of built-up area — municipality-dependent estimate as of October 2026; confirm the current rate paper at your municipality's revenue counter
- **Commercial** — Commonly quoted up to ~Rs 45 per sq ft — estimate; confirm locally
- **Revision after approval** — Additional fee to review an amended drawing (extra floor/area/structure)
- **Professional fees** — Architect + structural engineer design fees separate; soil test fee separate (1–2 weeks, geotechnical firm)

Because fees track built-up area and use, a 2-floor house and a 4-floor apartment on the same plot pay very differently. Confirm the current rate per sq ft at your municipality’s revenue counter before budgeting and include it as a separate line in your <a href="/nepal/construction/house-construction-cost">House Construction Cost</a> estimate.

<h2 id="timeline">Timeline</h2>

- **Official window** — Commonly cited as 35 working days under the Local Government Operation Act — confirm the current provision text; practice varies
- **1–2 floor residential (clean file)** — Commonly reported 3–6 weeks (estimate)
- **3–5 floors (structural review)** — Commonly reported 6–10 weeks (estimate)
- **Heritage zones (Durbar Square etc.)** — Commonly quoted 10–16 weeks+ (unverified — confirm with the heritage desk)
- **Per correction round** — Commonly +3–6 weeks (estimate)
- **KMC EBPS (clean, no conflicts)** — Sometimes reported as ~25–30 days for online-tracked files — confirm the current published turnaround at KMC

<h2 id="inspections">Inspections During Construction</h2>

- Keep approved drawings on site — inspectors compare them directly to the structure
- Expect inspections at plinth/DPC and at mid-construction; notify the office as required
- Any setback/FAR/height deviation needs a formal revised Naksa Pass before building — building past an unpassed inspection is built at risk

<h2 id="mistakes">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>

- Starting excavation/foundation before the permit (stop-work/fine risk)
- Measuring setback from the visible road edge instead of the official ROW (ROW is often wider)
- Plot area differing across Lalpurja vs trace map vs drawings
- Missing joint-owner consent or old demolition papers for a rebuild
- Architectural vs structural drawings inconsistent (grid, floor area, stair)
- Submitting without a soil test where required, or with a generic report not tied to your plot
- Changing the design during construction without a revised permit

<h2 id="checklist">Checklist</h2>

- Lalpurja, Char-killa, Thapad Napi, malpot receipt — area and plot number consistent
- Citizenship + ward road/FAR/zoning confirmation
- Architect + structural engineer engaged (NEC-registered) + soil test if 3+ floors
- Drawings with consistent title blocks, scales and area tables
- EBPS online submission where available + acknowledgement slip saved
- Site inspection attended; Asthai → plinth inspection → Sthai → keep set on site
- Completion Certificate applied for after construction
